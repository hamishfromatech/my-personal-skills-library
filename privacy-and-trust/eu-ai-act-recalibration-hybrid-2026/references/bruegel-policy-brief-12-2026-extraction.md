# Bruegel Policy Brief 12/2026 — The Right Balance: How to Fix EU AI Regulation

**Author:** Mario Mariniello  
**Date:** 11 June 2026  
**DOI:** 10.64153/BVAQ6479

## Executive Summary

The EU AI Act was conceived as traditional ex-ante product-safety regulation. AI escapes the narrow product definition because it operates in unknown environments and takes actions unforeseen at coding time. An ex-ante regime cannot effectively safeguard against unforeseeable harm. The proposal: shift to a balanced mix of ex-ante and ex-post measures, trading reduced ex-ante compliance burden for solid ex-post judicial review, ad-hoc AI liability, and new monitoring/enforcement tools. Net effect on compliance costs expected to be negative.

## Five Blind Spots of the Current AI Act

1. **False dichotomy between high- and low-risk systems:** Risk changes during ordinary use; a minimal-risk chatbot can become harmful without any new product or deliberate act.
2. **Disproportionate compliance costs:** For an average AI system (€170K dev cost), compliance costs €14,623–€29,277 (9–17% of development cost). Burden falls disproportionately on SMEs.
3. **Fragmented enforcement:** 2,000+ national market surveillance authorities, most originally designed for physical product safety, not AI.
4. **Democratic deficit:** Standards written by industry experts behind closed doors; value-laden decisions (what is "fair," "safe," "unbiased") made without civil society input.
5. **No ad-hoc AI liability mechanism:** Victims must navigate existing product liability and tort law not designed for algorithmic decision-making.

## Theoretical Model Results

The brief develops a formal model comparing social costs under ex-ante vs. ex-post regimes:

- **Figure 1 (Damage threshold):** Below a certain damage threshold, ex-post is preferable. Above it, ex-ante is necessary.
- **Figure 2 (Risk revelation):** Ex-ante costs skyrocket when "safe" applications prove unexpectedly risky — because they face no requirements.
- **Figure 3 (Developer misperception):** The more developers underestimate risk, the stronger the case for ex-ante requirements.

Conclusion: Neither regime always dominates; a hybrid is optimal.

## Three Policy Recommendations

### 4.1 Multitiered Ex-Ante Requirements Based on Deployment Scale

| Tier | Turnover | Users Affected | Requirements | Assessment |
|------|----------|----------------|----------------|------------|
| Light | < €50M | < 100,000 | Basic data governance, risk assessment, conformity checklist | Self-assessment |
| Standard | €50M–€150M | Up to 1M | QMS, documented risk mitigation, data governance, conformity | Self-certified |
| Intense | > €150M | > 1M | All current AI Act requirements + mandatory third-party assessment | Third-party |

### 4.2 Ex-Post Liability and Supervision

- **Revive AI Liability Directive:** Strict liability for prohibited/high-risk systems; rebuttable presumption for others.
- **Detection infrastructure:** API traffic sampling + AI observability platforms, supervised by AI Office.
- **Aviation near-miss model:** Non-punitive reporting of AI near-misses to independent EU authority.

### 4.3 Ex-Post Universal Transparency

- **Researcher access:** Structured access to API traffic, training data, model architectures for vetted researchers.
- **Near-miss reporting:** Confidential, non-punitive, aggregated analysis.
- **Public incident registry:** Based on OECD 29-criteria taxonomy; universal reporting requirement.

## Political Timeline

- **May 2026:** Digital Omnibus / AI Omnibus agreed — relaxes some deadlines, simplifies rules
- **August 2028:** European Commission required to start evaluation of AI Act effectiveness
- **August 2031:** Commission may propose amendments
- **Bruegel recommendation:** Do not wait until 2031; start refining framework now

## Comparison with GDPR

The brief explicitly warns against replicating GDPR outcomes:
- GDPR contributed to market concentration by disproportionately burdening smaller firms
- AI Act risks similar concentration if SME compliance costs are not reduced
- Proposed tiered system mimics DSA structure to avoid this pitfall
