# Usercentrics State of Digital Trust 2026: Evidence Base

## Study Design

- **Source:** Usercentrics, "State of Digital Trust 2026 Report" (June 24, 2026)
- **Research partner:** Sapio Research
- **Sample:** 11,000 consumers across 7 markets
- **Fieldwork:** March 2026
- **Markets:** UK, USA, Germany, Spain, Italy, Netherlands, Sweden
- **Note:** Sweden joins the study for the first time in 2026; year-on-year comparisons exclude Swedish data
- **Year:** Second annual study (first was 2025)
- **Platform context:** Usercentrics processes 8.8 billion user consents per month across 2.4 million websites/apps in 195 countries

## Core Findings

### Finding 1: The AI Transparency Premium

52% of consumers globally will pay more for brands that are transparent about how they use AI with their data, at an average 7% premium.

| Market | % willing to pay more | Avg premium | Notable |
|--------|----------------------|-------------|---------|
| Germany | 73% | 9% | Highest globally |
| UK | (not specified) | (not specified) | 80% would stop if misused |
| Spain | (not specified) | (not specified) | 76% acted against brands |
| Sweden | (not specified) | (not specified) | 69% trust banking |

### Finding 2: The Revenue Consequence Cascade

47% of consumers have taken at least one action with direct revenue consequence in the past 6 months because of concerns about how their data was being used in AI.

Specific actions:
- 31% warned friends and family or complained publicly
- 35% have taken two or more actions (canceled, switched, reduced spend)
- 24% avoided trying a new product from that brand
- 20% switched to a competitor they believed handled AI data more responsibly
- 20% reduced their spending

**Scale:** For a brand with 1 million customers, up to 240,000 purchase-affecting decisions in 6 months driven entirely by AI data concerns.

Quote (Tilman Harmeling, Usercentrics): "Consumers are making purchasing decisions based on how brands handle their data, and over half are willing to pay more to the ones that get it right. The brands that move first won't just earn the premium. They'll earn a category position that's almost impossible to compete against once it's established."

### Finding 3: Trust Decline

- 52% of consumers now trust AI less than humans with their data (up from 48% in 2025 — largest single-year movement)
- 71% concerned that AI-driven personalization feels intrusive
- 48% click "accept all" on cookies less often than three years ago (up from 46% in 2025)

### Finding 4: Three Forces Driving 2026's Inflection

1. **AI moved from answering questions to taking actions:** Agentic AI now accesses financial accounts, calendars, and customer records on behalf of users, raising the stakes of data misuse in ways consumers directly feel.

2. **Privacy regulation expanding faster than any point since GDPR:** EU AI Act in active enforcement; 20+ US states with distinct privacy laws; UK regulators publishing agentic AI governance paper (March 2026).

3. **Consumers crossed from passive acceptance into active decision-making:** Steady accumulation of data breaches, AI training controversies, and cookie banner enforcement actions has shifted the baseline.

### Finding 5: Privacy-Aware vs. Privacy-Unaware Personalization Gap

- Privacy-aware consumers: 53% comfortable with personalization
- Privacy-unaware consumers: 19% comfortable with personalization
- Gap: 2.8x (privacy-aware are nearly 3x more comfortable)

**Key insight:** Privacy-aware consumers — the ones most likely to act on AI data concerns — are also the ones most comfortable with personalization when they trust the brand. Transparency doesn't suppress personalization; it enables it for the segment that matters most.

## Market-by-Market Detail

### Germany
- 73% will pay more for AI transparency (highest globally)
- 9% average premium (highest globally)
- 75% have acted against a brand over AI data concerns
- **Positioning:** Lead with AI transparency as primary differentiator; highest premium capture opportunity

### United Kingdom
- 80% would stop using a service if data was misused (highest of any market)
- Data privacy rights awareness improved more than anywhere (43% unaware, down from 50% in 2025)
- **Positioning:** Emphasize data control and stopping guarantees; highest churn risk from misuse

### Spain
- 76% have acted against a brand over AI data concerns (highest)
- 92% have taken some form of data protection action in past 6 months
- **Positioning:** Trust recovery is critical — high damage already done; emphasize reform and transparency

### Netherlands
- 77% find AI-driven personalization intrusive (highest globally)
- Only 35% will pay more for AI transparency (lowest globally)
- **Positioning:** Less personalization, more transparency; privacy minimalism; don't over-invest in premium capture

### Sweden (new in 2026)
- 69% trust banking with their data (highest of any market)
- 56% don't have a good understanding of how their data is collected (also highest)
- **Positioning:** Education + transparency; banking as trust benchmark; high trust ceiling if understanding improves

### Italy
- 42% will pay more for AI transparency (lowest willingness alongside Netherlands)
- 5% average premium (lowest of any market)
- **Positioning:** Cost-competitive transparency; don't over-invest in premium; focus on avoiding churn

### United States
- 50% will pay more for AI transparency
- Only 39% trust government services with their data (lowest of any market)
- **Positioning:** Position against institutional failure; privacy as independence; low institutional trust = high opportunity for private-sector trust

## Methodology Notes

- Survey-based (not telemetry or experimental) — represents stated preferences, not revealed behavior
- "Willing to pay more" is stated willingness, not observed purchasing behavior — actual premium capture may differ
- The 7% premium is an average across willing consumers, not across all consumers
- 6-month action rate may not annualize linearly (one-time actions vs. ongoing behavior)
- The "privacy-aware vs. privacy-unaware" segmentation is based on self-reported awareness, not measured behavior
- Seven-market focus (European-heavy) — findings may not generalize to all global markets
