# ITU Focus Group on Trust and Identity for Humans and Agentic AI — Evidence Base

## Full Mandate Extraction

### The Initiative

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU), the UN agency for digital technologies, announced a new initiative to develop frameworks for trusted digital identity and to ensure that the behaviour of AI agents remains trustworthy and accountable throughout their lifecycle.

The new ITU Focus Group on Trust and Identity for Humans and Agentic AI was announced at the AI for Good Global Summit. It comes as AI evolves beyond assistive tools into autonomous agents acting on behalf of people.

### The Problem

While agentic AI promises major gains in productivity, it also introduces new risks:
- Autonomous agents impersonating people or organizations
- Taking unauthorized actions across interconnected systems
- The need for agents to identify and authenticate one another
- The need for agent decisions and actions to remain accountable, controllable, and trustworthy

### Three Core Challenge Areas

1. **Trust management for people and AI agents** — How humans and agents establish, maintain, and repair trust
2. **Overall trustworthiness of agentic AI systems** — Behavioural accountability throughout the lifecycle
3. **Meaningful human control** — Preserving human authority, especially for financial transactions and critical infrastructure

### Six Deliverables

The Focus Group will develop:

1. **Common terminology and definitions** — Shared vocabulary for agentic AI trust and identity
2. **Reference architectures** — For identity, trust, agent discovery, and interoperability
3. **Trust frameworks and lifecycle (assurance) models** — From deployment through continuous operation
4. **Interoperability mechanisms** — For digital identity and credentials across agent systems
5. **Security criteria and benchmarks** — For continuous assessment of AI agents
6. **Standardization roadmap** — Coordinating action across expert communities

### About ITU

The International Telecommunication Union is the United Nations agency for digital technologies, driving innovation for people and the planet with 194 Member States and a membership of over 1,000 companies, universities, civil society, and international and regional organizations. Established in 1865, ITU coordinates the global use of the radio spectrum and satellite orbits, establishes international technology standards, drives universal connectivity and digital services.

## Strategic Implications for A-Tech

### 1. First UN-Level Agentic AI Identity Standard

This is the first UN-level standardization initiative specifically for agentic AI identity and trust. Unlike the EU AI Act (regional regulatory compliance) or NIST AI RMF (national voluntary framework), this is a global standards initiative with 194 Member States. Products that align early gain a structural advantage in global enterprise procurement.

### 2. Identity + Trustworthiness = The Foundation

The ITU framework explicitly distinguishes:
- **Identity**: Who is acting (establishes the actor)
- **Trustworthiness**: How that actor can be expected to behave (establishes reliability)

Together they provide the foundation for safe interaction between humans and autonomous AI systems. This maps directly to A-Tech's existing trust-calibration-ux-pattern (trustworthiness) and verifiable-intent-agentic-trust-layer (identity credentials).

### 3. Meaningful Human Control as Core Principle

The ITU emphasizes preserving "meaningful human control for tasks such as executing financial transactions and operating critical infrastructure." This aligns with A-Tech's privacy-first architecture — local-first processing means the human retains the key; the agent cannot act without the human's device authorizing the action.

### 4. Agent-to-Agent Authentication

The ITU explicitly notes that "AI agents need to identify and authenticate one another." This validates the agent-protocol-stack-2026 skill (MCP, A2A, AP2) and the contribution-economy-trust-loop skill (W3C DID + VC for agent identity). The ITU will likely build interoperability mechanisms on top of these existing open standards.

### 5. Open-Source as Structural Advantage

The ITU's emphasis on trustworthiness through transparency, continuous assessment, and interoperability creates a structural advantage for open-source AI:
- Open code is auditable (trustworthiness through transparency)
- Public benchmarks and reproducible builds (continuous assessment)
- Open standards and protocols (interoperability)
- Community security audits (security criteria)
- Self-hostable agents (meaningful human control — the user controls the infrastructure)

Closed-source agent providers will face higher compliance costs because they cannot provide the same level of transparency and auditability.

### 6. Timeline Alignment

- **November 2026 (Paris)**: First meeting — terminology and definitions, initial reference architecture discussion
- **January 2027 (Geneva)**: Second meeting — trust frameworks, interoperability mechanisms
- **2027-2028**: Standard development and publication
- **2028+**: Enterprise procurement requirements begin incorporating ITU standards

A-Tech should:
1. Participate in the November 2026 meeting (open to all experts)
2. Build architecture aligned with existing open standards (W3C DID, VC, SD-JWT) that ITU will likely build upon
3. Prepare compliance evidence packs before standards are finalized
4. Position open-source as the trustworthiness advantage in marketing
