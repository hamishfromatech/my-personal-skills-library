# GAP Framework — Evidence Base

## Source
Costa, S., Mills, S., Duyck, W. & Dirix, N. (2026). "Advancing applied behavioral science: the GAP framework." *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 13(1), 261. DOI: 10.1057/s41599-026-06542-3

Published: 02 February 2026 (version of record 06 March 2026). Open Access (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0).

## Framework Structure

GAP = General Tools (G) + Algorithms (A) + Practical Considerations (P)

### General Tools
1. **SHELL mnemonic**: Social influence, Habits, Emotions, Limited cognitive processing, Limited willpower
2. **Behavioral audits**: Sludge audits (Sunstein 2022b/c), Bias audits (Fang et al. 2019; Morewedge et al. 2023), Noise audits (Kahneman et al. 2021)
3. **Choice architecture**: Münscher et al. (2016) taxonomy — Decision Information, Decision Structure, Decision Assistance

### Algorithms
1. **Enhanced Collection**: AI unlocks existing data sources; enables mega-studies (Milkman et al. 2021a/b)
2. **Enhanced Identification**: AI pattern detection for biases/noise (Ludwig & Mullainathan 2022; Mills et al. 2023); ML for habit formation (Buyalskaya et al. 2023 — 12M gym obs, 40M handwashing obs); behavioral systems mapping / causal Bayesian networks (Fung et al. 2023 — COVID vaccine intentions)
3. **Enhanced Efficiency**: Autonomous choice architects (Mills & Sætra 2024), adaptive nudging (Peer & Mills 2024), smart nudging (Mele et al. 2021)

### Practical Considerations (TEAM)
1. **Teams and Units**: centralized vs. decentralized vs. networked (World Bank 2019); multidisciplinary composition; strategic positioning statement (Soman & Feng 2023; Soman & N-Marandi 2022)
2. **Ethical and Legal**: FORGOOD framework (Lades & Delaney 2020); GDPR compliance; EU AI Act (2023)
3. **Affordability**: nudge ROI (Benartzi et al. 2017 — $100:$1 for retirement nudge vs. $1.24:$1 for tax incentives; 27.3 kWh/$1 social norm vs. 14.0 kWh/$1 education); Tor & Klick (2022) revision; noise audit savings (Kahneman et al. 2021 — hundreds of millions for insurance); sludge audit savings (Sunstein 2022b — TSA Precheck hundreds of millions)
4. **Methods**: RCTs, A/B testing, longitudinal, qualitative (OECD 2023 roadmap); culture of continuous experimentation

## Comparison to Existing Frameworks

| Framework | Strengths | What GAP Adds |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| COM-B (Michie et al. 2011) | Links diagnosis to intervention design via Behaviour Change Wheel | Organizational embedding, AI integration |
| MINDSPACE (Dolan et al. 2012) | Accessible nudge categories | Diagnostic depth (SHELL extends categories), audits, algorithms |
| EAST (BIT 2024) | Accessible design principles | Diagnosis-before-design structure, practical implementation layer |
| Behavioral Drivers Model (Petit 2019) | Comprehensive mapping in development contexts | Organizational/operational applicability, technology integration |
| 4S (Mills & Whittle 2024) | Evaluation framework | Full lifecycle (diagnosis + design + implementation) |

## Key Empirical Anchors

- Kleinberg et al. (2017): algorithms predict reoffending better than human judges with same data
- Buyalskaya et al. (2023): ML on 12M gym observations → habit formation takes months; 40M handwashing observations → weeks
- Milkman et al. (2021a): mega-studies enable apples-to-apples comparison of dozens of interventions
- Fung et al. (2023): causal Bayesian network for COVID vaccine intentions → 3 key causal factors (social responsibility, vaccine safety, anticipated regret)
- Kahneman et al. (2021): insurance executives believed expert judgment varied ≤10%; actual variation 43–55%
- Hallsworth (2023): "manifesto for applying behavioural science" — need for behavioral lens, not off-the-shelf problem solving
- Mills et al. (2023): "AI, behavioural science, and consumer welfare" — AI as noise-reducing technology, behavioral algorithms for real-time bias monitoring
- Soman & Feng (2023): six prescriptions for healthy BI units — vision, positioning statement, unique function

## Limitations (per original paper)
1. General Tools not exhaustive — not all behavior change techniques covered
2. May constrain discussions; miss "meta" diagnostic problem of whether issue is fundamentally behavioral
3. Ethical considerations increasingly pressing with AI; regulation may require revision of GAP components
4. Future work may formalize elements using design science methodologies for testability

## Adjacent Research (from same search batch)

### BOTTOM Framework (Ballicu 2026, Homo Oeconomicus 43(1))
- Integrates six validated taxonomies, extends MINDSPACE
- Decomposes each MINDSPACE category into six analytical dimensions: Brain, Orientation, Transparency, Triggers, Objective, Mind
- Shifts from fixed typologies to underlying mechanisms
- Applications: vegetable consumption, vaccine adherence, dark nudges in finance

### VEM-BC (Becker & Veling 2026, Health Psychology Review)
- Value and Experience-Based Model of Behaviour Change
- Adapts Value-based Choice Model of self-control
- Adds experiential component: affective experiences during behavior execution drive long-term change
- Central recommendation: create positive experiences with target behavior, not just reduce friction

### Context-Dependent Habit-Goal Interaction (Wagner et al. 2026, Communications Psychology 4:88)
- N=60, 600ms response deadline
- Habits strengthen through repetition (not reward alone)
- ~60% of participants context-modulate habit influence: exploit when congruent, inhibit when incongruent
- DDM: congruent habits shift starting-point bias (proactive control); incongruent habits reduce drift rate (reactive control)
- Habits as action sequences (Dezfouli & Balleine 2012), chunked by basal ganglia (Graybiel 1998)

### Habitlock (Economic Research Collective 2026, Zenodo)
- Economic behaviors persisting through habit formation after incentives disappear
- Formalizes state dependence and reinforcement
- Operationalizes with index: behavior persistence, incentive decay, switching friction, cue dependence

### Transparency in Nudging (Cuypers et al. 2026, Behavioural Public Policy)
- N=1916 vignette experiment, salience nudge for sustainable food
- Four disclosure types: presence, purpose, mechanism, combined
- Finding: disclosures neither enhanced nor reduced nudge effectiveness; nudge caused small but significant decrease in perceived autonomy that disclosures did not offset
- Implication: transparency is necessary but not sufficient for autonomy preservation
