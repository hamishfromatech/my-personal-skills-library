**UPDATE 2026-09-07 (cycle 21, run 1) — the intervention-spillover test with objective multi-utility data:**

The library's spillover evidence has relied on self-report and framed designs; this closes that gap. Ling, Liu & Xu (Journal of Environmental Psychology, vol. 110, March 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.jenvp.2026.102926) evaluated the cross-domain spillovers of a Home Energy Report–style social-norm nudge for household **waste sorting** ("Home Waste-Sorting Report," issued monthly by community committees in Guali Town, Hangzhou, China) on households' **water, electricity, and gas** use — a two-year natural field experiment, 1,788 households across 14 communities, opt-out design, objective utility data.

**Headline: on average, no significant spillovers** to water, electricity, or gas — despite a large positive target effect on waste sorting (monthly accurate-sorting days +27.0%, sorted-waste weight +33.8%; persistent-but-attenuated post-intervention at +16.7%/+20.7%). But the heterogeneity is the finding: **positive and persistent spillovers arise in specific localized institutional contexts** (communities without financial incentives for the target behavior and with low community social capital — the mechanisms analysis attributes suppressed spillovers to external justification via incentives and to extrinsic motivation under strong social capital/peer pressure). Even small spillovers "can substantially affect the cost-effectiveness of the nudge," and the paper cleanly separates *intervention spillovers* (an intervention affecting non-target behaviors) from *behavioral spillovers* (initial PEB affecting others) — a distinction most prior work conflates.

**Design rules added:** (1) always pre-register spillover outcomes alongside the target outcome — the null-on-average result would have been missed without the multi-utility instrumentation; (2) diagnose the institutional context first (incentivized target behaviors and high-social-capital communities suppress identity-based internalization); (3) measure spillovers with objective telemetry over multi-year horizons, not post-hoc surveys; (4) for any intervention, report target effect and spillover cost/benefit together when computing cost-effectiveness. The null-average result is methodologically convergent with `nudge-effectiveness-reality-check` and this skill's earlier bias-corrected framing: **cross-domain spillovers are not a default property of nudges — they are a property of specific contexts.**
