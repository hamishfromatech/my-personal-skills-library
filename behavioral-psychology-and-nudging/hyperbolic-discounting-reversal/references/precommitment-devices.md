# Precommitment Devices: Research and Applications

## Core Definition

A precommitment device is a choice made in the present that restricts future options, designed to overcome time-inconsistent preferences caused by hyperbolic discounting. Named after Ulysses binding himself to the mast to resist the Sirens.

## Key Research

### Thaler & Shefrin (1981) — The Planner-Doer Model
Thaler, R. H., & Shefrin, H. M. (1981). An economic theory of self-control. *Journal of Political Economy*, 89(2), 392–406.

- The "planner" wants to save; the "doer" wants to spend.
- Precommitment devices are how the planner binds the doer.

### Benartzi & Thaler (2004) — Save More Tomorrow
Benartzi, S., & Thaler, R. H. (2004). Save More Tomorrow: Using behavioral economics to increase employee saving. *Journal of Political Economy*, 112(S1), S164–S187.

- Showed that precommitment to future savings increases dramatically when framed as a future choice.
- Key insight: people are more willing to commit to future actions than present actions.

### Bryan, Karlan, & Nelson (2010) — Commitment Devices Review
Bryan, G., Karlan, D., & Nelson, S. (2010). Commitment devices. *Annual Review of Economics*, 2, 671–698.

- Comprehensive review of commitment devices across health, finance, and education.
- Found that commitment devices work best when:
  1. The future self is made salient
  2. The commitment is voluntary, not imposed
  3. The stakes are meaningful but not catastrophic
  4. There is social accountability

### Ashraf, Karlan, & Yin (2006) — Savings Commitments
Ashraf, N., Karlan, D., & Yin, W. (2006). Tying Odysseus to the mast: Evidence from a commitment savings product in the Philippines. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 121(2), 635–672.

- Field experiment showing that commitment savings accounts increased savings by 81%.
- Demonstrated that people will pay for commitment — they value the restriction.

## Applications to Developer Tools

| Precommitment Device | How It Works for A-Tech |
|---|---|
| One-time lifetime purchase | Locks in pricing, eliminates future subscription decisions |
| Annual plan with auto-renew | Less effective than lifetime; still requires periodic re-commitment |
| Founder's rate lock | Creates scarcity of commitment opportunity, not scarcity of product |
| Public ownership pledge | Social accountability increases follow-through |
| Future-self letter | Makes the beneficiary of precommitment vivid and personal |

## Ethical Precommitment

Precommitment is ethical when:
- The user understands the commitment and its consequences
- The commitment aligns with the user's stated values (ownership, privacy, financial freedom)
- The user can exit if circumstances genuinely change
- The device is framed as empowerment, not restriction

Precommitment is unethical when:
- It exploits present bias to trap users in unfavorable deals
- Exit is difficult or costly (dark patterns)
- The commitment serves the vendor, not the user
