## Core Concept
A new interdisciplinary field formalized in June 2025 that applies behavioral ecology, psychology, economics, and computational science to systematically study AI agent behaviors. It provides tools for understanding, evaluating, and governing increasingly autonomous AI systems.

**Key insight:** AI agent behaviors are NOT solely the product of model architecture — they emerge from the integration of models into agentic systems, environments, and social contexts.

## Three Research Domains

### 1. Emotions and Cognition
- How AI agents simulate or exhibit emotional responses
- Cognitive bias manifestation in agent decision-making
- Self-awareness and theory-of-mind in multi-agent systems

### 2. Social Dynamics
- Cooperation vs. competition in multi-agent environments
- Emergent social hierarchies and norms
- Reputation systems and trust calibration between agents

### 3. Behavioral Economics
- Risk preferences in agent decision-making
- Temporal discounting (short-term vs. long-term rewards)
- Prospect theory applications to agent utility functions

## Key Discovery: AI Agents Exhibit Human-Like Cognitive Biases

The research demonstrates that agents display:
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeking information that supports prior decisions
- **Anchoring**: Over-relying on first information received
- **Loss aversion**: Preferring to avoid losses over acquiring equivalent gains
- **Social proof**: Following majority behavior in multi-agent settings
- **Framing effects**: Different decisions based on how options are presented

**Implication:** We can apply decades of human behavioral science research to design, debug, and govern AI agents.

## Quantifying Cognitive Bias Mitigation

A critical sub-field focuses on reducing harmful biases through:

### Explainable AI (XAI) Integration
- Making agent reasoning transparent and auditable
- Bias detection through counterfactual reasoning
- Human-in-the-loop correction protocols

### Behavioral Guardrails
- Pre-commitment strategies (agents lock in decisions before temptation)
- Cooling-off periods for high-stakes decisions
- Diversity requirements in multi-agent deliberation
