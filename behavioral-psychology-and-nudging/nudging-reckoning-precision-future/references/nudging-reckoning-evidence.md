# Nudging Reckoning — Evidence Base

## Source 1: "After Nudging: The Rise and Fall of a Behavioral Economics Fad"

**Author:** Richard Morrison (reviewing "It's On You" by Nick Chater and George Loewenstein)
**Publication:** The Daily Economy (AIER)
**Date:** May 1, 2026
**URL:** https://thedailyeconomy.org/article/after-nudging-the-rise-and-fall-of-a-behavioral-economics-fad/

### Summary

Nick Chater and George Loewenstein — leading researchers in behavioral psychology and economics who previously worked on nudge theory — have turned from advocates to critics. Their book "It's On You" argues that nudging is both ineffective and actively harmful.

### Key Arguments from "It's On You"

1. **Nudge interventions are ineffective long-term:** After a first flush of success, nudge behavioralism yielded disappointing long-term results. Upon wider reading and investigation, the authors grew disenchaned with their own specialty.

2. **Nudges are actively harmful:** People concerned about societal problems can be seduced by the supposed effectiveness of nudges, thus eroding support for more aggressive interventions. The "seduction" effect: nudges make policymakers think problems are solved when they aren't.

3. **The proposed alternative — mandates:** The authors claim major problems can only be solved by "flushing away any pretense of voluntary inducement" and going full speed ahead on banning/mandating behaviors. They consider it "absurd and unjust" to expect anyone to manage their own choices.

### Their Proposed Mandate Scope

Under their plan, the government would decide:
- Which health care plan you have
- How much and under what conditions you can gamble
- How you interact with social media
- What vehicle you are allowed to drive
- How much you save for retirement
- THC content of cannabis you consume
- How often you are allowed to fly (climate-based rationing)
- Diet regulation ("ultraprocessed" foods discouraged, sugary drinks regulated, meat consumption flagged)

### Critical Assessment (Morrison's Review)

- The critique represents "the zeal of the convert" — former advocates doing "righteous penance"
- The book is "shrill and devoid of self-awareness"
- Their alternative is "an alarming and deeply illiberal vision"
- They argue no "thoughtful and well-informed" person could disagree — structurally necessary for their argument
- The book includes "a few reasonable policy proposals" (cutting agricultural subsidies, eliminating tax loopholes) but "in total presents an alarming and deeply illiberal vision"
- The authors don't recognize meaningful limits to their anti-libertarian paternalism
- "If we don't trust ordinary Americans to choose their own vehicles or play high-limit slot machines, why would we allow them to vote and serve on juries?"

### The Diagnostic vs. Prescriptive Split

**Diagnostic (has merit):** Nudges have disappointing long-term results. The initial success didn't scale or persist.

**Prescription (extreme):** The answer is comprehensive government mandates over virtually all personal decisions. This is a false binary that ignores the boosting alternative.

## Source 2: Precision Nudging Research

**Publication:** "Differential Impacts of Combined Nudge Strategies Across Age..."
**Journal:** Journal of Behavioral Economics and Finance (ScienceDirect)
**Date:** June 4, 2026
**DOI Reference:** S2214804326000893

### Summary

The research advances the paradigm of **"precision nudging"** — offering actionable insights for designing targeted interventions that optimize long-term outcomes.

### Key Finding

Nudge effectiveness varies significantly across demographic segments (age in particular). Combined nudge strategies have differential impacts across age groups. Population-level nudges that ignore this heterogeneity are suboptimal.

### Implication

The one-size-fits-all nudge is failing. The future of nudging (if it has one) is precision/targeted nudging that adapts to individual or segment characteristics. This aligns with the existing nudging-mental-health-evidence-synthesis finding that "tailored nudges" are a critical research priority.

### Connection to A-Tech

This research validates A-Tech's skepticism of population-level nudges and supports the precision-nudging approach for path-clearing, while boosting remains the primary intervention for building durable competence.

## Source 3: The Boosting Paradigm (Hertwig et al., 2025)

**Full details:** Already extracted in the boosting-empowering-behavior-change skill's references file.

### Key Points Relevant to the Reckoning

1. **The deficit model is insufficient:** Nudging's deficit model — leveraging or working around human biases — is insufficient for complex, collective challenges.
2. **Boosting builds competence:** The competence model builds people's capacities to make informed choices.
3. **Six proven boost domains:** Digital literacy, risk literacy, financial literacy, statistical reasoning, health literacy, decision-making.
4. **Boosts are durable:** Transferable across contexts, unlike context-dependent nudges.
5. **Self-nudging bridges the gap:** Citizens become their own choice architects (Reijula & Hertwig, 2022).
