# BOTTOM Nudge Analysis Framework — Evidence Base

## Primary Source

Ballicu, G. — "Which Nudge is Better to Use? BOTTOM, a Holistic Framework for the Analysis of Nudges" (Homo Oeconomicus, Vol. 43, Issue 1, pp. 1-28, December 2026). University of Cagliari, Department of Economics and Business. DOI: 10.1007/s41412-026-00154-7.

## The Framework

BOTTOM (Brain, Orientation, Transparency, Triggers, Objective, Mind) integrates six validated taxonomies and extends the widely used MINDSPACE framework. Rather than replacing MINDSPACE, BOTTOM decomposes each of its nine nudge categories into six analytical dimensions, shifting attention from fixed typologies to the underlying mechanisms through which nudges operate.

## The Six Dimensions

1. **Brain** — Which cognitive or neural mechanism does the nudge engage?
2. **Orientation** — Automatic (System 1) or deliberative (System 2) processing?
3. **Transparency** — Is the nudge visible and disclosed?
4. **Triggers** — What specific behavioral trigger is deployed?
5. **Objective** — What behavior is the nudge trying to change?
6. **Mind** — What is the target psychological state or mental model?

## Application Examples (from the paper)

- Nudges to increase vegetable consumption
- Vaccine adherence
- Dark nudges in financial decision-making

## Integrates Six Validated Taxonomies

The paper integrates six validated nudge taxonomies (the six are decomposed across the BOTTOM dimensions). The MINDSPACE framework (Messenger, Incentives, Norms, Defaults, Salience, Priming, Affect, Commitment, Ego) is the most widely used and serves as the base that BOTTOM extends.

## Corroborating Sources

### The PNAS Meta-Analysis (Mertens et al., 2021)
- 447 effect sizes from 212 publications, n = 2,148,439
- Overall effect size: Cohen's d = 0.43 (95% CI [0.38, 0.48])
- Decision structure interventions consistently outperform decision information and decision assistance
- Food choices: d = 0.65 (2.5× larger than other domains)
- Financial decisions: d = 0.24 (smallest)
- ~15% of interventions backfire (95% prediction interval [-0.36, 1.22])
- Moderate publication bias toward positive results

BOTTOM explains the mechanism behind these findings: decision structure nudges share the Orientation dimension (automatic processing, lower cognitive engagement), while decision information nudges require deliberative processing that exceeds cognitive limits under load.

### The 789-Intervention Database (Gandhi, Kiyawat, Tipton, Watts, 2026)
- 789 interventions from 280 randomized controlled experiments (2018-2023)
- Coded on 298 variables describing domain, geography, population, behavior, intervention techniques, standardized effect sizes, experimental design
- Variables from multiple behavioral science taxonomies
- Analysis-ready database, visual intervention stimuli, coding templates, code, interactive explorer — all open resources

This database enables mechanism-based meta-analysis (grouping by BOTTOM dimensions) rather than just type-based aggregation.

### Nudge Persistence (Brandon, Ferraro, List, Metcalfe, Price, Rundhammer — Review of Economic Studies, 2026)
- 38 natural field experiments on energy consumption nudges
- Two channels: technology adoption and habit formation
- Fully half of energy reductions persist after treatment ends (resident moves, treatment discontinued)
- Persistence is consonant with technology adoption channel
- "The role of technology in creating persistent behaviour change has important implications for designing behavioural interventions"

BOTTOM's Mind dimension (target psychological state) connects to this: technology adoption changes the physical context (a BOTTOM Objective dimension factor), while habit formation changes the psychological state (a BOTTOM Mind dimension factor). The persistence finding suggests that nudges targeting technology adoption (Objective) produce more durable change than nudges targeting habit formation (Mind) alone.

### Nudge Transparency and Autonomy (Cuypers, Raymaekers, Van de Walle — Behavioural Public Policy, 2026)
- Vignette survey experiment, n = 1,916
- Salience nudge for sustainable food choices, four different disclosures
- Nudge proved effective; disclosures neither enhanced nor reduced effectiveness
- Nudge led to small but significant decrease in perceived autonomy, which disclosures did not offset
- Differences between disclosure types not substantial enough for significant effects

BOTTOM's Transparency dimension integrates this finding: transparency does not compromise effectiveness, but does not fully mitigate autonomy concerns either. The Transparency dimension is necessary but not sufficient for ethical nudge design.

### How Nudges Create Habits (Byrne, Goette, Martin, Miles, Jones, Schob, Staake, Tiefenbeck — SSRN, 2024)
- Field experiment monitoring behavioral responses at high frequency during and post-treatment
- Asymmetry: treatment effects emerge immediately, neither grow nor wane with continued treatment, gradually decay after nudges stop
- Decay takes longer the longer the duration of treatment
- Dynamic attention-based mechanism best predicts consumption responses

BOTTOM's Brain dimension (cognitive mechanism) connects to this: the attention-based mechanism (Brain=attention allocation) explains the dynamics that the consumption-based habit model does not.

### What Makes a Habit (Bürgler, Milyavskaya, McMillan, Burns, Lally, Gardner — Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2026)
- Preregistered reanalysis of 6-month longitudinal study, N = 254
- Two trajectories: habit formation and habit degradation
- Six determinants significantly associated with habit strength: autonomous motivation, behavior frequency, context stability, feeling energized, feeling pleased (positive), perceived difficulty (negative)
- Significant interaction between time and behavior frequency

BOTTOM's Mind dimension (target psychological state) connects: autonomous motivation and positive affect (Mind dimensions) are determinants of habit formation, not just outcomes.

### The Value and Experience-Based Model of Behaviour Change (VEM-BC) (Becker & Veling — Health Psychology Review, 2026)
- Value-based decision making perspective on behavior change
- Adapts the Value-based Choice Model of self-control
- Incorporates experiential component: affective experiences during behavior execution are crucial for long-term change
- Central recommendation: interventions should aim at creating positive experiences with the desired target behavior

BOTTOM's Mind dimension connects: the target psychological state (positive affective experience) is the mechanism for long-term behavior change, not just the behavioral outcome.
