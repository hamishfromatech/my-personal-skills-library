# Nudge Persistence: Technology Adoption vs Habit Formation — Evidence Base

## Primary Source

**Brandon, A., Ferraro, P. J., List, J. A., Metcalfe, R. D., Price, M. K., & Rundhammer, F. (2026).** "Do The Effects of Nudges Persist? Theory and Evidence from 38 Natural Field Experiments." *The Review of Economic Studies*, rdag012.

### Study Design
- **Scale:** 38 natural field experiments on energy-consumption social-comparison nudges (Opower-style home energy reports)
- **Persistence test:** Compare treatment and control homes *after the initial resident moves*, which discontinues the treatment for the home (the new resident never received reports)
- **Key finding:** Fully **half of the energy reductions persist** in the home after treatment ends
- **Mechanism:** Persistence is consonant with a **technology adoption channel** — the nudge triggered durable changes (efficient appliances, thermostat settings, insulation) that remain in the home after the treated resident departs
- **Implication:** The role of technology in creating persistent behavior change is large and underappreciated; long-term social impact evaluations that ignore the technology channel will underestimate persistence

### Theoretical Framework
The study formalizes a research design to disentangle two channels:
1. **Technology adoption:** The nudge induces investment in a durable good or configuration that changes the default operating state. The effect persists because the technology persists.
2. **Habit formation:** The nudge creates a learned cue-response association. The effect persists because the habit persists, but decays without reinforcement.

The Becker-Murphy (1988) theory of rational addiction and the Pollak (1970) habit-formation framework provide the theoretical scaffolding. The key empirical insight is that technology adoption creates a "ratchet" — the durable artifact locks in the behavior change.

### Prior Persistence Evidence
- **Allcott & Rogers (2014, AER):** Energy nudges show short-run and long-run effects; the short-run effect fades but the long-run effect persists at ~50% — consistent with Brandon et al.
- **Bernedo, Ferraro & Price (2014, J. Consumer Policy):** Norm-based water-conservation messaging has persistent impacts, consistent with durable changes
- **Frey & Rogers (2014, Policy Insights):** Persistence framework for behavioral interventions

### Connection to Habit Formation Literature
- **Byrne et al. (2024, SSRN):** "How Nudges Create Habits" — high-frequency monitoring of water-consumption nudges finds asymmetry: effects emerge immediately, neither grow nor wane during treatment, and decay gradually after cessation. Decay time is proportional to treatment duration. An attention-based (not consumption-based) habit model best fits the data.
- **Bürgler et al. (2026, PSPB):** Six determinants of habit strength — autonomous motivation, behavior frequency, context stability, feeling energized, feeling pleased (positive), perceived difficulty (negative)

### Design Implications for A-Tech

| A-Tech Value | Alignment |
|---|---|
| **Open-source AI** | Technology-adoption nudges align with open-source philosophy: the "technology" is often an open tool that, once adopted, persists |
| **Practical implementation** | Designing for persistence means choosing the technology pathway over the attention pathway — more engineering, more durable outcome |
| **Financial freedom** | Financial nudges (auto-enroll in savings, debt-payoff automation) that trigger a durable configuration change will persist; pure reminder nudges will not |

### Implications for Intervention Design
1. **If persistence is the goal, prefer technology adoption over repeated cues.** A nudge that triggers enrollment in an auto-saving program persists; a nudge that merely reminds people to save does not.
2. **Measure the artifact, not just the behavior.** The persistence carrier is the durable technology; track its installation/configuration rate as a leading indicator of persistence.
3. **Time nudges to transition windows.** The cost of adopting a durable technology is lowest during life transitions (move-in, onboarding, renewal) — this is when technology-adoption nudges are most effective.
4. **Long-term social impact evaluations must model the technology channel.** Ignoring it underestimates persistence by ~50%.
