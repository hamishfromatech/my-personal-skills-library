# AI Consumer Behavior & Brand Relationship — Evidence Base

## Primary Source

**Ribeiro, A., Rivero, A.J.L., Abrantes, J.L.** (2025). "The impact of artificial intelligence on consumer behavior towards brands: a systematic review." *Electronic Commerce Research*. Open access. DOI: 10.1007/s10660-025-10063-7. Published November 7, 2025.

## Methodology

- Systematic literature review (PRISMA methodology)
- Web of Science (WOS) database
- Search terms: "Artificial Intelligence" AND "Consumer Behaviour"
- Filters: Article type, Business Economy + Communication area, English language
- Initial: 373 articles (11,888 total citations, 1991–2025)
- VOSviewer analysis: bibliographic coupling, minimum 20 citations → 132 articles in 5 clusters
- AI-assisted review with Sciscape software
- Keyword co-occurrence: 1,393 keywords, minimum 5 occurrences → 38 keywords in 7 clusters

## Cluster Details

### Cluster 1: Consumer Experience — The Potential of AI (37 articles, 1991–2024)

Key findings:
- Voice assistants provide emotional + informational support via personalized recommendations
- Convenience and speed are primary adoption drivers
- Humanization leads to pleasant interactions and regular use
- Both emotional and functional bonds moderated by social + technical attributes
- Trust is vital; brand credibility moderates the relationship
- Emotional value drives positive word-of-mouth
- AI cuteness elicits attachment → satisfaction, commitment, purchase intention
- Too much humanization not always better (disappointment risk)
- Social influence (friends, family) affects chatbot adoption
- Personalized suggestions based on habits drive loyalty
- Digital natives comfortable with innovations but less comfortable with AI-enabled banking
- Consumers have variety of purchasing strategies (brand preference, promotion-driven, loyal)
- Personalized experiences increasingly important
- Security/privacy concerns, lack of human interaction, complexity may deter individuals
- Ethical perspectives on autonomous vehicles relate to adoption intentions

### Cluster 2: The Relationship with AI (26 articles, 2018–2024)

Key findings:
- Positive attitudes toward AI service robots post-pandemic (safety provision)
- Humanized appearance + interaction essential, but chatting doesn't always increase satisfaction
- Consumers perceive robots with human/social traits + superior AI as more competent
- Competence perception reduces skepticism
- Human social presence enhances satisfaction vs. humanized robots
- Prevention-focused consumers prefer humanized robots (safer advisors)
- Combining human + robot employees may be advantageous
- Prolonged machine-human relationships → dependence, diminished genuine human interaction
- More human + friendly AI → continued interaction, brand connection, more purchases
- Too much human likeness can cause discomfort
- Consumer expectations increase with similarity; unmet expectations → adverse reactions
- AI influences decision-making, trust, recommendations, interactions, satisfaction, sharing, security
- Security concerns (data theft, misuse), ethics, access inequalities, unfair competition
- Need to study long-term social impact of robot inclusion
- Self-AI concept: AI becomes part of person's identity
- Self-endorsement enhances perceived personal relevance → stronger purchase intentions
- Digital dementia: cognitive decline from technology dependence
- When robots achieve self-awareness, they take on brand expression roles

### Cluster 3: Online Challenges (25 articles, 2009–2024)

Key findings:
- AI enables optimization, customer management, B2B markets, decision-making, marketing strategies
- Predictive analytics essential for understanding market needs
- Consumer data is important resource when used effectively
- Big Data essential for understanding consumer needs and decision-making
- AI valuable for data analysis, marketing decision-making, automation
- AI helps predict behavior patterns, understand attitudes, adapt strategies
- AI measures feelings more objectively, faster, less costly than traditional methods
- Personalized, segmented, targeted content
- Interpretative AI personalizes advertising campaigns based on consumer behavior data
- Personality-message match enhances persuasion
- AI successful when prioritizing convenience over companionship
- E-WOM crucial in digital marketing; AI makes it more engaging
- Gen Z strongly influenced by customization enabled by technology
- AI influencers interact naturally, personally → engagement + brand impact
- Choice of AI depends on functionality, adaptability, communication, relationships, privacy, individual psychology
- Online commerce challenges: trust, security (payments, privacy, authentication)
- Design and navigation factors crucial for purchasing decisions, trust, loyalty
- AI more efficient for daily/low-involvement purchases than complex tasks
- Must ensure AI doesn't accentuate prejudices, maintain user autonomy

### Cluster 4: The Functionality of AI (22 articles, 2019–2024)

Key findings:
- AI impact on marketing gradual; greater effectiveness when collaborating with professionals, not replacing
- AI enables engaging, tailored campaigns → exclusivity → satisfaction, loyalty
- Predictive capacity: personalized recommendations (Netflix model)
- AI challenges managers in strategy, decision-making, CRM
- AI impacts labor markets, productivity, skills, innovation, organizational structures
- When both service provider and client are AI → unique AI-to-AI relationship
- AI as customer base increases, potentially replacing human clients
- Consumer resistance to role transfer (human → AI) from negative perceptions
- AI services must align with consumer motives, concerns, values
- Positive e-WOM gained prominence since pandemic, surpassing personal recommendations
- Power of data enables targeted strategies, accurate products/services, trend prediction
- Transparency and care reduce perceived risk in online purchases
- Conversational AI: consumers seek companionship, emotional support, personal growth
- Consumers prefer cute AI for simple interactions; less cute for complex tasks (competence perception)
- Branding must balance humans and machines for easier acceptance
- New technologies can induce stress; marketing can help familiarize
- Protecting customers against security hazards essential
- Privacy innovation → market advantage → consumer trust

### Cluster 5: Autonomous and Automated Purchasing (22 articles, 2020–2025)

Key findings:
- Top-of-mind awareness still essential
- Utilitarian products: lower AI humanization → higher purchase intention
- Hedonic products: anthropomorphism enhances brand evaluation and purchase intention
- Ideology influences adoption (stronger political ideology → more AI checkout acceptance)
- Competence-oriented brand personality → higher AI customer service acceptance
- Psychological barriers: loss of control, fear of losing experiences/individuality, emotional aspect
- AI-enabled checkouts accepted when convenient
- AI in purchasing decisions saves time, changes decision process
- Success of technological autonomy depends on humans — expansion-oriented, human-led approach
- Consumer guilt diminishes with AI interaction → increased unethical behavior
- Less human-like AI → less guilt → more unethical behavior
- More humanized AI → reduced unethical behavior
- Social judgment and consumer feedback management mitigate unethical behavior
- Generative AI for content creation perceived as less authentic → negative behaviors (reduced credibility, weaker e-WOM, lower loyalty)
- Consumer brand perception can mitigate negative effects
- AI personalized advertising in charity may reduce donations (autonomy undermining)
- Healthcare: brain activity shows greater connection with human doctors vs AI
- Financial: consumers prefer experienced human advisors
- Convenience of AI recommendations gaining ground over traditional brand name
- Digital education → positive experiences, loyalty
- Showrooming facilitated by AI boosts online sales
- AI adoption in live streaming increases with streaming power
- AI systems making decisions → reduced consumer time/effort but concerns about losing control, monopolization

## Conceptual Model

Five clusters share specific characteristics:
1. **Customization** — enhances consumer experience across humanized tech, automation, functionality, platforms
2. **Value + trust** — expected to meet skepticism; security concerns transversal
3. **Dependence risk** — convenience may affect genuine human relationships (Clusters 2, 3)
4. **Security/privacy** — transversal concern across all clusters
5. **Personalization** — central, but must balance with autonomy

## Research Questions Answered

### RQ1: What is known about the impact of AI on consumer behavior?
AI impacts consumer behavior through convenience, effectiveness, trust, security, and personalization. The relationship is complex, varies by context, and depends on individual traits. Personalization is important but context-dependent. Humanization appreciated in specific contexts, not others. Higher-value decisions benefit from efficiency and security over empathy.

### RQ2: Does AI exposure influence consumer behavior towards brands?
Yes. AI has impacted and will continue to impact the consumer-brand relationship. Several factors must be analyzed: consumer group characteristics, tool design, context. The five pillars (convenience, effectiveness, trust, security, personalization) collectively influence purchase decisions and loyalty.

5. Perceive consumer motivation influences engagement and anthropomorphism perception
