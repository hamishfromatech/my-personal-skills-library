## Theoretical Foundation

### The Deficit Model Critique
The nudge approach (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008) is rooted in the heuristics-and-biases program (Kahneman & Tversky, 1974), which interpreted Herbert Simon's bounded rationality concept to mean systematic biases in human judgment. The deficit model frames people as cognitively and motivationally deficient — they need behavioral engineers to leverage or overcome their limitations.

Hertwig et al. argue this model:
1. **Deprioritizes effective strategies** — nudging crowds out justifiably expensive or coercive interventions (taxes, bans, mass media campaigns) that may be more effective
2. **Absolves governments/institutions of systemic responsibility** — framing issues as individual decision failures diverts attention from systemic drivers
3. **Underestimates collective capacity** — COVID-19 showed that shared identity and community sustain adherence to demanding measures, contradicting the "behavioral fatigue" assumption
4. **Neglects the role of trust** — governments assuming public deficiency don't build the trust that is critical for behavior change

### The Competence Model Alternative
The boosting approach (Hertwig & Grüne-Yanoff, 2017) works by harnessing and building on human strengths. Boosts are interventions designed to improve people's competences to make informed choices that align with their goals, preferences, and desires.

## The Six Boost Categories (with tried-and-tested examples)

### 1. Digital Literacy
- **Lateral reading** (Kozyreva et al., 2024; Wineburg et al., 2022): Professional fact-checkers assess credibility by leaving the site and searching for information about the source, rather than evaluating the site's own content. Field-tested in high school government classes district-wide.

### 2. Risk Literacy
- **Experienced simulations** (Wegwarth et al., 2022): Simulated experiences of risks help people understand temporal and cumulative nature of health risks, outperforming descriptive formats for chronic pain patients understanding opioid risks.

### 3. Financial Literacy
- **Simple rules of thumb** (Drexler, Fischer & Schoar, 2014): Teaching microentrepreneurs to separate business and private accounts using physical analogues (two boxes, two ledgers) improved financial outcomes more than standard accounting training.
- **Amortization bias reduction** (Foltice, 2017): Experience-based rules decrease the tendency to misunderstand compound interest and exponential growth.

### 4. Statistical Reasoning
- **Frequency representations** (Sedlmeier & Gigerenzer, 2001): Training people to convert probability representations into frequency representations ("1 in 10") improves Bayesian reasoning, with effects persisting weeks later.
- **Debiasing through contrast** (Morewedge et al., 2015; Franiatte et al., 2024): Showing correct solutions alongside typical biased responses in single or repeated training trials improves reasoning.

### 5. Health Literacy
- **Drug label comprehension** (Sahm et al., 2012): Patient-centered drug instructions improve understanding and adherence.
- **Implementation intentions** (Oettingen, Hönig & Gollwitzer, 2000): "If-then" planning improves self-regulation across health behaviors.

### 6. Decision-Making
- **Decision trees** (Katsikopoulos et al., 2021): Transparent classification trees support decision-making in finance, medicine, HR, and science education, often outperforming complex statistical models while being intuitively understandable.

## Self-Nudging

Reijula & Hertwig (2022) define self-nudging as the practice where the individual serves as both nudger and nudgee — a "citizen choice architect." This addresses:
- Paternalism objections (the user chooses their own nudge)
- Preference heterogeneity (the user knows their own preferences)
- Agency concerns (the user builds meta-cognitive awareness of choice architecture)

**Example:** The "one sec" app (Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, 2023, PNAS) empowers users to reduce social media consumption by introducing friction they design themselves (introduction delays, breathing exercises). Published in PNAS with demonstrated effectiveness.

## The COM-B Model

Michie, van Stralen & West (2011) — the Behaviour Change Wheel: all behaviors involve an interaction of:
- **C**apability (competence)
- **O**pportunity (environmental enablement)
- **M**otivation (goals, beliefs, emotional responses)

Boosting must address all three. Competence is only useful if the environment provides opportunity and the person has motivation to deploy it.

## Failure Modes of Nudging That Boosting Addresses

From Banerjee, Grüne-Yanoff, John & Moseley (2024, *Behavioral Public Policy*):
1. **Non-persistent treatment effects** — nudged behavior reverts when nudge is removed
2. **Compensatory negative spillovers** — improvement in one domain causes degradation in another
3. **Psychological reactance and backfiring** — perceived manipulation triggers oppositional behavior
