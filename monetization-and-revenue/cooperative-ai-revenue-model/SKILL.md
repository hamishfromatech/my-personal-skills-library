# Cooperative AI Revenue Model

## Overview

The dominant AI economy operates through what researchers call an "extraction stack" — vertically integrated control of hardware, cloud, models, labor, and applications by a handful of corporations. A growing body of research and practice in 2025–2026 demonstrates that cooperative ownership structures can create viable, often superior alternatives. The "solidarity stack" reclaims each layer: community-owned infrastructure, democratic data stewardship, dignified labor governance, and collectively governed knowledge.

This skill operationalizes cooperative economics for AI revenue generation, directly aligned with A-Tech's open-source and financial freedom values.

## The Solidarity Stack Framework

| Layer | Extraction Model | Cooperative Alternative | A-Tech Application |
|-------|-----------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Earth & Infrastructure** | Corporate cloud monopolies (AWS/Azure/GCP = 66% market) | Federated community servers, cooperative clouds (Co-op Cloud, Commons Cloud, Hostsharing eG) | A-Coder self-hosted tier; Builder's Club shared compute pool |
| **Data** | Raw material pulled from users without consent | Data cooperatives (MIDATA, Superset, Driver's Seat) with fiduciary governance | Be Practical zero-party data consent; federated training data pools |
| **Labor** | Hidden workforce treated as liabilities | Worker-owned cooperatives (Gamayyar African Tech Workers, Facttic, Outlandish) | Open-source contributor ownership; maintainer cooperative |
| **Knowledge** | Black-box models with imposed values | Open models with collective governance (Apertus, AI4Coops) | Builder's Club community model governance; explainable-by-default products |
| **Application** | Rent-seeking platforms | Worker-owned platforms with OpenCourier-style interoperability | Agent marketplace with cooperative revenue sharing |

## The Cooperative Revenue Architecture

### Data Cooperative Revenue
Users pool data, retain control, and collectively negotiate access:
- **Membership fee:** Small recurring fee to maintain cooperative infrastructure
- **Data licensing:** Negotiated access fees from model trainers, with revenue distributed to members
- **Quality premium:** Higher fees for verified, high-quality, consented data
- **Derivative rights:** Members retain ownership of insights derived from their data

### Platform Cooperative Revenue
Worker-owned AI platforms that compete with extractive alternatives:
- **Service fees:** Transparent markup (e.g., 5–15%) on AI services rendered
- **Collective bargaining:** Platform negotiates enterprise contracts on behalf of all worker-owners
- **Reinvestment mandate:** Fixed percentage (e.g., 30%) reinvested into open-source core
- **Exit to community:** Transition path from startup to cooperative ownership

### Open-Source Cooperative Revenue
Combining open-source distribution with cooperative economic structure:
- **Dual licensing with cooperative twist:** Commercial licenses fund cooperative, not shareholders
- **Contributor dividends:** Revenue distributed to active contributors based on contribution quality and longevity
- **Enterprise cooperative membership:** Companies pay membership for support + governance voice
- **Grant + revenue hybrid:** Foundation grants seed development; cooperative revenue sustains operations

## Federated Cooperative Governance

### The OpenCourier Protocol Model
OpenCourier demonstrates how cooperative platforms interconnect:
- **Directory:** Platforms discover each other without corporate gatekeeper
- **Common language:** Shared protocol lets any cooperative platform exchange orders/data
- **Transparent management:** Publicly available tools for order routing and revenue sharing
- **No middleman extraction:** Value flows directly between cooperatives and their members

### Governance Patterns for AI Co-ops
| Pattern | Description | Best For |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **One member, one vote** | Equal democratic control regardless of contribution size | Small, values-aligned co-ops |
| **Sensible stake-weighted** | Voting proportional to verified contribution (code, data, capital) | Larger technical co-ops |
| **Multi-stakeholder** | Separate governance tracks for users, workers, and community | Platform co-ops with diverse participants |
| **Liquid democracy** | Delegated voting with instant recall | Fast-moving technical decisions |

## Revenue Distribution Models

### The 30-30-30-10 Rule
A practical allocation for cooperative AI revenue:
- **30% to contributors:** Dividends based on contribution tracking (git commits, documentation, support, design)
- **30% to operations:** Infrastructure, legal, marketing, community management
- **30% to reinvestment:** R&D, new features, open-source core improvements
- **10% to solidarity fund:** Supporting smaller co-ops, diversity initiatives, education

### Contribution Tracking Mechanics
- **Code:** Weighted by complexity, review count, and maintenance burden
- **Documentation:** Weighted by page views, support ticket reduction, translation
- **Community:** Weighted by event organization, mentorship, onboarding help
- **Capital:** Weighted by risk and duration (equity-like but non-voting or limited-voting)

## Challenge Mitigations

| Challenge | Cooperative Response |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Capital intensity | Federated finance: multiple co-ops pool resources; public-cooperative partnerships (LESTAC model) |
| Network effects | Protocol interoperability (OpenCourier approach); shared standards prevent fragmentation |
| Regulatory uncertainty | Clear legal structure from inception (cooperative corporation + fiduciary governance); policy advocacy via Cooperative Councils Innovation Network |
| Talent competition | Ownership + dignity as retention mechanism; profit-sharing aligned with mission |
| Scale limitations | Federation: independent co-ops share protocols and standards without merging |

## A-Tech Application Areas

### A-Coder
- **Cooperative self-hosted tier:** Users can join a cooperative cloud for shared compute, reducing individual cloud costs by 40–60%
- **Contributor ownership:** Open-source contributors to A-Coder core receive revenue share from enterprise licensing
- **Federated model training:** Cooperative members train shared code intelligence model without exposing proprietary code

### Be Practical
- **Data cooperative option:** Readers who contribute anonymized learning data become members of a Be Practical data cooperative, sharing in revenue from adaptive content licensing
- **Exit to community pathway:** Be Practical transitions from sole proprietorship to cooperative ownership as scale permits
- **Solidarity pricing:** Sliding scale based on member income, with surplus redistributed to education funds

### Builder's Club
- **Cooperative marketplace:** Agent services and MCP servers sold through a cooperative marketplace with 85% to creator, 15% to shared infrastructure
- **Open-source cooperative dividend:** Revenue from enterprise licensing of Builder's Club open-source tools distributed to contributors
- **Global federation:** Local Builder's Club chapters operate as independent cooperatives connected via shared protocols

## Measurement Framework

| Metric | Description | Target |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Member ownership clarity | % of members who can explain their ownership rights | ≥ 80% |
| Revenue per member | Annual cooperative revenue / active member | Sustainable living wage |
| Reinvestment ratio | % of revenue reinvested in open-source core | ≥ 30% |
| Democratic participation | % of members voting in key decisions | ≥ 50% |
| Exit readiness | Months of runway if all members left | ≥ 6 |
| Inter-cooperative connections | Number of active protocol connections to other co-ops | ≥ 3 |

## Ethical Boundaries
- **No co-op-washing:** Genuine workplace democracy, not marketing veneer
- **Transparency by default:** All financials, governance decisions, and contribution weights public
- **Fiduciary duty to members:** Legal obligation to act in members' collective interest
- **Anti-concentration:** No single member or member class can capture control
- **Open protocols:** Cooperative success depends on interoperability, not lock-in
