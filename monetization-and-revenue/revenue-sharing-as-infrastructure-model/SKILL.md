# Revenue-Sharing as Infrastructure (RSI) Model

## Overview
RSI is a third-generation business model for generative AI platforms where the platform offers its AI infrastructure for free and takes a percentage of the revenues generated by developers' applications. This reverses the traditional upstream payment logic (pay-per-token, freemium, subscription) and aligns platform incentives with developer success. The platform only earns when developers earn.

## When to Use
- Designing open-source AI platform monetization strategies
- Building developer ecosystems with zero entry barriers
- Creating AI agent marketplace economics
- Designing revenue-share models for AI infrastructure
- Building platforms that align incentives with developer success
- Creating business models for emerging-market AI ecosystems

- NOT for platforms that cannot track developer revenue (requires payment integration)
- NOT for pure API consumption models without application layer
- NOT without legal review (revenue-share contracts are complex; regulatory risk exists)

## Core Process / Workflow

### 1. Define the Platform-Developer Relationship
- Platform provides AI infrastructure (models, APIs, compute) for free
- Developer builds applications using the infrastructure
- Developer integrates the platform's payment system (mandatory)
- Platform takes a percentage (typically 20-30%) of developer revenue

### 2. Set the Commission Rate (α)
The optimal commission rate balances two opposing effects:
- **Higher α:** More revenue per developer, but fewer developers participate and reduced effort
- **Lower α:** More developers and higher effort, but less revenue per developer

**Simple model (illustrative):**
If developer revenue R = effort e, cost φ(e) = ½e², requests q = e, and N(α) = 1:
- Developer maximizes: (1-α)e - ½e² → e* = 1-α
- Platform profit: Π(α) = (1-α)(α - c) where c = unit cost
- Optimal: α* = (1 + c) / 2

**Key insight:** The optimal commission rate increases with infrastructure cost c. If c = 0.2, α* = 0.6; if c = 0.4, α* = 0.7.

### 3. Implement the Payment Integration
- Developer applications must integrate the platform's payment system
- Platform tracks all transactions (Stripe Connect, Google Pay, etc.)
- Revenue share is automatic at the point of transaction
- Alternative: advertising revenue share for non-transactional applications

### 4. Design the Co-Creation Mechanisms
Value is co-created through four developer mechanisms (Heimburg et al., 2025):
1. **System instructions:** Developers fine-tune AI behavior with precise instructions
2. **Contextual data:** Developers provide domain-specific information
3. **User input curation:** Developers filter/reformulate user requests
4. **Output revision:** Developers check/correct/enrich AI responses

RSI aligns platform incentives to encourage all four mechanisms because platform revenue depends on application success.

### 5. Model the Societal Impact
RSI enables developers without initial capital to participate in the digital economy. In emerging markets with 84% mobile penetration, this could unlock the "latent jobs dividend" in health, agriculture, and services.

## Key Findings (Mondjo, 2026)

### Three Generations of GenAI Business Models
1. **Pay-per-use (like gasoline):** Developer pays per token; transfers all adoption risk to developer
2. **Diversification (freemium/subscriptions, like Spotify):** Free tier with quotas; paid tiers with more
3. **Revenue-sharing (like YouTube):** Platform hosts for free; takes percentage of generated revenue

### RSI Comparative Advantages
| Dimension | Pay-per-token | Freemium | Subscription | Marketplace | RSI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry barrier | High | Medium | High | Low | **None** |
| Developer risk | High | Medium | High | Low | **Low** |
| Platform risk | Low | Low | Low | High | **High** |
| Interest alignment | Low | Low | Medium | High | **Very high** |
| Innovation incentive | Constrained | Medium | Medium | High | **Maximized** |
| Revenue potential | Usage-capped | Usage-capped | Fixed | Success-based | **Unlimited upside** |

### Optimal Commission Analysis
- Stackelberg equilibrium: platform sets α first, developers choose effort and price
- Effort is decreasing in α: higher commission reduces incentive to invest
- Platform trades off: higher α yields more per developer but fewer developers and lower effort
- The optimal rate depends on infrastructure cost, demand elasticity, and developer heterogeneity

### Emerging Market Impact
- 84% mobile penetration in low/middle-income countries (World Bank 2025)
- RSI enables developers without initial capital to build AI applications
- Could unlock the "latent jobs dividend" (World Economic Forum 2025)
- Applications in health, agriculture, legal services, and business services

## A-Tech Value Alignment

| A-Tech Value | RSI Alignment |
|---|---|
| **Open-source AI** | Infrastructure is free; aligns with open-source ethos; no paywall on core functionality |
| **Data privacy** | Developer retains control of their application data; platform only tracks revenue |
| **Financial freedom** | Zero entry barrier; developers without capital can participate; aligns with financial freedom mission |
| **Practical implementation** | Payment system integration is the main complexity; SDKs and APIs make it deployable |
