# PrestaShop Case Study — Methodology and Detailed Dynamics

## Source

Lanteri, A., De Ruosi, S., & Santoro, G. (2026). "Hybrid Monetization in an Open-Source Platform: Freemium, Data, and Value Capture in the PrestaShop Ecosystem." Administrative Sciences, MDPI, vol. 16(6), pages 1-22, May 2026. DOI: gam:jadmsc:v:16:y:2026:i:6:p:255-d1953528.

## Research Design

- **Method**: Abductive, qualitative single case study
- **Data sources**: Semi-structured interviews with senior and middle managers, internal documents, performance dashboards, participant observation in strategic and product meetings
- **Analysis**: Process analysis tracing three dynamics of business model reconfiguration
- **Keywords**: open-source software; hybrid monetization; multi-sided platforms; freemium; business model dynamics; data and artificial intelligence; platform governance; e-commerce ecosystem

## Subject: PrestaShop

PrestaShop is a large open-source e-commerce platform. The core is free and self-hosted. The platform supports a marketplace of extensions (modules and themes) developed by the community and third-party partners. The case study traces how PrestaShop reconfigured its business model to assemble a hybrid monetization architecture on top of the free, self-hosted core.

## The Three Dynamics in Detail

### Dynamic 1 — From Community-Led Freeness to Intentional Monetization

**Starting state:**
- PrestaShop's core was free and self-hosted, sustained by a community of contributors
- A marketplace existed for extensions, generating loosely governed revenue
- Value capture was minimal and opportunistic
- The platform had grown significantly but commercial sustainability was fragile

**The shift:**
- Recognition that community-led freeness alone could not sustain the organization
- Strategic decision to move from opportunistic to intentional monetization
- This was not a single pivot but the beginning of a sequential reconfiguration
- Required cultural shift: from pure open-source community steward to commercial platform sponsor

**Control point adjustment:**
- Marketplace governance tightened: quality standards introduced, curation began
- The sponsor began to actively define what constitutes a premium vs. standard offering

**Partner relationship adjustment:**
- Extension developers informed of new marketplace policies
- Some friction as loosely governed revenue became more structured
- Transparency about the need for sustainable monetization was critical

### Dynamic 2 — Data and AI Capabilities as Monetization Infrastructure

**The problem:**
- Self-hosted deployments meant PrestaShop lacked centralized visibility into actual usage
- The ecosystem was opaque: who uses the platform, how, and where value can be captured were unknowns
- Without this legibility, intentional monetization was guesswork

**The solution:**
- Construction of data and AI capabilities as monetization infrastructure
- Data collection (within appropriate governance constraints) to understand ecosystem composition and activity
- AI capabilities for segmentation, targeting, and personalized offering design
- This made the ecosystem "legible and segmentable" — the foundation for all subsequent monetization

**Key insight:** Data and AI are not features or products — they are the *infrastructure* that enables monetization. Without ecosystem legibility, no revenue mechanism can be effectively targeted.

**Data governance adjustment:**
- Data flows designed to balance commercial intelligence needs with user trust
- The open-source ethos required transparency about data collection
- Privacy considerations became strategic, not just compliance

### Dynamic 3 — Layering Transactional, Infrastructural, and Curated Subscription Revenues

**The layering:**
- Multiple revenue streams built around the open-source core:

  **Transactional revenues:**
  - Marketplace transaction fees (commission on extension sales)
  - Payment processing fees

  **Infrastructural revenues:**
  - Managed hosting services
  - Premium support with SLAs
  - Certification programs

  **Curated subscription revenues:**
  - Premium modules with subscription pricing
  - Enterprise feature bundles
  - Curated service packages

**The key finding — each new revenue mechanism entails adjustments:**
- **Control points**: each stream requires a different control point (marketplace curation, infrastructure SLA, subscription entitlement, payment processing)
- **Partner relationships**: each stream affects different partners differently (extension developers, hosting partners, enterprise customers, training partners)
- **Data governance**: each stream requires different data flows and has different privacy implications

**This is why hybrid monetization cannot be a single pivot:** the adjustments compound. Adding a second revenue stream on top of the first requires re-calibrating the first stream's control points, partner relationships, and data governance in light of the second. Sequential, overlapping moves — not simultaneous launch.

## Key Conceptual Contributions

1. **Hybrid monetization as staged reconfiguration**: Not a single pivot but a sequence of overlapping moves, each requiring adjustments in control points, partner relationships, and data governance.

2. **Data and AI as monetization infrastructure**: The infrastructure that makes an open-source ecosystem legible and segmentable — the prerequisite for intentional monetization, not a feature or product.

3. **Structural constraints of decentralized data**: Self-hosted open-source deployments create a structural data deficit that must be overcome before monetization can be intentional rather than opportunistic.

4. **The freeness-participation-capture triangle**: Open-source platforms must balance three forces — freeness (sustains ecosystem), community participation (drives value creation), and value capture (funds sustainability). Hybrid monetization is the architecture that balances all three.

## Implications for Open-Source Platform Sponsors

1. **Build data capabilities early**: Without ecosystem legibility, monetization is guesswork. Even privacy-first, opt-in telemetry provides more intelligence than no data at all.

2. **Move sequentially**: Each new revenue mechanism requires adjustments that compound. Launch one stream, stabilize it, then add the next.

3. **Design control points per stream**: Different revenue streams require different control points. Do not assume one governance model fits all streams.

4. **Manage partner transitions transparently**: Tightening marketplace governance or adding subscription tiers affects partners. Transparent communication about the need for sustainable monetization is critical.

5. **Reinvest in the core**: The open-source core's health is the foundation of all revenue streams. Community trust is the moat. Value capture without reinvestment erodes the foundation.

## Limitations of the Case Study

- Single case study (PrestaShop) — generalizability to other open-source platforms requires further research
- Abductive qualitative method — rich process detail but not statistically generalizable
- E-commerce platform context — dynamics may differ for developer tools, AI platforms, or other open-source categories
- The study captures a specific reconfiguration period — ongoing evolution may reveal additional dynamics

## Relevance to A-Tech

A-Tech's three products map to different stages of this framework:

- **A-Coder**: An open-source developer platform with marketplace dynamics (plugin marketplace), self-hosted deployment option, and enterprise feature potential. Currently at Stage 2 (intentional monetization forming). The PrestaShop dynamics provide the roadmap for the Stage 2→3 transition.

- **Be Practical**: A learning platform with community participation and curated content. At Stage 1→2 transition. The data-and-AI-as-infrastructure dynamic is directly applicable — building learning outcome measurement capabilities is the prerequisite for intentional monetization.

- **Builder's Club**: A community platform with marketplace potential. At Stage 1→2 transition. The community-led-freeness-to-intentional-monetization dynamic is the immediate challenge.

A-Tech's privacy-first values provide a structural advantage in the data-and-AI-as-infrastructure dynamic: federated learning and on-device analytics can provide ecosystem legibility without the centralization that creates privacy risk and community distrust.
