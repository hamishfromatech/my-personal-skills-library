Source: "Open Source: The Surprising Engine of Profit and Sustainability" (UNICEF Venture Fund, March 11, 2025).

## The Myth-Breaking Portfolio Data

Since 2016, the UNICEF Venture Fund has invested in Open Source solutions. The portfolio results:
- Over 70% of these companies generate revenue.
- More than 40% have reached profitability.
- Collectively raised 12x the seed investment through follow-on funding.
- 20 startups raised more than $1M.
- 4 surpassed $5M.
- 1 secured more than $10M.
- 8 portfolio companies exited through acquisitions: Dymaxion Labs, GeoSpoc, Veative, Leaf, Kimetrica, Giraffe, Atix Labs, Weni.

The era of Open Source as "simply idealistic" is over — it is now a strategic engine of profit and sustainability.

## Somleng Case (Cambodia)

Somleng began with humanitarian communications solutions and expanded into thriving private-sector partnerships with telecommunications companies in Mexico and Canada. Business model: B2B, capitalizing on UI modifications, custom-built integrations, and feature requests as a paid service. Telcos needing functionality beyond the open-source version contract Somleng for paid development. All improvements contribute back to the open-source ecosystem while Somleng is compensated for engineering time.

### David Wilkie (founder) on the de-platforming lever
"One key concern is de-platforming — if a telco builds its entire product on a proprietary service and gets kicked off, their entire business could collapse. Open Source offers them a safety net, giving them access to the source code so they can maintain operations independently if needed."

### On cost and scalability
"Proprietary telecom software often requires expensive per-user or per-number licensing fees. Somleng, as Open Source, allows telcos to scale without significantly increasing software expenses. Somleng fills the gap for cost-effective, Open Source telecom infrastructure, providing a scalable alternative to proprietary CPaaS solutions."

### On flexibility
Open Source platforms are more cost-effective for telcos managing millions of phone numbers; flexibility to white-label and customize solutions to fit branding needs; eliminates costly per-user licensing; long-term operational sustainability in a competitive market.

## Venture Capital Landscape

- Over 50 venture capital firms actively invest in open source projects.
- Specialized players: Open Core Ventures, OSS Capital (focus exclusively on this model).
- Battery Ventures developed the BOSS Index to identify, evaluate, and track promising open-source startups.
- Five years ago, 40+ open-source-based companies generated over $100M in annual revenue.
- Sequoia Capital's Open Source Fellowship and a16z's Open Source AI Grant Program demonstrate major-investor confidence.
- Zerodha (India's largest stockbroker) launched a $1M annual fund to support FOSS/FLOSS projects globally — cross-sector signal.

## The Sustainability Logic

Open source combines adaptability, community-driven innovation, and financial viability. The collaborative method reduces development costs, enhances product quality, and keeps solutions relevant to market demands. With buy-in from VC, governments, and global communities, open source has evolved from a niche concept into a dominant force.

Core proposition: it is possible to deliver both financial returns and social impact. As more innovators and investors recognize its potential, open source is poised to become a cornerstone of sustainable innovation.
