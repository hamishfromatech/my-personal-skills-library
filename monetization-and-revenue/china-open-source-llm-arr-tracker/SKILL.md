# China Open-Source LLM ARR Tracker — Cycle-28 Refresh

*(Full framework established in cycle 16: sector ARR ~$10–15B total with core LLM ~$6–8B; Zhipu/MiniMax/DeepSeek/Kimi trajectories; the "price-war is dead" finding — DeepSeek raised prices 3–12× and usage grew; binding constraint is GPU capacity.)*

## Cycle-20 addendum (2026-09-06): the monetization-mechanism wave diversifies

Three September datapoints extend the tracker's core thesis (open-source monetization lives at the hosted-inference/infrastructure layer, not licensing):

1. **Moonshot's cloud toll play (kimi-cloud-revshare-negotiation skill):** Kimi K3 — 2.8T parameters, ARR ~$100M March → $300M+ mid-June, API revenue +400% YoY (>70% of total) — is negotiating up to **30% of cloud MaaS revenue** with Microsoft, Amazon, and Google, against a historical 20% ceiling and a $20M qualification threshold. If one major cloud signs, open-weight model makers get a hosting-revenue *precedent* — the first structural answer to the usage-vs-revenue gap (Mozilla: 33% usage / 4% revenue). Moonshot's ~$50B HK IPO (165× run-rate) prices the ladder of which only the model rung is built; the toll contracts are unpriced options until signed.
2. **Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max profit-sharing pivot** (TechShots, Sept 4): updating licensing terms to demand revenue share from large commercial users of the upcoming open-source flagship — "emulating a freemium strategy recently adopted by Moonshot" — the second major Chinese lab converging on commercial-tier extraction from open distribution (already tracked via `metered-open-license-revenue-share-2026`; this is the confirmation).
3. **K2 Horizon's open-science contrast** (IFM/MBZUAI): monetizes *reputation and compliance* rather than tolls — full-lifecycle openness as the academic-reference and regulated-market strategy. The sector now spans three monetization mechanisms: hosted inference (DeepSeek/Zhipu), hosting tolls (Kimi negotiation), and open-science positioning (K2). Which mechanism dominates is the sector question for H2 2026.

**Tracker implication:** add a mechanism column (inference-revenue / license-toll / cloud-revshare / open-science) to any lab-level ARR analysis — revenue quality differs as much as quantity.

## Cycle-23 addendum (2026-09-11): retirement routing + vertical agents

1. **DeepSeek's forced model swap** (`deepseek-v41-flash-retirement-routing`): the V4.1-Flash release converts a model retirement into live-traffic migration — from Sept 14, every `deepseek-v4-pro` call serves V4.1-Flash at Flash pricing (cache-hit −86.4%, output −69.7% at peak), no opt-out, "until V4.1 Pro ships." The tracker's price-war-dead thesis extends: DeepSeek is now using PRICE as a MIGRATION lever, not just a demand lever — routing its own flagship-tier traffic onto the cheap architecture to reset cost baselines ahead of the Pro successor. The MIT license makes the swap survivable for customers (self-host exit), which is exactly why the tactic is low-risk for the vendor.
2. **Alibaba's Accio agent as vertical ARR** (Nikkei Asia, Sept 10): the B2B e-commerce agent has **60,000+ paid users in five months and $60M+ annual revenue** — priced ~50% cheaper than general agents from OpenAI/Anthropic for commerce tasks. This is the first large, named vertical-agent ARR line from a Chinese open-weight stack: the mechanism column gains a row — **vertical-agent ARR** (domain-priced agents built on open base models), distinct from generic hosted inference. Watch: whether Accio's vertical pricing generalizes to other Alibaba agent lines and whether the 50%-cheaper claim survives independent benchmarking.
3. **Nex-AGI's 1.6T open-weight MoE** (Nex-N2.5-Max, Apache-2.0, 644 safetensors shards, deepseek_v4 config, 1M context — HF card Sept 7–8): the trillion-parameter open-weights tier now has a third entrant alongside DeepSeek and Moonshot, and the shard-list-first verification pattern (weights verifiable before benchmarks claimed) is the release-discipline signal the tracker should require for every new lab claim. **Pro still has no weights** — the honest inventory is Max + Mini on disk, Pro as a listing.

**Tracker implication:** the mechanism column now reads — inference-revenue / license-toll / cloud-revshare / open-science / **vertical-agent ARR** — and every new lab datapoint should carry both a mechanism tag and a weights-verification tag (shards verifiable vs listing-only).

## Cycle-27 addendum (2026-09-15): the Zhipu margin inversion + the velocity leader

1. **Zhipu's H1 print (36Kr/光锥智能, Sept 2, 2026):** revenue ¥954M (+399.7% YoY, exceeding full-year 2025); **ARR $1.6B (monthly annualization) / >$2B (weekly annualization, matching MiniMax's 52× method)** as of end-August — August alone ~$133M; the open-weight leader by ARR, at ~2.5× MiniMax. The strategic payload is the **margin inversion**: total gross margin fell 50.0% → 26.4% (H1) even as average API pricing rose ~101%, because the revenue structure inverted — API/open-platform revenue ¥825M (+2,735.7%), now **86.5% of revenue**, while high-margin localized deployment collapsed from 73.7% → 13.5% (¥129M, −20.5% YoY). Cloud-API margin turned positive (−0.4% → 24.6%) but price increases (+101% average) did not move it materially — **pricing power is not converting to margin** (R&D at 2.2× revenue; adjusted net loss ¥1.96B). The open-weight thesis sharpens: at scale, hosted open-weight inference is a low-margin, high-velocity business — the revenue ladder runs on capability leadership (SOTA retention), not unit economics.
2. **Velocity leader + strategy divergence:** Zhipu's "model as product" bet (API toll at scale, localized deployment deliberately wound down; enterprise agents +304.4% to ¥55.56M) versus MiniMax's C→B pivot and Moonshot's hosting-toll ladder — the open-weight sector now shows at least four distinct revenue structures from the same open-weights premise.
3. **Tracker implication:** mechanism column unchanged (inference-revenue / license-toll / cloud-revshare / open-science / vertical-agent ARR / consent-corpus barter / sovereign-full-stack) but every lab row now needs a **margin-quality tag** — revenue velocity is not margin quality; a $2B ARR at 26% gross margin is a different business from the same ARR at 60%.

## Cycle-28 addendum (2026-09-15): the OpenSats funding wave + agent-membership identity

1. **OpenSats' $36.6M pass-through wave (announced Sept 7, 2026):** the 501(c)(3) public charity allocated **over $36.6M in Bitcoin to 413 grantees across 40+ countries**, with a 100% pass-through model (operating costs covered separately; Q2 2026: 36 BTC sent for $12.51 in fees). The **Nostr Fund** is one of two primary pools; major donors include Jack Dorsey's #startsmall and the Reynolds Foundation, with monthly disbursements now ~$1M. Adjacent: Dorsey's **$10M to "and Other Stuff"** (May 2026 collective — Nostr, ActivityPub, Cashu; "community of hackers" rejecting VC structures, building ethical guidelines for privacy/interoperability/transparency). This is the sixth funding lane for the tracker's capital-stack: **pass-through public-goods grants** (vs endowments, co-ops, patronage, corporate sponsorship, and the OpenAI-Cursor-style vendor-funded support) — breadth-first (413 grantees in 40+ countries means contributors who would never appear on a corporate shortlist), protocol-native (protocol-specific funds like Nostr Fund), and VC-structure-averse.
2. **The agent-membership identity pattern (Buzz on Nostr, NostrMag analysis Sept 8, 2026):** AI agents hold their **own Nostr keypairs** and appear as full network members — every message, code review, and workflow step is a signed event in the same event log as human teammates (trust follows the signature, not a permission flag). Vendor-internal scale: BuilderBot executes **200,000+ operations/day, merges 1,500+ PRs/week, ~15% of production code changes**. The tracker's identity layer extends beyond wallet-scale KYA (Ant AMP) and on-chain registries (ERC-8004) to **cryptographic peer membership** on open protocols — relevant because every mechanism lane above (inference-revenue, license-toll, cloud-revshare, vertical-agent ARR, consent-corpus barter, sovereign-full-stack) eventually needs to attribute agent traffic, and the open-protocol answer is now shipping in production rather than as a spec.
3. **Tracker implication:** mechanism column unchanged (inference-revenue / license-toll / cloud-revshare / open-science / vertical-agent ARR / consent-corpus barter / sovereign-full-stack) but the **funding-source column** gains its sixth lane (pass-through grants), and the **identity column** gains its first open-protocol lane (agent-as-cryptographic-peer). Every lab row now carries: mechanism tag + margin-quality tag + weights-verification tag + funding-source tag + identity-verification tag.
