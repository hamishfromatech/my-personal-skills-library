# CreatorFi — Creator Capital Markets

## Overview
CreatorFi raised $45M (debt+equity) on Sept 2, 2026 to finance creator-led media businesses against their recurring platform revenue — turning creator income streams (Roblox earnings, Spotify royalties, YouTube AdSense, TikTok Shop sales) into an asset class that lenders can underwrite.

## When to Use
- Modeling creator monetization as an investable, financeable revenue stream rather than an ad-driven payout
- Designing or evaluating financial products for creators (advances, receivables lending, IP-backed loans)
- Analyzing which revenue streams are "underwriteable" for a creator business
- NOT for: influencer sponsorships, ad-network economics, or creator-side "sell courses" playbooks

## Core Process / Workflow

### The financing structure
- **$45M raised** (equity + debt; EV3-led equity; Uncorrelated Ventures reported co-investor) — announced Sept 2, 2026, with capacity for **up to another $100M**; prior $12M credit facility (2025)
- Founders: Jack Cameron + Billy Huang (CEO); "CreatorFi advances capital to creators against the revenue they earn from platforms like Roblox, Spotify, TikTok, and YouTube" + traditional business loans
- **Underwriting thesis**: "cult-like" audiences — unusually loyal, predictable, engaged audiences with strong IP; factors include engagement quality, IP strength, revenue reliability — not raw follower counts
- **The core claim**: creator income is becoming *legible* enough for lenders to treat it as a forecastable, financeable cash flow — the creator economy shifting from sponsorship income to underwritable recurring cash flow

### The 3-layer monetization shift
1. **Revenue legibility**: platform revenue (Roblox, Spotify, YouTube, TikTok Shop) is now measurable, recurring, and documentable — a receivable, not a lottery
2. **IP ownership**: capital explicitly funds IP acquisition/creation, not just operational scaling — licensing, merch, catalog development
3. **Infrastructure demand**: as revenue becomes underwritable, creators need better record-keeping, payment history, campaign performance data — the "creator OS" becomes the bottleneck

### Design workflow
1. **Audit the revenue streams**: for each platform revenue stream (Roblox, Spotify, YouTube, TikTok Shop), document: recurrence, platform dependency, volatility, seasonality, documentation quality
2. **Score underwritability**: predictable + platform-diversified + IP-backed revenue = strong collateral; one-off sponsorships + volatile AdSense = weak
3. **Model the financing cost**: revenue-based advances typically price against future receivables; compare against equity dilution and debt APR
4. **Design the financing product** (if building): receivables purchase / revenue-share advances / IP-secured lending — with clawback and revenue-share caps
5. **Creator playbook**: consolidate revenue data, diversify platforms, build IP that persists beyond platform policy changes, document audience reliability — these are the financing-readiness assets

## A-Tech Alignment
- **Open source:** not OSS itself, but the financing *model* (revenue-based, IP-backed, audience-loyalty-weighted) is transferable to open-source maintainers and open-weights monetization.
- **Privacy:** underwriting requires revenue data sharing — the creator's payment/engagement history becomes an operational asset with privacy implications.
- **Financial freedom:** this is the literal thesis — creator income as a financeable asset class, turning audiences into collateral.
- **Practical:** the underwritability audit is a 5-step checklist any creator can run.

## Honesty Caveats
- Single-deal datapoint ($45M) in a still-early market; "up to another $100M" is capacity, not deployed capital
- "Cult-like audience" is the company's marketing framing, not a defined underwriting metric
- Underwriting details (advance rates, fee structures, default rates) are not publicly disclosed
- Business Insider's exclusive is a company-favorable profile; independent performance data does not yet exist
