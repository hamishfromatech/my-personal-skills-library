# Evidence Base — CreatorFi Creator Capital Markets

## The deal (Sept 2, 2026)

- **$45M raised** in combined equity + debt financing. Business Insider (Lucia Moses, exclusive): "CreatorFi raised a $12 million credit facility in 2025. The new financing is a combination of equity and debt. The equity financing was led by Escape Velocity (EV3)…" (coingape adds Uncorrelated Ventures to the equity syndicate).
- **Capacity**: the structure gives it capacity for **up to another $100M**.
- **Founders**: Jack Cameron and Billy Huang (CEO).
- **What it does**: "CreatorFi advances capital to creators against the revenue they earn from platforms like Roblox, Spotify, TikTok, and YouTube. It also offers traditional business loans." Lends against IP- and royalty-backed cash flows.

## The financing thesis (coingape/BI synthesis)

1. **From sponsorship income to cash flow**: traditional influencer economics are volatile — one big sponsorship month, then silence. CreatorFi's model focuses on *repeatable* revenue: platform income from businesses built on Roblox, Spotify, YouTube, TikTok Shop.
2. **Underwriting**: BI reported CreatorFi looks for creators with unusually loyal, "cult-like" audiences and evaluates engagement and IP strength. A creator with 200K committed fans and predictable monthly revenue may be more financeable than one with 3M followers and erratic income.
3. **What's being financed**: "gaming creators are unusually good examples" — Roblox studios earning platform revenue, Fortnite creators monetizing islands, YouTube gaming channels earning ad revenue, roleplay communities selling services/memberships, game creators licensing IP.
4. **The creator is an operator**: "The creator may still be the face of the business, but the economics can extend far beyond sponsored posts… Once creator revenue can support financing, a successful creator does not necessarily have to sell the company… They can borrow against future income. That is standard behavior in mature industries."
5. **IP layer**: financing announcement says capital can be used to acquire, create and grow IP — different from a one-off sponsorship; a gaming creator might develop a character, game, server, format or brand expandable into licensing, merch, events or new titles.
6. **Infrastructure implication**: if creators are financed on future revenue, systems recording their work/performance/payment history become operationally valuable — reputation, completed work, campaign performance, payment history become underwriting data.

## Creator-side financing-readiness assets

1. **Documented revenue**: platform dashboards exportable, payout history reconciled, taxes separated.
2. **Platform diversification**: multiple recurring streams, no single-platform dependency that a policy change kills.
3. **IP inventory**: characters, formats, catalogs, brands — assets that survive platform churn and can be licensed.
4. **Audience reliability metrics**: retention, repeat purchase, subscription renewal — the loyalty data that makes "cult-like" underwritable.

## Market maturity signal

- "First, creators proved they could attract audiences. Then they proved they could make money. Now the market is starting to ask whether that income can be forecast, financed and used to build larger businesses." (coingape)
- The financing thesis is a *maturation* datapoint — creator revenue becoming an input to capital markets — rather than a new monetization channel itself.

## Caveats

- Company-favorable exclusive sourcing (BI is an exclusive; coingape is a press-release syndication).
- No public data on advance pricing, fee structures, default/recovery rates, or portfolio performance.
- "Up to another $100M" is *capacity* (credit lines/facility headroom), not deployed capital.
- "Cult-like" is a marketing phrase, not an underwriting definition.
- Platform-dependency risk is real: Roblox/Spotify/YouTube/TikTok policy shifts can reprice receivables overnight.
- Not to be confused with the unrelated "Clanker Ecosystem Fund" (Farcaster, $112K total) — different mechanism, different scale.
