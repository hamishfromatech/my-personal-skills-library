# Skill: Friction-Based Pricing Discovery

## Summary
A practical, intuition-driven method for finding the optimal price point by incrementally raising prices until you encounter genuine customer friction. If customers immediately say "sold," you're too cheap. Stop raising when you hear "we have to think about that." This is how multi-billion dollar companies found their pricing sweet spot.

## Core Principle
Most founders default to cost-plus pricing (calculate costs, double it) because asking for more feels awkward. The best founders lead with value and find their sweet spot through real market friction, not spreadsheets.

## The Friction Method
1. **Start with a price** (e.g., $12K/year or $99/month)
2. **If customers immediately say "sold"** → You're too cheap. Raise 20-50%.
3. **If customers hesitate, compare, ask questions** → You're in the zone. This is your sweet spot.
4. **If pricing becomes a blocker to conversation** → You've gone too far. Dial back slightly.
5. **Iterate**: Continue until you consistently hear "we have to think about that" but deals still close

## Why Cost-Plus Fails
- Ignores customer willingness-to-pay
- Leaves massive revenue on the table
- Signals lack of confidence in value proposition
- Creates unhealthy margin pressure when COGS rise (especially in AI where compute costs are real)

## Hybrid Pricing Formula (Early-Stage Bridge)
When outcome-based pricing feels too risky early on:

**Platform fee (2X calculated delivery costs) + Outcome credits**

Example:
- $12K annual platform fee covers infrastructure
- Includes 100 ticket resolutions
- Additional resolutions: $5K per 100 tickets

Benefit: As outcomes scale, price per outcome falls — but total revenue from the customer increases. Natural expansion while maintaining margins.

## A-Tech Alignment
- **Financial freedom**: Maximizes revenue capture; no underpricing
- **Practical implementation**: Simple, actionable framework requiring no complex analysis
- **Open-source AI**: Open-source core + paid outcome credits = sustainable open-source business

## Applications
- **A-Coder (IDE)**:
  - Test pricing per outcome ($0.99 per shipped feature? $5 per resolved bug?)
  - Start at $29/month; if instant conversions, raise to $49, then $79
  - Find friction point where developers say "hmm, let me check my budget"
- **Be Practical (Book/Playbooks)**:
  - Chapter: "How to Price Your AI Product Without a Spreadsheet"
  - Worksheet: Record customer reactions at each price point
  - Case study: How Cursor or GitHub Copilot could have priced differently
- **Open Source AI Builder's Club**:
  - Workshop: "Price Your Open Source Tool in 30 Days Using Friction Testing"
  - Shared template: track customer reactions across price tiers
  - Accountability: members report pricing experiments weekly

## Implementation Steps
1. Define your initial price (gut feeling or competitor benchmark)
2. Pitch to 5-10 real prospects at this price
3. Record their reaction (immediate yes / hesitation / blocker)
4. Adjust and repeat until you find the "think about it" zone
5. Lock in pricing for 3 months, then test raising again
6. Never let pricing complexity proliferate — one model that works at 10 and 1,000 customers

## Key Metrics
- Price elasticity (reaction speed at each price point)
- Conversion rate by price tier
- Deal velocity (time from pitch to close)
- Customer lifetime value (LTV) at each price point
- Net revenue retention (expansion minus churn)
