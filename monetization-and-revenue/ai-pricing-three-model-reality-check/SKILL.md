# AI Pricing Three Model Reality Check

## Overview
Bain analysis of ~200 B2B SaaS companies reveals three distinct AI pricing models often confused under "usage-based": effort (35%), output (55%), and outcome (10%). Most "usage-based" pricing is actually capacity pricing with overages.

## When to Use
- Designing AI product pricing models
- Evaluating effort vs. output vs. outcome pricing options
- Avoiding the common confusion between output and outcome pricing
- Understanding market distribution of AI pricing approaches
- NOT for traditional SaaS pricing without AI variable costs
- NOT when pricing is purely seat-based with no usage component

## Core Process / Workflow

### 1. Distinguish the Three Models

| Model | What You Charge For | Market Share | Quality Risk | Best For |
|-------|---------------------|-------------|--------------|----------|
| Effort-based | Resources AI consumes (tokens, compute, agent hours) | ~35% | Buyer | Broad portfolios, infrastructure |
| Output-based | What AI produces (summaries, records, leads) | ~55% | Buyer | Focused use cases with visible deliverables |
| Outcome-based | Business results (resolutions, revenue, savings) | ~10% | Vendor | Customer support, verifiable outcomes |

### 2. Apply the Output vs. Outcome Test
- **Who carries the quality risk?**
  - Output: Vendor gets paid regardless of whether output creates value
  - Outcome: Payment depends on business results
- **Examples**: Recommended lead = OUTPUT; qualified lead = OUTCOME. Updated record = OUTPUT; completed process = OUTCOME

### 3. Evaluate Capacity vs. Direct Usage
- **Capacity** (~80% of market): Fixed commitment, no refund if unused, optional overages
  - Preserves vendor economics, provides buyer predictability
  - Examples: Atlassian Rovo, ServiceNow Now Assist, Adobe Firefly
- **Direct usage**: Pay only for what's consumed
  - Better for infrastructure/technical buyers
  - More common in infrastructure software

### 4. Check Outcome-Based Feasibility (Three Conditions)
1. **Observable**: Can the outcome be measured?
2. **Attributable**: Can it be uniquely attributed to the AI?
3. **Contractible**: Can buyer and seller agree on definition?
- If all three → outcome-based is viable
- If any fails → fall back to output or effort-based

### 5. Match Model to Product Position
- Infrastructure → Effort-based (common currency across use cases)
- Focused application → Output-based (connect charge to visible work)
- Customer support → Outcome-based (resolved conversation is observable/attributable/contractible)
- Broad portfolio → Effort-based or hybrid
