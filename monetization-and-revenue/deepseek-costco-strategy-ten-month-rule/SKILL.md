# DeepSeek Costco Strategy: The Ten-Month Rule

## Overview

Applies DeepSeek's leaked investor-transcript strategy (Liang Wenfeng, May 2026) — the "ten-month rule" for compute pricing, "restraint is a strategy" philosophy, and the Costco-style capped-margin moat — to design AI business models that win by keeping almost nothing, making them un-undercuttable in commodity intelligence markets.

## When to Use

- Designing pricing for inference-based or API-based AI businesses
- Evaluating unit economics of open-weight AI deployments
- Building competitive moats through deliberate price discipline
- Studying how capped-margin strategies outperform margin-maximizing approaches
- Analyzing the "Costco hot dog" strategy applied to AI compute
- Understanding DeepSeek's roadmap: Language Models → CoT → Agents → Continual Learning → Singularity → Embodiment
- NOT for premium SaaS pricing where differentiation supports fat margins
- NOT for luxury or enterprise-skin-only models where the product is exclusivity

## Core Process / Workflow

### 1. The Ten-Month Rule

DeepSeek's core pricing discipline: buy a batch of GPUs, price the API so the hardware pays for itself in 10 months. Over a server's 3–5 year life, this produces ~6x margin on compute — Liang considers this "a reasonable profit" and deliberately refuses to maximize.

```
months 1–10: hardware paying for itself (break-even)
months 10–60: pure margin (~5x), price stays put anyway
month 60: server retires, cycle repeats
```

**Application:** For any inference-based AI product, calculate the ten-month payback price and treat it as the ceiling, not the floor. This is the opposite of cost-plus pricing — it is cost-plus-discipline.

### 2. "Restraint Is a Strategy"

Liang's mantra: "sometimes you can give up some things in exchange for many other things." What DeepSeek deliberately refuses:

- **No to maximizing profit** — capped margins are the moat
- **No to chasing consumer users** — "watermelons behind, sesame seeds in front"
- **No to becoming a super app** — "Become the next ByteDance? No such thought."
- **No to video generation, 3D, world models, vertical agents** — not on AGI path
- **No to going closed-source, ever** — open weights pull the world onto their stack
- **No to vertically integrating into chips** — focus on model layer
- **No to overtime** — research needs slack and extreme focus

**Application:** Map everything your AI business could do. Delete everything not on the critical path to your core mission. The restraint is not sacrifice — it is the moat.

### 3. The Costco Moat Logic

Costco caps markups at ~14–15% as internal law. The $1.50 hot dog combo has survived every inflation cycle since 1985. The cap looks like generosity and works like a weapon — every competitor needing fat margins must fight on terrain where fat margins are impossible.

```
Costco:     cap markup → undercuttable by nobody → customers stay
DeepSeek:   cap API margin → undercuttable by nobody → ecosystem standardizes
Result:     competitors who need 80% margins cannot compete at 5x margin
```

**Key insight:** The cap itself is the AGI strategy, not just the business model. Cheap tokens and open weights pull the world onto DeepSeek's stack and standardize the ecosystem around them.

### 4. The Commodity Intelligence Thesis

Liang's core bet: intelligence is bound to become a commodity (potentially ~10% of GDP). The strategy follows:

- If intelligence commoditizes → taking less margin beats taking more
- "You don't even need to actually take more; if your vision is to take more, you will be beaten by those whose vision is to take less."
- Open source and low prices make employees feel real accomplishment → organizational cohesion
- Capped margins create an industry with no reason to fight them

**Counter-argument (included for intellectual honesty):** "Taking less beats taking more" is true only in commodity markets with near-zero switching costs. If distribution, integration, habit, and trust are the real moats, the 1% player loses to the 10% player. Apple has taken the fattest margins in consumer hardware for 20 years because an iPhone is a habit, not a fungible good. The thesis holds only if tokens stay fungible.

### 5. The AGI Staircase

Liang's path to AGI as a staircase, each step standing on the previous:

1. **Language Models** — the base (completed)
2. **Chain-of-Thought** — climbed last year (now "a completed stair"; surpassed top humans at olympiad math/competitive programming but stopped short of AGI)
3. **Agents** — climbing this year
4. **Continual Learning** — the next stair, unsolved ("the whole world still hasn't found a good method")
5. **Singularity** — gradual, AI develops AI
6. **Embodiment** — the endpoint

**The irony:** Continual learning (a model that joins your company and learns it like a new hire) is the least fungible product imaginable. If DeepSeek gets what it wants, intelligence stops being a commodity, and the Costco math stops binding.

### 6. Compute Economics

- DeepSeek: ~20,000 H-equivalent GPUs; ~47B active params vs US ~800B active
- "We train models this big not because I think a model this big is enough, but because that is all the resources we have."
- Money in the bank earns 2%/year; a GPU earns its own price back in 10 months → "turning money into Nvidia cards is definitely better than putting it in the bank."
- China-US talent gap: "literally the same pool of people" — fewer chips → fewer experiments → slower researcher development → the talent gap is a compute gap in a trenchcoat.
- "When Silicon Valley says Scaling has hit its limit, that is for Silicon Valley; for Chinese people, we are still very far from that."

### 7. Organizational Design

- No KPIs, no performance reviews
- Vision has never been written down anywhere
- Half of every researcher's time unassigned to explore whatever they want
- Authority runs on consensus
- No overtime (research needs slack; extreme focus means very little to do)
- "Our biggest core interest is maintaining the stability of the team; you could even say it is the only core interest. As long as I can keep the team stable, I will surely achieve AGI."
- Half the core researchers are currently labeling data — "high-end data work is the research now"

**Funding round conditions (reportedly):** no poaching researchers, no funding spinoffs.

### 8. A-Tech Application Framework

For A-Tech's open-source AI products, apply the Costco strategy selectively:

| Principle | A-Tech Application |
|---|---|
| Ten-month rule | Price inference so infrastructure pays for itself in 10 months; treat margin above that as surplus, not target |
| Restraint | Decline features not on the open-source-AI-for-developers critical path |
| Open weights | Pull developers onto A-Tech's stack; open weights = zero-friction adoption |
| Capped margins as moat | Competitors needing 80% margins cannot match 5x margins |
| Team stability | The only unrecoverable loss is people; invest in retention over optimization |
| Continual learning | The stair that, if climbed, makes intelligence non-fungible — and breaks the Costco math |
| "Watermelons behind" | Don't fight the chatbot war; build the AGI-path product |
