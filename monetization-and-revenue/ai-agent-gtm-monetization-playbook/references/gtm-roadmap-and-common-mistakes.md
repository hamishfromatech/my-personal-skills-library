# GTM Roadmap and Common Mistakes

## The Zero-to-$5K-Month Roadmap

### Week 1–2: Pick Your Niche and Validate

**The cardinal rule:** Don't build before you have a buyer. "Build it and they will come" kills more AI agent businesses than anything else.

**Niche selection criteria:**
- One industry, one use case (not "AI agents for everyone")
- Painful, obvious problem that AI solves immediately
- Clear willingness to pay
- Examples: "after-hours lead capture for dental practices," "client intake automation for immigration lawyers," "product recommendation agents for Shopify stores"

**Validation process:**
Talk to 10–15 potential buyers. Not surveys — actual conversations. Ask:
1. What's your biggest headache with [the problem your agent solves]?
2. How are you handling this today?
3. What are you spending on the current solution?
4. If I could solve this for $X/month, would that be interesting?

If at least 4–5 people say yes to the last question, you have a viable niche. If fewer, the problem isn't painful enough or the price point is wrong — iterate before building.

### Week 2–3: Build Your MVP Agent

Use a no-code platform. You don't need to code. You need to deeply understand the problem.

**MVP requirements:**
- Handle the core use case well (not perfectly — well)
- Connect to 1–2 essential integrations (CRM, calendar, email)
- Solid knowledge base covering most common scenarios
- Professional appearance (custom branding, clean UI)

**The 2-week rule:** Don't spend more than 2 weeks building. If still tweaking after 2 weeks, you're procrastinating, not perfecting. Ship it and iterate based on real usage.

### Week 3–4: Land Your First 3 Clients

Go back to validation conversation participants. Offer a founding client deal:
- 50% off the first 3 months
- In exchange for: feedback and a testimonial

**This isn't discounting — it's smart GTM strategy.** You get:
- Paying clients immediately (revenue is revenue, even at 50% off)
- Real usage data to improve your agent
- Social proof for future sales

At 3 clients paying $500/month (or $250/month founding rate), you're at $750–$1,500/month.

### Month 2: Optimize and Raise Prices

With real client data, you can now:
- See which agent features get used and which don't
- Identify gaps in your knowledge base
- Collect outcome metrics (leads captured, tickets resolved, hours saved)
- Build case studies from your founding clients

**Raise prices for new clients.** Your founding clients locked in their rate, but every new client pays full price. With proof of results, you can confidently charge $750–$1,500/month.

### Month 3: Scale to $5K

You need:
- 5–7 clients at $750–$1,000/month, OR
- 10+ clients at $500/month

**Focus on three channels:**

1. **Referrals:** Ask happy clients to introduce colleagues. In local business especially, referrals are gold. A satisfied dentist introduces you to three other practice owners.

2. **Content:** Share results on LinkedIn, in industry forums, in local business groups. "I built an AI agent that booked $12K in new appointments for a med spa" gets attention. Specific results > generic pitches.

3. **Outbound:** Reach out directly to businesses in your niche. Personalized messages that reference their specific situation convert 3–5x better than generic pitches. "I noticed your dental practice doesn't have after-hours lead capture — I built an agent that handles this and booked 40 extra appointments for a practice like yours last month."

$5K/month is 3–7 clients depending on pricing. That's not a massive sales operation — it's a handful of conversations with the right people.

## Scaling Beyond $5K

Once consistently at $5K/month, you have four scaling options:

### Option 1: Go Deeper in Your Niche

Become the AI agent provider for dental practices in your region. Then expand nationally. Vertical dominance beats horizontal sprawl every time. You become the known expert, referrals compound, and your agents get better with every client because you understand the domain deeply.

### Option 2: Productize and White-Label

Take your best-performing agent, templatize it, and sell it to other agencies or consultants who serve the same niche. Now you're making money while they do the selling. Your build cost is sunk; every new license is nearly pure margin.

### Option 3: Add Marketplace Channel

List your agents on relevant platforms and marketplaces to add a passive revenue stream on top of your direct sales. Revenue splits are 70–85% to you, with zero customer acquisition cost. The downside: you don't own the customer relationship. Use as a supplementary channel, not your only one.

### Option 4: Build a Team

Hire a part-time VA to handle onboarding and support. Hire a salesperson on commission. Your $5K/month becomes $20K/month when you're not the bottleneck for every task. The key: document your processes before hiring so the handoff is clean.

## The Five Revenue-Killing Mistakes (Expanded)

### Mistake 1: Pricing on Cost Instead of Value

**What happens:** You calculate that your agent costs $50/month in compute, so you charge $99/month and think 50% margin is great. Meanwhile, the agent saves the client $3,000/month in labor costs. You left $2,900 of value on the table.

**The fix:** Always price based on the outcome the agent delivers, not what it costs you to run. If your agent saves a business $3,000/month, charging $500–$750/month is a no-brainer for them and dramatically better for you.

**Value-based pricing formula:**
- Calculate the measurable value: hours saved × hourly cost, or revenue generated, or costs avoided
- Price at 10–25% of the value created
- The client gets 75–90% of the value; you capture 10–25% — a deal they can't refuse

**Example:** Speed-to-lead agent for a dental practice. Each new patient is worth ~$2,000 lifetime value. If the agent books 5 extra appointments/month, that's $10,000 in value. Charging $750/month captures 7.5% of value — the practice owner would be foolish not to accept.

### Mistake 2: Building Before You Have a Buyer

**What happens:** You spend two months building the perfect agent, launch it, and get crickets. Nobody wants it because you built what seemed interesting to you, not what the market was willing to pay for.

**The fix:** Flip the order. Talk to potential buyers first. Understand their problems. Validate willingness to pay. Then build the minimum viable agent that solves their problem. Your first version doesn't need to be perfect — it needs to be useful enough that someone will pay for it.

**The validation-first protocol:**
1. Identify 10–15 potential buyers in your target niche
2. Conduct problem-discovery conversations (not sales pitches)
3. Confirm the problem is painful enough to pay for
4. Confirm the price point is acceptable
5. Only then build the MVP

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Retention

**What happens:** Acquiring a new client costs 5–7x more than keeping an existing one. But most agent builders spend all energy on new sales and zero on making current clients stickier. Three months in, clients churn because they don't see ongoing value.

**The fix:** Build retention into the product, not just the sales pitch:
- Monthly performance reports showing exactly what the agent accomplished
- Usage dashboards so clients see the value in real-time
- Regular knowledge base updates (stale KB = the #1 churn cause for subscription agents)
- Quarterly optimization reviews
- Proactive check-ins before the client thinks about churning

**The agents with the best retention aren't necessarily the most sophisticated — they're the ones that visibly deliver value every month.**

### Mistake 4: Feature Creep Death Spiral

**What happens:** Client asks for one more integration. Then another. Then they want the agent to handle a completely different workflow. Before you know it, you're maintaining a Frankenstein agent that does 20 things poorly instead of 1 thing brilliantly.

**The fix:** Scope discipline is everything.
- Each agent should do one thing extremely well
- If a client needs multiple capabilities, build multiple agents (modular agents are easier to maintain and resell)
- Maximum 4 integrations per agent
- For complex workflows, use a primary orchestrator agent that routes to specialized sub-agents

**The scope discipline script:** "That's a great idea, and it's outside the scope of this agent. Let me build that as a separate agent that works alongside this one. The current agent stays focused and reliable, and you get the new capability without breaking what works."

### Mistake 5: No Governance Story

**What happens:** Deloitte found only 1 in 5 companies has mature governance for AI agents. Enterprise buyers increasingly require it. If you can't answer basic questions about data privacy, model transparency, error handling, and compliance, you lose deals to competitors who can.

**The fix:** Build your governance story early, before you need it:
- Document how your agents handle sensitive data (data retention, encryption, access controls)
- Explain your model selection criteria (why you chose specific models, what alternatives you considered)
- Show error-handling workflows (what happens when the agent fails, how errors are caught and reported)
- Leverage platform compliance: if building on a SOC2/GDPR/CCPA-compliant platform, use that in sales conversations
- Prepare a one-page governance summary for enterprise procurement teams

**The governance moat:** Because only 20% of companies have mature agent governance, building this story early is a structural competitive advantage. It's a moat that's still wide open.
