# Narrative Transportation Research Synthesis

## Core Papers

### Green & Brock (2000) — "The Role of Transportation in the Persuasiveness of Public Narratives"
- **Journal**: Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
- **Key Finding**: Transportation reduces counter-arguing and increases acceptance of story-consistent beliefs.
- **Mechanism**: When readers are immersed in a narrative, they generate fewer counter-arguments and show greater attitude change in the direction of the narrative.
- **Relevance to A-Tech**: Developer audiences who would normally dismiss marketing claims may accept them when embedded in an immersive story.

### Green (2004) — "Transportation Into Narrative Worlds"
- **Journal**: Media Psychology
- **Key Finding**: Transportation involves three components: attention, imagery, and emotional engagement. All three are required for persuasion.
- **Mechanism**: Vivid imagery and emotional connection create a "loss of access to real-world facts" during immersion.
- **Relevance to A-Tech**: Technical stories with vivid scenes (debugging at 3 a.m., the relief of a successful local deployment) create stronger transportation than feature lists.

### Van Laer et al. (2014) — "The Extended Transportation-Imagery Model"
- **Journal**: Journal of Consumer Research
- **Key Finding**: Narrative transportation influences both beliefs and behavioral intentions. The effect is stronger when the audience has low prior knowledge.
- **Mechanism**: Transportation increases empathy with characters, which mediates attitude change.
- **Relevance to A-Tech**: Developers new to local AI are the ideal transportation audience; experienced developers need higher technical specificity to achieve immersion.

### Green, Brock & Kaufman (2004) — "Understanding Media Enjoyment"
- **Key Finding**: Transportation is associated with greater enjoyment and memory of narrative content.
- **Relevance to A-Tech**: Playbooks and community content that transport readers will be remembered longer and shared more.

### Systematic Literature Review (2024)
- **Source**: ResearchGate / Wiley Online Library (doi: 10.1002/mar.22011)
- **Key Finding**: Narrative transportation is validated across 100+ studies, with consistent medium-to-large effects on attitude and behavior change.
- **Moderators**: Effect size increases when (a) the story is vivid, (b) the audience is engaged, and (c) the protagonist is similar to the reader.
- **Relevance to A-Tech**: Developer protagonists in A-Tech stories create maximum identification.

## Practical Implications

1. **Character Similarity**: Use protagonists who match the audience's role (freelance dev, startup founder, enterprise engineer).
2. **Vivid Imagery**: Replace abstract claims with specific scenes. "Local AI is fast" → "The model returned the answer before the autocomplete UI could render."
3. **Emotional Range**: Include frustration, relief, surprise, and triumph. Purely technical narratives do not transport.
4. **Attention Control**: Transportation requires sustained focus. One-page landing pages work better than scattered feature lists.
5. **Behavioral Follow-Through**: Include a clear post-transportation CTA that feels like the natural next chapter.

## Developer-Specific Considerations

- Developers are high-reactance audiences. Transportation works best when it feels like information sharing, not persuasion.
- Technical specificity increases credibility without breaking immersion.
- Open-source narratives ("we built this because we needed it") transport better than corporate narratives ("we built this for you").
