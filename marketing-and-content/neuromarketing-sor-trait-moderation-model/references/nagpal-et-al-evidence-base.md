## Study Design

- **Method:** Quantitative, cross-sectional survey
- **Sample:** 609 digitally active consumers from Delhi-NCR region, India (629 distributed, 20 excluded, 97% effective response rate)
- **Analysis:** Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0
- **Pilot:** n=50, all Cronbach's alpha > 0.70
- **Common method bias:** Harman's single-factor test + full collinearity VIF < 3.3 — no significant CMB threat

## Sample Demographics

- Gender: Male 55%, Female 45%
- Marital status: Married 75.4%, Unmarried 18.4%
- Education: Postgraduation 37.6%, Graduation 22.7%
- Occupation: Business 26.9%, Service 26.9%, Students 18.9%

## Theoretical Contributions

1. **Extends dual-process theory in neuromarketing:** Both System 1 (emotional, sensory, neuro-pricing) and System 2 (cognitive processing) significantly enhance neuromarketing efficacy. Impulsive behaviour is not purely affective — it emerges from interaction between intuitive activation and cognitively reinforced persuasion.

2. **Validates neuromarketing efficacy as the organismic mechanism in S-O-R:** The study empirically confirms efficacy as the mediating transmission mechanism linking stimuli to impulsive responses (β = 0.562).

3. **Establishes dispositional boundary conditions:** Consumer traits function as both direct predictors (β = 0.416) and moderators (β = 0.075) — the first integrated test of trait contingency in neuromarketing.

4. **Challenges the Theory of Planned Behaviour:** The strong efficacy→impulsivity effect suggests that in digitally intensive environments, automatic functions may operate alongside or supersede deliberate intentions.

5. **Reveals desensitisation to scarcity/endorsement:** In digitally saturated environments, repetitive exposure attenuates the persuasive impact of scarcity cues and influencer endorsements.

## Managerial Implications (from the study)

1. Prioritise affectively resonant storytelling aligned with aspirational/identity themes
2. Complement emotion with structured, credibility-enhancing information (cognitive processing)
3. Refine digital design elements — colour, hierarchy, responsiveness, auditory cues
4. Implement psychologically informed but transparent pricing (anchor comparisons, charm pricing)
5. Adopt trait-informed segmentation with AI-driven analytics (emotional susceptibility, FOMO, social sensitivity)
6. Reduce reliance on excessive time-bound promotions or influencer partnerships — diminishing returns
7. Balance persuasive effectiveness with transparency and long-term trust-building

## Limitations (acknowledged by authors)

1. Delhi-NCR lifestyle product segment — generalisation beyond similar urban/digital contexts should be cautious
2. Cross-sectional design — no causal inference; longitudinal/experimental needed
3. Self-reported quantitative data — perceptual bias possible (perception-based, not neurophysiologic)
4. Consumer traits focused on foundational tendencies — future research could add emotional intelligence, cognitive reflection, self-regulation
5. Cross-cultural replication needed
6. Future: investigate AI-driven neuromarketing and real-time personalisation systems
