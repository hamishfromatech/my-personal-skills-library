# Dynamic AI Personalization Nexus — Evidence Base

## Source 1: The DAPN Framework
**Title:** "The Dynamic AI Personalization Nexus (DAPN): Redefining Marketing Theory and Consumer Behaviour in the Age of Algorithmic Personalization"
**Journal:** International Journal of Scientific Research in Engineering and Management, 2026
**DOI:** 10.55041/ijsrem58789
**Authors:** Cynthia.L Sherin, .S Ruchitra, .T.S Kaavya, .K Avinash Sundar (Sonae, Portugal)

### Core Concept
The DAPN framework explains how firms and consumers interact in a deeply individualized, data-driven environment that goes beyond traditional segmentation. The core thesis: personalization relevance drives value creation, but a key tension exists — stronger personalization can boost firm performance while reducing consumer trust if it feels intrusive. This is termed the **personalization–autonomy paradox**.

### Eight Conceptual Propositions
1. Personalization relevance drives value creation
2. Excessive personalization reduces perceived autonomy
3. Algorithmic transparency moderates the personalization–trust relationship
4. The paradox is stronger for sensitive domains
5. Consumer trust is a sequential mediator (personalization → trust → engagement → decision)
6. The paradox is moderated by consumer AI literacy
7. Ethical AI marketing requires user control over personalization depth
8. Sustainable personalization balances efficiency with autonomy

### Theoretical Foundations
- Marketing theory (value creation, segmentation)
- Behavioural science (autonomy, trust, reactance)
- AI ethics (transparency, fairness, accountability)
- The framework aims to provide practical guidance for "Ethical AI Marketing Frameworks that prioritize fairness, transparency and consumer choice"

### Contribution
Adds a "human-centred perspective to algorithmic personalization" and offers practical guidance for developing ethical AI marketing frameworks. The DAPN provides a foundation for future research and strategic use within evolving intelligent marketing systems.

## Source 2: Neuroadaptive Retailing Study (UTAUT + Privacy Calculus)
**Title:** "Neuroadaptive retailing: How quantum-enhanced biometric systems drive consumer engagement through cognitive and emotional alignment"
**Journal:** Acta Psychologica, 2026
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107177
**Authors:** Sun, Faraz, Sarfraz, Gul

### Study Design
- 500 Chinese e-commerce consumers
- PLS-SEM analysis
- UTAUT framework extended to neuroadaptive digital retail environments
- Introduces constructs: quantum-enhanced processing efficiency, consumer digital twin fidelity
- Moderators: privacy calculus acceptance, technology readiness

### Key Findings
- Neuroadaptive system responsiveness enhances consumer engagement **primarily through emotional congruence alignment** (not cognitive load optimization alone)
- Quantum-enhanced processing efficiency operates through both cognitive and emotional pathways
- **Privacy calculus acceptance and technology readiness significantly strengthen these relationships** under conditions of higher consumer acceptance
- The model explains substantial variance in neuroadaptive consumer engagement and personalization effectiveness

### Relevance to DAPN
This study provides empirical validation for the privacy-calculus boundary condition in the DAPN framework: personalization effectiveness is moderated by privacy calculus acceptance. When acceptance is high, personalization enhances engagement; when low, it triggers resistance. The finding that emotional congruence (not just cognitive load) drives engagement supports the SOR-extension in the DAPN framework: the Organism response includes both cognitive and emotional processing.

## Source 3: Emotional Response Mediation Study
**Title:** "Emotional Responses to AI-Powered Personalised Advertising: The Role of Perceived Empathy and Social Cognition in Consumer Decision-Making"
**Journal:** Journal of Intelligence, 2026
**DOI:** 10.3390/jintelligence14060098
**Authors:** Tatu, Chivu (Popa), Orzan, Moise, Boboc (Bucharest University of Economic Studies)

### Study Design
- 234 adult respondents in Romania (18–65 years)
- PLS-SEM
- Constructs: AI-powered ad personalization, trust in AI, perceived AI empathy, emotional arousal, cognitive elaboration, social cognition, consumer engagement, purchase intention

### Key Findings
- Perceived empathy toward AI-generated advertising positively influences emotional arousal and cognitive elaboration
- Emotional arousal and cognitive elaboration significantly predict consumer engagement and purchase intention
- **Trust in AI emerged as a critical sequential mediator**
- Social cognition moderated the personalisation-to-trust pathway
- The study validates a marketing model capturing "socio-emotional dynamics underlying consumer responses to AI advertising"

### Relevance to DAPN
Confirms the sequential mediation (P5): personalization → trust → engagement → decision. Trust in AI is the critical mediator — without it, personalization does not convert into engagement. Perceived empathy (a form of relevance) drives both emotional and cognitive responses. Social cognition moderates the personalization-to-trust link, suggesting that users who anthropomorphize the AI (perceive it as a social agent) are more sensitive to the personalization–trust relationship.

## Source 4: AI + Neuromarketing + Trust (Pakistan PLS-SEM Study)
**Title:** "AI and Neuromarketing: A New Frontier in Understanding Consumer"
**Journal:** Research Journal of Psychology, 2025
**DOI:** 10.59075/rjs.v3i1.257
**Authors:** Khoso, Cervantes Monter, Shahzad, Alam (University of Missouri–Kansas City; Benemérita Universidad Autónoma de Puebla; University of Karachi; IBA Karachi)

### Study Design
- PLS-SEM (SmartPLS 4)
- Cross-sectional survey
- Grounded in Classical Conditioning Theory (Pavlov)
- Constructs: AI-powered personalization, neuromarketing stimuli, emotional responses, consumer trust, consumer decision-making

### Key Findings
- Neuromarketing stimuli showed strongest effect on emotional responses (β = 0.49, p < 0.001)
- AI personalization had weaker effect on emotional responses (β = 0.24, p < 0.001)
- Emotional responses predicted decision-making (β = 0.23, p = 0.02)
- **Trust both directly influenced decisions (β = 0.56, p < 0.001) and positively moderated the emotion-to-decision link (β = 0.06, p = 0.03)**
- Explained variance: R² = 0.44 (emotional responses), R² = 0.50 (decision-making)

### Relevance to DAPN
Confirms that trust is both a direct predictor and a moderator — consistent with the DAPN proposition that trust is a sequential mediator and a boundary condition. The finding that neuromarketing stimuli (sensory/emotional) have a stronger effect than AI personalization on emotional responses suggests that personalization alone is insufficient; the emotional/sensory design of the stimulus matters as much or more. The moderation effect (β = 0.06) is small but significant, confirming that trust amplifies the emotion→decision pathway.

### Boundary Conditions
- Study conducted in Pakistan (emerging digital market)
- Cross-sectional design limits causal inference
- PLS-SEM model fit below ideal thresholds (SRMR = 0.11–0.13; NFI = 0.59–0.62) — typical for exploratory prediction-oriented models
- Authors note: "in some low-involvement or impulse contexts, emotions can bypass trust"

## Source 5: AI + Neuromarketing Synergy Review
**Title:** "The synergy of neuromarketing and artificial intelligence: A comprehensive literature review in the last decade"
**Journal:** Future Business Journal, 11, 170 (2025)
**DOI:** 10.1186/s43093-025-00591-x
**Authors:** Alsharif, Wang, Isa, Salleh, Dawas (Universiti Sains Malaysia; Zhejiang Technical Institute of Economics; Universiti Teknologi Malaysia; Al Iraqia University; Sejong University)

### Study Design
- PRISMA-guided systematic literature review
- Scopus database, 2013–November 2023
- 1,577 studies refined to 642 relevant articles
- Focus: emotion, attention, memory models; AI models (BCI, DL, ML, DNNs); ethical and bias considerations

### Key Ethical Findings (relevant to DAPN)
- Privacy of consumer neuro-data is a primary ethical concern
- AI can analyze brain activity and biometric responses revealing subconscious preferences without explicit consumer knowledge
- Without strict ethical guidelines, AI-driven neuromarketing could exploit consumer vulnerabilities and undermine free will
- Informed consent is critical: many consumers do not fully understand how neuro-data is collected and used
- Explainable AI (XAI) is recommended to address interpretability concerns
- Cultural and contextual differences significantly influence personalization acceptance

### The NAIMM Framework (from the companion chapter)
Maestre & Bigné (2026, Springer) propose the **Neuro-AI Marketing Mix (NAIMM)** framework, which merges neuromarketing insights with AI-driven strategies across the marketing mix: product, price, marketing channels, and integrated marketing communications. This is the operationalization layer above the DAPN framework.

### Relevance to DAPN
Confirms the ethical imperative for transparency (P3) and user control (P7). The review's emphasis on XAI and informed consent aligns with the DAPN's Level 2 (mechanism disclosure) and Level 3 (control disclosure) transparency layers. The cultural-variation finding supports the privacy-calculus boundary condition: acceptance varies by cultural context.

## Source 6: Customer Digital Twins + Neuromarketing
**Title:** "An analytics-driven method for building ethical customer digital twins using neuromarketing and social media data"
**Journal:** Decision Analytics Journal, 2026
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.dajour.2026.100689
**Authors:** Sefa, Rezaei, Valilai (University of Bremen; Constructor University)

### Key Findings
- Proposes ethical customer digital twins (CDTs) integrating EEG + social media sentiment
- Highlights significant challenges in direct integration of brain-signal and text-sentiment data
- Simple fusion strategies exhibit limited predictive power under real-world, asynchronous conditions
- The study identifies "both technical and ethical challenges associated with multimodal consumer modelling"

### Relevance to DAPN
Customer digital twins represent the extreme of personalization depth — a full simulation of the individual consumer. The finding that simple fusion has limited predictive power and raises ethical challenges confirms the DAPN's caution: maximal personalization depth (a full digital twin) without maximal transparency and consent will trigger the personalization–autonomy paradox at its most severe.

## Theoretical Foundations

### Privacy Calculus Model
Dinev & Hart (2006), Information Systems Research: users weigh perceived benefits against perceived privacy risks. Extended by Bol et al. (2018), JCMC: personalization is evaluated through the privacy calculus across health, news, and commerce contexts. The DAPN framework inherits this as the boundary condition for personalization acceptance.

### SOR (Stimulus-Organism-Response) Framework
Jacoby (2002), Journal of Consumer Psychology: the S→O→R model, extended to AI-mediated contexts. The Stimulus is the AI-personalized content; the Organism processes it cognitively and emotionally; the Response is engagement or resistance. The DAPN predicts the Organism's processing depends on perceived relevance vs perceived intrusiveness, moderated by transparency.

### Classical Conditioning Theory
Pavlov (1927), applied by Khoso et al. (2025): repeated exposure to AI-tailored and emotive stimuli conditions affective responses that guide behavior. The DAPN extends this: conditioning works when trust is present; without trust, the conditioned response is resistance, not engagement.

### Reactance Theory
Brehm (1966): when perceived autonomy is threatened, individuals experience psychological reactance and may act against the intended direction. The personalization–autonomy paradox is a reactance phenomenon: excessive personalization without control triggers reactance.
