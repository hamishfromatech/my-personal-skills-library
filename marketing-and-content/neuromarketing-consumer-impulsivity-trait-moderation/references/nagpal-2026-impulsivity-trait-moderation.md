# Nagpal et al. (2026) — Neuromarketing, Consumer Impulsivity & Trait Moderation

## Full Citation
Nagpal, Bala, Singh, Yadav, & Paliwal (2026). Neuromarketing-driven consumer impulsivity moderated by consumer traits: A PLS-SEM approach. *Frontiers in Psychology*, March 2026.

## Study Design

### Methodology
- **Analytical approach**: Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
- **Sample**: 609 digitally active consumers
- **Geography**: Delhi-NCR, India
- **Respondent profile**: Digitally active consumers (engaged with e-commerce, social media commerce, digital advertising)

### Theoretical Framework
The study integrates three theoretical lenses:

1. **S-O-R (Stimulus-Organism-Response) Framework**
   - *Stimuli*: Six neuromarketing determinants (emotional appeals, scarcity cues, sensory triggers, neuro-pricing, endorsement influence, cognitive processing)
   - *Organism*: Perceived neuromarketing efficacy (psychological effectiveness perceived by consumer)
   - *Response*: Consumer impulsivity

2. **Dual-Process Theory**
   - *System 1 (affective/automatic)*: Rapid, emotion-driven processing — activated by emotional appeals, sensory triggers, neuro-pricing heuristics
   - *System 2 (cognitive/deliberative)*: Slow, analytical processing — activated by structured informational cues, credibility signals, transparent comparisons
   - Key finding: Impulsive behavior emerges from BOTH pathways, not purely affective

3. **Consumer Trait Moderation**
   - Dispositional traits (emotional susceptibility, FOMO, social influence sensitivity) moderate the relationship between neuromarketing efficacy and consumer impulsivity
   - Neuromarketing effectiveness is both mechanism-driven AND trait-contingent — NOT universally uniform

## Detailed Findings by Determinant

### 1. Emotional Appeals (β=0.469, p<0.001) — STRONGEST PREDICTOR
- Emotionally salient stimuli enhance perceived neuromarketing efficacy
- Facilitates rapid System 1 (affective) processing
- Affective resonance is the dominant pathway to perceived efficacy
- Implication: Prioritize affectively resonant storytelling — emotional narrative drives the largest share of perceived neuromarketing effectiveness

### 2. Cognitive Processing (β=0.378, p<0.001) — SECOND STRONGEST
- Structured informational cues reinforce credibility through System 2 processing
- Impulsive behavior emerges from BOTH affective activation AND cognitively reinforced persuasion
- Cognition is not the enemy of impulse — it is a co-driver when structured as credibility-enhancing information
- Implication: Complement emotion with structured credibility-enhancing information — do not assume rational information suppresses impulse

### 3. Sensory Triggers (β=0.173, p<0.001) — SIGNIFICANT, MODERATE
- Multisensory engagement operates through heuristic evaluation
- Highest perceived influence (M=4.09) but only moderate predictive power
- Attractive packaging, pleasant music, ambient scents, visually appealing displays
- Implication: Refine digital design elements for multisensory engagement — sensory is supportive, not primary

### 4. Neuro-Pricing Strategies (β=0.134, p<0.001) — SIGNIFICANT, MODERATE
- Charm pricing, anchoring, reference price framing activate heuristic-based value perception
- Operates through System 1 heuristics (perceived bargain)
- Implication: Use psychologically informed pricing — effective but lower-impact than emotion or cognition

### 5. Scarcity/Urgency Cues (β=0.043, p=0.095) — NOT SIGNIFICANT
- Repetitive exposure to time-bound promotions may attenuate persuasive impact
- High awareness (M=3.63) but no significant behavioral translation
- Reduced perceived authenticity from overuse
- Implication: If using scarcity tactics, prioritize authenticity and contextual relevance over frequency — diminishing returns likely

### 6. Endorsement Influence (β=0.045, p=0.127) — NOT SIGNIFICANT
- Excessive reliance on influencer partnerships may yield diminishing returns
- Possible influencer fatigue among digitally active consumers
- Implication: If using endorsements, prioritize authenticity and contextual relevance — do not over-rely on influencer partnerships

## Managerial Implications

### Strategy Prioritization (by effect size)

1. **Prioritize affectively resonant storytelling** (emotional appeals β=0.469)
   - Emotional narrative is the single strongest driver of perceived neuromarketing efficacy
   - Invest in emotion-led creative, not feature-led messaging

2. **Complement emotion with structured credibility-enhancing information** (cognitive processing β=0.378)
   - Structured information does not suppress impulse — it co-drives it through System 2 credibility reinforcement
   - Pair emotional narrative with transparent, well-organized supporting information

3. **Refine digital design for multisensory engagement** (sensory triggers β=0.173)
   - Supportive layer: packaging, music, scent, visual displays
   - Not a primary driver, but enhances heuristic evaluation

4. **Use psychologically informed pricing** (neuro-pricing β=0.134)
   - Charm pricing, anchoring, reference price framing
   - Effective but lower-impact than emotion or cognition

5. **Adopt trait-informed segmentation** (moderation effect β=0.075, p<0.001)
   - Segment audiences by dispositional susceptibility (emotional susceptibility, FOMO, social influence sensitivity)
   - Higher-susceptibility segments respond more strongly to neuromarketing — target accordingly
   - Novelty-seekers → product launch alerts
   - Emotionally susceptible → narrative-driven campaigns
   - Socially influenced → peer reviews and social proof

6. **Reassess scarcity and endorsement tactics**
   - Both NOT significant in this study
   - If used: prioritize authenticity and contextual relevance over frequency
   - Repetitive exposure attenuates persuasive impact

### Ethical Guardrails
- Persuasive power requires ethical responsibility
- Overuse of emotionally manipulative tactics → consumer fatigue, brand aversion
- Balance persuasive effectiveness with transparency and long-term trust
- Sustainable competitive advantage depends on balancing effectiveness with trust-building
- Trait-based targeting raises ethical questions about exploiting vulnerable susceptibilities — proceed with transparency

## Theoretical Contributions

1. **Integration of S-O-R with dual-process theory**: Demonstrates that both System 1 (affective) and System 2 (cognitive) pathways drive perceived neuromarketing efficacy — challenging the assumption that neuromarketing operates purely through affective/automatic channels.

2. **Consumer trait moderation**: Establishes that neuromarketing effectiveness is trait-contingent, not universal. Dispositional susceptibility amplifies the efficacy→impulsivity relationship (β=0.075, p<0.001).

3. **Scarcity and endorsement re-evaluation**: Provides empirical evidence that two commonly assumed neuromarketing levers (scarcity cues, endorsement influence) do NOT significantly predict perceived efficacy among digitally active consumers — suggesting overuse has attenuated their persuasive impact.

4. **High explanatory power**: R²=0.708 for consumer impulsivity demonstrates that the integrated model (determinants + traits + moderation) explains a substantial majority of variance in impulsive purchasing behavior.

## Limitations & Considerations

- **Geographic scope**: Sample from Delhi-NCR, India — generalizability to other markets and cultures should be validated
- **Cross-sectional design**: Causal inferences are model-based; longitudinal validation would strengthen causal claims
- **Digitally active consumers**: Findings specific to digital commerce context — may not generalize to offline or hybrid purchase journeys
- **Self-report measures**: PLS-SEM relies on self-reported perceptions; behavioral/cognitive neuroscience measures (fMRI, EEG, eye-tracking) would provide complementary validation
- **Trait moderation magnitude**: The moderation effect (β=0.075) is statistically significant but small-to-moderate in magnitude — meaningful at scale but not transformative per individual

## Key Insight (One-Line Summary)

> Neuromarketing effectiveness is BOTH mechanism-driven AND trait-contingent — not universally uniform. Emotional appeals and cognitive processing are the dominant co-drivers; consumer traits amplify the efficacy→impulsivity relationship; scarcity cues and endorsements show diminishing returns and should be reassessed. Trait-based segmentation is essential.
