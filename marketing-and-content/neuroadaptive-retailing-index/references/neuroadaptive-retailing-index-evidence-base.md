# Neuroadaptive Retailing Index (NRI) — Evidence Base

## Primary Source

**Qiao, X., Wang, L., & Zain, M. (2026).** "Neuroadaptive retailing: Integrating multisensory biometrics and predictive emotion modelling to decode consumer immersion in hybrid shopping environments." *Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services*, Volume 92, June 2026, 104776. DOI: 10.1016/j.jretconser.2026.104776.

## The Problem Formulation

Retailing is undergoing a profound transformation in which consumer experiences increasingly unfold within hybrid (phygital) environments that integrate physical settings with digitally mediated, sensor-enabled interfaces. Smart mirrors, augmented-reality overlays, emotion-aware digital signage, and algorithmic personalization systems now operate alongside traditional store elements, creating experiences that evolve dynamically during the shopping journey.

While these developments have expanded the experiential possibilities of retail design, dominant consumer-behavior frameworks remain limited in explaining how immersion forms and fluctuates in real time — particularly when environments *actively respond* to consumers' internal states rather than merely presenting fixed stimuli.

## The Theoretical Gaps NRI Addresses

### S-O-R limitations

The widely adopted Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) model posits that environmental cues (stimuli) influence internal affective and cognitive states (organism), which in turn drive behavioral outcomes (responses). However, most empirical applications of S-O-R in retailing implicitly assume **unidirectional causality and temporally stable organismic states**, offering limited insight into the continuous neurophysiological dynamics that underlie immersion.

### Experiential/sensory marketing limitations

Experiential marketing and sensory marketing research has demonstrated that multisensory atmospherics enhance emotional engagement and memorability, yet these studies typically examine **additive or isolated sensory effects** rather than adaptive, feedback-driven interactions between consumers and environments.

### Flow theory limitations

Flow theory provides an important conceptual bridge for understanding immersion as a dynamic state rather than a cumulative outcome. Flow emerges when environmental demands are optimally aligned with an individual's cognitive and emotional capacities, producing deep absorption, intrinsic motivation, and temporal distortion. In retail contexts, flow has been linked to enjoyment, exploration, and purchase intention; however, **flow has rarely been operationalized using objective neurophysiological indicators, nor has it been examined within environments that adapt in real time**.

## The NRI Construct

The Neuroadaptive Retailing Index (NRI) is grounded explicitly in S-O-R logic, flow theory, and experiential design principles.

**Conceptual definition:** NRI operationalizes **neuroadaptive coherence**, defined as the degree to which environmental adaptations dynamically align with consumers' moment-to-moment cognitive-affective states.

Rather than equating immersion with emotional intensity or stimulus richness, NRI conceptualizes immersion as **adaptive alignment**, consistent with flow-based optimal stimulation and organism-environment resonance. In this sense, the retail environment is not merely a source of stimuli but an active participant in a closed-loop experiential system, continuously adjusting to consumer feedback.

## The Three Biometric Channels

NRI integrates cross-modal synchrony among three biometric modalities:

| Channel | What It Measures | Theoretical Ground |
|---|---|---|
| EEG | Motivational and attentional asymmetries linked to approach-avoidance behavior | Consumer neuroscience; approach-avoidance motivation |
| GSR | Continuous index of autonomic arousal | Affective computing; arousal dynamics |
| Eye-tracking | Attentional allocation and visual stability; visual-emotional congruence | Visual attention; emotional congruence |

When combined with predictive emotion modelling, these modalities allow researchers to infer short-term affective trajectories rather than relying solely on retrospective evaluation.

Existing studies typically analyze biometric channels in isolation, producing fragmented insights that fail to capture the integrative nature of immersive experience. The retail literature lacks a unified, theory-driven metric capable of translating multisensory biometric synchrony into an interpretable indicator of consumer immersion. NRI fills that gap.

## The Beneficial-Adaptivity-vs-Excessive-Responsiveness Distinction

NRI distinguishes between beneficial adaptivity and excessive responsiveness, recognizing that **over-adaptation may violate flow conditions and induce cognitive fatigue or privacy discomfort**.

The study examines not only whether neuroadaptive alignment enhances immersion, but also **where adaptive interventions become intrusive**, thereby identifying cognitive and ethical thresholds for responsible deployment.

This is the critical contribution: the metric is designed to surface the threshold, not to monotonically maximize adaptation.

## Methodology

### Experimental Design

A controlled mixed-reality retail simulation combining physical product interaction with adaptive digital overlays.

### Participants

- Recruited through a commercial urban lifestyle panel to ensure demographic diversity across age, income, and occupation
- Explicitly avoiding student or convenience sampling
- Final analytical sample: **86 participants** (after exclusions for data quality anomalies such as signal artifacts exceeding predefined thresholds)

### Ethical Safeguards

- Ethical approval, informed consent, and data-protection safeguards consistent with biometric and privacy regulations (GDPR) embedded in the research design
- Acknowledgment of the sensitivity of affective and physiological data

### Conditions

High-adaptivity vs. low-adaptivity conditions. Contrasting the two examines not only whether neuroadaptive alignment enhances immersion, but where adaptive interventions become intrusive.

## Empirical Results

| Condition | NRI Mean | SD |
|---|---|---|
| High-adaptivity | **0.68** | 0.14 |
| Low-adaptivity baseline | **0.42** | 0.16 |

The elevated NRI scores in high-adaptivity conditions demonstrate how neuroadaptive coherence links to cognitive-affective mechanisms.

## The Three Contributions (per the paper)

1. **Theoretical** — extends consumer-experience scholarship by integrating neuroscience with S-O-R and flow frameworks, reframing immersion as a dynamic, adaptive system state rather than a static psychological response.
2. **Methodological** — introduces a replicable multimodal framework for biometric fusion and predictive emotion modelling in retail research. Positions physiological data not as standalone indicators but as manifestations of underlying cognitive-affective processes.
3. **Practical** — offers retailers actionable and ethically bounded principles for designing adaptive environments that enhance engagement while respecting consumer autonomy and well-being.

The study responds directly to growing concerns regarding ecological validity, interpretability, and ethics in neuroadaptive retail innovation.

## Related Adjacent Research (context)

- **Customer Digital Twins with neuromarketing + social media data** (Okyere Sefa, Rezaei, & Valilai, *Decision Analytics Journal*, 2026, DOI: 10.1016/j.dajour.2026.100689): integrates EEG recordings with sentiment analysis of user-generated content to capture complementary dimensions of consumer emotion and decision-making. An experimental design monitored emotional responses via EEG during an online shopping task, followed by an ethical priming intervention and sentiment-based analysis. Results show EEG-derived indicators (engagement, workload, emotional valence) and text-based sentiment measures provide partially complementary views, while simple fusion strategies exhibit limited predictive power under real-world, asynchronous conditions. Establishes a foundation for temporally aligned and dynamically adaptive customer digital twin architectures. Reinforces the NRI insight that simple biometric fusion is insufficient — temporal alignment and adaptive architecture matter.

- **Neuromarketing + AI predictive analytics** (Salqaura & Nasib, 2026, Neuro-Viral AI Predictive Framework): ~200 respondents in food and beverage sectors, neuromarketing + viral marketing + purchase decisions. Confirms neuromarketing's effect on purchase decisions but does not address adaptive environments.

- **EEG + Computer Vision purchase intent prediction** (Sathiya & Jeyanthi, 2026, Zenodo): 120 participants, 500 e-commerce images. Hybrid model (Temporal Convolutional Network for EEG + Graph Attention Network for visual attention) reaches 88.3% accuracy and AUC 0.94 in predicting consumer purchase intent. Unimodal EEG: 74.2%; unimodal visual: 72.8%. Reinforces the NRI thesis that multimodal fusion outperforms single-channel analysis.

## Limitations

- The study is a single mixed-reality retail simulation; independent replication in other phygital contexts needed.
- The NRI computation's exact weighting of the three channels (the "empirically validated relationships with subjective immersion and behavioral intention") is detailed in the full paper but the specific weight derivation is methodology-heavy.
- The threshold where adaptation becomes intrusive is identified but not fully quantified as a generalizable function — it is consumer- and context-specific.
- The sample (86) is adequate for the design but not large enough for robust individual-difference modeling.
- The study is in a retail context; translation to IDE/developer-experience contexts (the A-Coder application) is an inference, not a validated finding.
