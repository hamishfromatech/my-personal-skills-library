# Neural Ad Liking Temporal Dynamics — Evidence Base

## Source
Chan, Boksem, Venkatraman, Dietvorst, Scholz, Vo, Falk & Smidts.
"Neural Signals of Video Advertisement Liking: Insights into Psychological Processes and Their Temporal Dynamics."
*Journal of Marketing Research*, 61(5), 891–913, 2024.
DOI: 10.1177/00222437231194319

## Study Design

### Part 1: Neural Signal Mapping
- **Modality:** fMRI
- **Datasets:** Three independent fMRI datasets
- **Stimuli:** Video advertisements
- **Neural measures:** Perception, language, executive function, memory, social cognition, emotion
- **Method:** Neural signals mapped to moment-to-moment self-reported ad liking

### Part 2: Aggregate Prediction
- Same neural signals examined for ability to predict aggregate ad liking across viewers
- Combined neural measures predict out-of-sample ad liking

## Key Findings

### Temporal Dynamics of Ad Liking

| Time Window | Dominant Process | Predictive Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | Emotional response | Peak predictiveness; fast, visceral, automatic |
| 3–10 seconds | Emotional decline | Emotional signals lose predictive power rapidly |
| 10+ seconds | Social cognition | Becomes dominant predictor; stable throughout |
| Throughout | Executive function | Suppressed (reflects passive viewing, not active deliberation) |

### Emotion vs Social Cognition
- **Emotion:** Early, fast, visceral reaction. Peaks around 3 seconds. Declines quickly.
- **Social cognition:** Later, reflective. Builds as viewers process narrative, characters, meaning. Sustains engagement.
- **Implication:** Ads that rely only on early emotional hooks lose predictive power. Ads with social cognition elements sustain liking.

### Liking as Cumulative Process
- Liking is not a single moment but a cumulative process shaped by evolving neural states.
- Different neural systems engage at different times during ad exposure.
- The temporal flow between emotional peaks and sustained meaningful storytelling drives overall liking.

## Practical Implications

### For Ad Structure
1. **Early emotional hook (0-3s):** Use visceral, attention-grabbing emotional content
2. **Transition to narrative (3-10s):** Begin building socially meaningful stories
3. **Sustained social cognition (10s+):** Characters, scenarios, empathy, perspective-taking
4. **Avoid over-reliance on pure emotion:** Excitement ads create short-lived interest; love ads build long-lasting connections

### For Content Strategy
- Stories that foster social connection and meaning create receptive context for product information
- Product messaging should be integrated within storytelling, not appended after emotional hook
- Social cognition activation sustains receptivity to information longer than emotional activation alone

## Platform Considerations

| Format | Expected Dynamics |
|---|---|
| Traditional video ads | Full temporal sequence (emotion → social cognition) |
| Short-form (TikTok, Reels) | Compressed; emotion and social cognition nearly simultaneous |
| Influencer content | Stronger social cognition from outset (parasocial relationship) |
| Interactive ads | Enhanced executive function throughout (deliberative processing) |
| AI-generated ads | Uncanny valley risk; social cognition system penalizes subtle artificiality |

## Viral Content Connection

### Related Study (PNAS, 2024)
- Same research group found brain activity in reward and mentalizing regions predicts whether content goes viral
- Same psychological processes that drive ad liking (emotion + social cognition) also drive sharing behavior
- People share content as "social currency" to signal group belonging, values, or identity
- Emotional content spreads rapidly (early processing dominance explains viral speed)
- Social cognition explains why shared content reinforces social bonds
