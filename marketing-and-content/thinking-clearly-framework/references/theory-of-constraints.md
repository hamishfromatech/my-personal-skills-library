# Theory of Constraints & Dependency Mapping

## Theory of Constraints (TOC)

### Origin
Developed by Dr. Eliyahu Goldratt in his 1984 book "The Goal." Originally for manufacturing, now applied to business, personal productivity, and decision-making.

### Core Principle
**Every system has at least one constraint** (bottleneck) that limits its performance. Improving anything OTHER than the constraint is an illusion — it won't improve overall system performance.

### The Five Focusing Steps:
1. **Identify** the system's constraint
2. **Exploit** the constraint (get maximum output from it without new investment)
3. **Subordinate** everything else to the constraint
4. **Elevate** the constraint (invest to increase its capacity)
5. **Repeat** — once the constraint is broken, find the next one

### Application to Thinking Clearly
When overwhelmed, ask: **"What is the ONE biggest constraint that, if resolved, would make everything else simpler?"**

This is NOT about doing everything. It's about finding the single lever that moves everything else.

### Why It Works for Overwhelm
- Overwhelm comes from trying to resolve everything simultaneously
- TOC tells you to focus on ONE thing
- Resolving the constraint creates momentum that makes other problems easier
- It transforms "how do I do it all?" into "what do I do FIRST?"

## Dependency Mapping

### What Is It?
A visual technique where you:
1. **Dump** every factor, consideration, blocker, and variable onto paper
2. **Connect** them — which factors cause, enable, or block others?
3. **Map** the relationships visually
4. **Identify** the biggest constraints (nodes with the most dependencies pointing TO them)

### How to Create a Dependency Map

#### Step 1: The Brain Dump
Write down EVERYTHING that's making this situation difficult. No filtering. No organization. Just get it all out of your head.

#### Step 2: Identify Relationships
For each item, ask:
- What does this DEPEND on? (What must happen before this can happen?)
- What does this BLOCK? (What can't happen until this is resolved?)
- What does this ENABLE? (What becomes possible once this is done?)

#### Step 3: Draw Connections
- Arrows from dependencies to dependents
- Thick arrows = critical dependencies
- Cluster related items
- Highlight items with the most incoming arrows (these are your constraints)

#### Step 4: Act on the Map
- Start with items that have NO dependencies (things you can do immediately)
- Move to items with the most dependencies pointing TO them (your biggest constraints)
- Schedule constraint-busting time

### When to Use Dependency Maps
- Complex decisions with many factors
- Project planning with multiple interdependent tasks
- Product strategy with competing priorities
- Personal upskilling (what skills block other skills?)
- Any situation where "everything feels urgent"

## Scheduling Constraint-Busting Time

### The Principle
Long-term constraints that aren't urgent yet will BECOME urgent. If you don't proactively unblock them, you'll be on a hamster wheel of constant firefighting.

### The Method
1. **Identify** long-term constraints from your dependency map
2. **Estimate** how long it takes to unblock each one
3. **Schedule** small blocks of time weekly (even 1 hour)
4. **Review** weekly — has progress been made?

### Example: Learning to Learn
- **Constraint:** Low learning efficiency wastes hours/week on documentation, study, etc.
- **Impact:** 20-30% efficiency improvement compounds into massive time savings
- **Solution:** 3-5 minutes per week reading a newsletter, understanding one insight, practicing it
- **Result:** Compounding benefit for the rest of your life

### The Hamster Wheel Warning
When you neglect long-term constraints:
- New constraints always appear around the corner
- You're constantly working on things that feel "top priority"
- But nothing moves the needle long-term
- You're busy but not effective

### The Alternative
- Proactively unblock constraints before they become urgent
- Schedule small, consistent time blocks
- Compound small improvements
- Move from firefighting to strategic progress
