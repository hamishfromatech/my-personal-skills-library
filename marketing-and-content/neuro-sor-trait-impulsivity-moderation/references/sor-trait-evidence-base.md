# S-O-R Trait Impulsivity Moderation Evidence Base

## Primary source

Nagpal, K., Bala, K., Singh, S., Yadav, A., & Paliwal, M. (2026). "Exploring neuromarketing's influence on consumer impulsivity through the lens of personality traits." *Frontiers in Psychology*, 17:1778248. doi: 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1778248. Published 18 March 2026. CC BY.

## Study design

- **Sample:** 609 digitally active consumers from the Delhi–NCR region, India, who frequently interacted with neuromarketing-driven content (from 629 distributed; 20 excluded for missing values, outliers, inconsistent responses, failed attention checks; effective response rate ≈ 97%).
- **Method:** Quantitative, cross-sectional; Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0.
- **Common-method-bias controls:** anonymity assurance, psychological separation of predictor/criterion variables, randomised item order, attention checks, Harman's single-factor test, full collinearity VIF < 3.3.
- **Demographics:** predominantly middle-aged (35–45 = 35.6%), 55% male, 75.4% married, 37.6% postgraduate; diverse occupations.

## Theoretical foundation

### S-O-R framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974)
- **Stimuli (S):** external environmental cues — here, six neuromarketing determinants.
- **Organism (O):** internal psychological/evaluative processes — here, *neuromarketing efficacy* (the consumer's perceived psychological effectiveness of the stimuli).
- **Response (R):** observable behavioural outcomes — here, consumer impulsivity (spontaneous, unplanned purchasing driven by immediate psychological activation).

### Dual-process theory (Evans & Stanovich, 2013)
- **System 1:** intuitive, affect-driven processing. Neuromarketing stimuli frequently activate this.
- **System 2:** analytical, deliberative processing. Structured informational cues can reinforce credibility through System 2.
- Impulsive behaviour in neuromarketing contexts emerges from an interaction between intuitive activation and cognitively reinforced persuasion, not purely affective arousal.

### Consumer traits as moderators
Relatively stable intrinsic characteristics influencing perception, interpretation, and response to persuasive stimuli. The study focuses on traits reflecting emotional susceptibility, FOMO, and social-influence sensitivity. These function as boundary conditions within the S-O-R framework.

## Measurement model (reliability and validity)

- Cronbach's alpha: 0.756–0.952 (all > 0.70).
- Composite reliability (rho_c): 0.782–0.963.
- AVE: 0.572–0.841 (all > 0.50).
- VIF: 1.005–1.999 (all < 3.3 — no multicollinearity).
- Discriminant validity: Fornell–Larcker satisfied; HTMT all < 0.90 (highest 0.875 between Cognitive Process and Consumer Impulsivity).

## Structural model results

### R² and predictive relevance
- Consumer impulsivity: R² = 0.708, adjusted R² = 0.707, Q²predict = 0.689.
- Neuromarketing efficacy: R² = 0.697, adjusted R² = 0.694, Q²predict = 0.748.
- SRMR: 0.070 (saturated), 0.079 (estimated) — both < 0.08.

### Path coefficients (bootstrap, 5,000 resamples)

| Sensory triggers → neuromarketing efficacy | 0.173 | 8.277 | 0.000 | Significant |

### Effect sizes (f²)
- Neuromarketing efficacy: 0.836 (large)
- Consumer impulsivity: 0.454 (substantial)
- Emotional appeals: 0.415 (substantial)
- Cognitive process: 0.236 (moderate)
- Sensory triggers: 0.098 (small-moderate)
- Neuro pricing: 0.059 (small)
- Consumer traits: 0.022 (small)
- Scarcity & urgency: 0.006 (negligible)
- Endorsement influence: 0.005 (negligible)

## Key interpretive findings

1. **Emotional appeals dominate** (β = 0.469). Affect-dominant decision models confirmed; emotionally salient stimuli enhance perceived persuasive effectiveness and facilitate rapid evaluative processing (System 1). Emotional activation reduces cognitive resistance.

2. **Cognitive processing is the second-strongest lever** (β = 0.378). This does not contradict impulsivity — structured informational cues enhance evaluative confidence and credibility, amplifying the organismic evaluation that translates into impulsive action. Impulsive behaviour emerges from affective activation *and* cognitively reinforced persuasion.

3. **Sensory triggers and neuro-pricing are significant but smaller** (β = 0.173 and 0.134). Multisensory engagement and value framing operate through heuristic evaluation.

4. **Scarcity/urgency and endorsement are NOT significant** (p = 0.095 and 0.127). In digitally saturated environments, repetitive exposure to time-bound promotions and influencer endorsements attenuates persuasive impact, potentially due to reduced perceived authenticity. This is a direct contravention of conventional marketing wisdom.

5. **Neuromarketing efficacy is the strong transmission mechanism** to impulsivity (β = 0.562).

6. **Consumer traits have a strong direct effect** on impulsivity (β = 0.416) and **significantly moderate** the efficacy → impulsivity relationship (β = 0.075, p < 0.001). The relationship strengthens at higher dispositional susceptibility. Neuromarketing responsiveness is trait-contingent, not uniform.

## Managerial implications (from the study)

- Prioritise affectively resonant storytelling aligned with aspirational/identity-based themes (short-form video on Reels/Shorts).
- Complement emotion with structured, credibility-enhancing information (concise bullet-point value propositions, certifications, transparent comparison charts).
- Refine digital design elements (colour palettes, visual hierarchy, interface responsiveness, auditory cues).
- Implement psychologically informed pricing (anchor comparisons, bundle framing, charm pricing) — but keep transparent.
- Adopt trait-informed segmentation supported by AI-driven analytics (novelty-seeking → launch alerts; emotionally susceptible → narrative campaigns; socially influenced → peer reviews).
- De-prioritise excessive scarcity promotions and influencer partnerships — diminishing returns.
- Ethical responsibility: overuse of manipulative tactics produces fatigue and brand aversion; sustainable advantage requires transparency and trust.

## Limitations (from the study)

- Delhi–NCR lifestyle-product segment; generalise cautiously across categories, income, cultures.
- Cross-sectional — limits causal inference; longitudinal/experimental needed for temporal dynamics.
- Self-reported perception-based measures, not direct neurophysiological data — reflects perceived responses, not neural activation itself.
- Traits limited to emotional susceptibility, FOMO, social-influence sensitivity; future work could add emotional intelligence, cognitive reflection, self-regulatory capacity.
