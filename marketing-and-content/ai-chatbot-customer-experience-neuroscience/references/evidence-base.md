# AI Chatbot Customer Experience Neuroscience — Evidence Base

## Study Details

**Institution:** University of Twente, Enschede, The Netherlands

**Publication:** Journal of Consumer Marketing, 2026

**Research focus:** Neuroscientific assessment of affective (arousal) and cognitive (attention) customer experience with AI-powered chatbots across customer journey stages (prepurchase and purchase), using electrodermal activity (EDA) and eye-tracking.

**Significance:** This is one of the first studies to use neurophysiological measures (rather than self-report surveys) to empirically compare AI-chatbot-mediated customer experience against a standard-website control across distinct customer journey stages. The dissociation between attention (captured) and arousal (not moved) is a finding with direct implications for how conversational AI should be designed.

## Study Design

**Design type:** Mixed-design experiment with a between-subjects factor (AI chatbot vs standard website) and a within-subjects factor (customer journey stage: prepurchase vs purchase).

**Two groups:**

| Group | Interface | Role |
|-------|-----------|------|
| **Experimental (AI chatbot)** | AI-powered conversational chatbot guiding the customer through the journey | Tests whether conversational AI changes affective and cognitive experience |
| **Control (standard website)** | Standard website interface without an AI chatbot | Baseline for comparison of arousal and attention dynamics |

Both groups traversed the same customer journey stages (prepurchase and purchase) so that stage effects could be compared within groups and interface effects could be compared between groups.

**Customer journey stages measured:**

1. **Prepurchase:** Need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives. The customer is exploring, comparing, and building a decision model.
2. **Purchase:** The actual transaction / buying decision. The customer is committing to a choice.

## Neurophysiological Methodology

### Electrodermal Activity (EDA) — Affective (Arousal) Dimension

**What it measures:** EDA (also known as galvanic skin response or skin conductance) measures the electrical conductance of the skin, which fluctuates with sweat gland activity driven by the sympathetic nervous system. Increases in skin conductance indicate increases in autonomic arousal — emotional activation intensity. EDA cannot distinguish valence (positive vs negative emotion), only arousal intensity.

**Why it was chosen:** EDA is the standard non-invasive measure of affective arousal in consumer neuroscience. It captures the emotional activation component of customer experience that self-report surveys miss (social desirability bias, post-hoc rationalization, inability to introspect autonomic responses).

**What was analyzed:** Arousal dynamics across the customer journey stages — specifically whether the AI-chatbot group showed different arousal trajectories compared to the control group, and whether arousal peaked at particular stages.

### Eye-Tracking — Cognitive (Attention) Dimension

**What it measures:** Eye-tracking records gaze position, fixation duration, fixation count, dwell time, and pupil dilation. Fixation metrics indicate where and for how long visual attention is allocated. Pupil dilation correlates with cognitive load and emotional arousal.

**Why it was chosen:** Eye-tracking is the standard measure of overt visual attention and cognitive engagement in consumer research. It captures the cognitive investment the customer directs toward the interface and the information it presents — the attentional component of customer experience.

**What was analyzed:** Attention levels across the customer journey stages — specifically whether the AI-chatbot group showed different attention profiles compared to the control group, and how attention evolved through the journey.

### Why EDA + Eye-Tracking Together

The two measures are orthogonal: EDA captures affective arousal (the "feeling" dimension) while eye-tracking captures cognitive attention (the "thinking" dimension). Using both allows the study to detect whether the chatbot affects one dimension, both, or neither — and critically, whether the two dimensions dissociate (move independently). This cross-modal pairing follows the triangulation logic established in the neuromarketing consumer-journey framework: arousal without attention misses cognitive engagement, and attention without arousal misses emotional impact.

## Key Findings in Detail

### Finding 1: AI chatbot did NOT significantly impact arousal dynamics

The AI-chatbot group showed no statistically significant difference in EDA-measured arousal compared to the control (standard website) group. The chatbot did not make the customer experience more emotionally activating than a standard website.

**Interpretation:** This challenges the common industry assumption that conversational AI is inherently more emotionally engaging than static web interfaces. The chatbot's conversational format did not translate into greater emotional activation. The affective dimension of the customer experience was equivalent regardless of whether the customer interacted with a chatbot or a standard website.

**Implication:** Chatbots should not be deployed on the assumption that they provide emotional engagement by default. If emotional engagement is the goal, it must be deliberately designed into the chatbot (reassurance language, playful elements, empathetic register) rather than assumed to emerge from the conversational format.

### Finding 2: Chatbot users developed HIGHER attention levels throughout the customer journey

The AI-chatbot group showed significantly higher eye-tracking-measured attention levels compared to the control group, sustained across the customer journey stages. The chatbot captured and maintained greater cognitive engagement throughout the experience.

**Interpretation:** The conversational format is a cognitive-engagement engine. Users pay more attention, process more information, and stay more cognitively invested when interacting with a chatbot than when navigating a standard website. This is a genuine strength — but it is a cognitive strength, not an affective one.

**Implication:** The elevated attention means users are actively processing the information the chatbot provides. This makes information quality critical: low-quality, generic, or contradictory chatbot responses waste the cognitive investment and create dissonance. The chatbot must reward the attention it captures with high-quality, stage-tailored information. It also means the chatbot is sustaining cognitive effort — which can become cognitive load if not managed with supportive visual/audio features.

### Finding 3: Peak arousal occurred during the purchase stage for both groups

Both the AI-chatbot group and the control group showed peak arousal at the purchase stage. The transaction moment is the emotional high point of the customer journey regardless of interface.

**Interpretation:** The purchase decision carries inherent emotional weight — the commitment of money, the risk of a wrong decision, the finality of the choice. This emotional weight is interface-independent: it arises from the decision itself, not from the medium through which the decision is made.

**Implication:** The purchase stage is where emotional support matters most, and where the chatbot's affective flatness (Finding 1) is most costly. The chatbot must deliberately shift register at the purchase stage — from informational to supportive, from exploratory to reassuring — to match the elevated arousal it is not causing but also not addressing.

### The Dissociation: Attention Up, Arousal Flat

The combined finding is a dissociation between the cognitive and affective dimensions of the chatbot experience:

| Dimension | Chatbot vs Control | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| Affective (EDA arousal) | No significant difference | Chatbot does not increase emotional activation |
| Cognitive (eye-tracking attention) | Significantly higher for chatbot | Chatbot increases cognitive engagement |
| Arousal peak (both groups) | Purchase stage | Transaction moment is emotionally weighty regardless of interface |

This dissociation is the study's central contribution. It means that chatbot analytics that measure only engagement (session length, interaction count, completion rate) are measuring the cognitive dimension and missing the affective gap. A chatbot can look highly effective on engagement metrics while leaving the customer emotionally unaffected — and the purchase stage, where emotional support matters most, is exactly where the chatbot's affective neutrality does the most damage.

## Practical Implications

The study derives four practical implications for AI chatbot design, each responding to the empirical findings:

### 1. Offer emotional reassurance during the purchase stage

**Root finding:** Peak arousal occurs at purchase for all users; chatbots do not add affective engagement.

**Design action:** The chatbot should explicitly shift to a supportive, reassuring register at the purchase stage. Acknowledge the decision weight. Provide social proof, guarantees, and confirmatory language. Offer a human handoff. Use warmer, shorter, simpler language. The goal is to provide the emotional support that the chatbot format does not inherently supply, precisely at the moment the customer needs it most.

### 2. Incorporate playful elements

**Root finding:** Chatbots capture attention but not arousal; the affective gap is present throughout the journey.

**Design action:** Deliberate playfulness during the prepurchase stage can close the affective gap. Light conversational personality, curiosity prompts, gentle humor, micro-interactions that produce small positive emotional moments. Playfulness is affective compensation, not gamification-for-its-own-sake. Critically, playfulness should be withdrawn at the purchase stage, where it reads as trivializing a high-stakes, high-arousal moment.

### 3. Provide high-quality, customized information tailored to each journey stage

**Root finding:** Chatbot users sustain higher attention, meaning they are actively processing information.

**Design action:** The chatbot must reward the elevated attention with information quality. Generic FAQ dumps, contradictory responses, or irrelevant recommendations waste the cognitive investment and create dissonance. Information should be tailored to the journey stage:
- **Prepurchase:** Rich, comparative, exploratory information. The chatbot as a knowledgeable guide, not a search bar.
- **Purchase:** Minimal, decision-critical information only. Reduce the information surface to what the user needs to complete the transaction confidently.

### 4. Incorporate supportive visual and audio features to reduce cognitive load

**Root finding:** The chatbot sustains attention, which means it also sustains cognitive effort.

**Design action:** Supportive modalities offload cognitive work from the verbal/textual channel to visual and audio channels. Comparison tables, product images, progress indicators, decision-tree diagrams, optional voice output, audio confirmation cues, structured formatting with visual hierarchy. The goal is not to add stimulation but to reduce the cognitive friction the chatbot's attention-sustaining format creates.

## A-Tech Alignment

### Open-Source AI

The study examines AI-powered chatbots — a category that is increasingly dominated by open-source and open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, etc.). The practical implications (emotional reassurance, playful elements, stage-tailored information, visual/audio support) are design patterns that can be implemented on top of any open-source LLM. The findings are not model-specific; they are interface-specific. This means A-Tech can build chatbot experiences that implement the study's implications using fully open-source AI, with no dependency on proprietary APIs.

The four design implications translate directly into open-source implementation opportunities:
- **Emotional reassurance:** System-prompt engineering and register-shifting logic that detects the purchase stage and adjusts the chatbot's tone. Fully implementable with open-source LLMs.
- **Playful elements:** Conversational personality design via prompt engineering and response-style configuration. No proprietary dependency.
- **Stage-tailored information:** Journey-stage detection and information-routing logic. Implementable as an open-source orchestration layer.
- **Visual/audio support:** Front-end rendering of chatbot responses with structured visual elements. Open-source UI frameworks and text-to-speech models.

### Data Privacy

The study used EDA (biometric) and eye-tracking (biometric-adjacent) measures in a controlled lab setting. A-Tech's values prohibit biometric surveillance in live products. The privacy-first translation is to substitute behavioral signals collected on-device:

| Lab measure | Privacy-first behavioral proxy | Collection method |
|-------------|-------------------------------|-------------------|
| EDA arousal | Response latency variance, message-length variability, pause-before-action duration | On-device timestamp deltas |
| EDA arousal peak at purchase | Pause-before-confirm duration, checkout-stage message length drop | On-device event logging |
| Eye-tracking fixation duration | Dwell time on chat messages, re-read (scroll-back) frequency | On-device viewport telemetry |
| Eye-tracking fixation count | Number of distinct UI elements interacted with, comparison depth | On-device interaction logging |
| Pupil dilation (cognitive load) | Error recovery time, help-query rate, session fragmentation | On-device error/help event logging |

All proxies are computed locally from interaction telemetry the user already generates by using the product. No biometric sensors, no third-party data sharing, no neural data transmission. This is the privacy-first version of the study's methodology — executable in live A-Tech products without the lab equipment.

### Financial Freedom

The study's findings directly support revenue durability and financial independence:

- **Purchase-stage reassurance reduces drop-off.** The arousal peak at purchase is where customers are most likely to abandon. Chatbots that shift to reassurance mode at this stage reduce purchase-stage abandonment, directly improving conversion and revenue.
- **Attention capture increases information processing.** Customers who pay more attention process more information, which — if the information is high-quality and stage-tailored — increases purchase confidence and reduces post-purchase regret (returns, churn, chargebacks).
- **Cognitive-load reduction increases completion.** Supportive visual/audio features reduce the friction of sustained attention, increasing the likelihood that a cognitively engaged customer completes the journey rather than abandoning it from mental fatigue.
- **The affective gap is a revenue leak.** If a chatbot captures attention but does not address the emotional dimension — especially at the purchase arousal peak — it is leaving conversion on the table. The study gives A-Tech the diagnostic to detect this leak and the four design patterns to close it.

### Practical Implementation

The study's findings are immediately actionable without lab equipment:

1. **Audit existing chatbot:** Map conversational flows to prepurchase and purchase stages. Identify whether the chatbot shifts register at purchase or uses a single register throughout.
2. **Implement stage detection:** Detect when the user has transitioned from exploration (prepurchase) to commitment (purchase) — via intent signals, checkout-page entry, or explicit confirmation language.
3. **Apply the four implications:** Emotional reassurance (register shift at purchase), playful elements (prepurchase only), stage-tailored information (rich at prepurchase, minimal at purchase), visual/audio support (reduce cognitive load throughout).
4. **Measure with privacy-first proxies:** Track the behavioral proxies for attention (dwell time, re-read frequency, follow-up depth) and arousal (response latency variance, pause-before-confirm, message-length drop at purchase).
5. **Watch for the dissociation:** If attention proxies rise but satisfaction proxies (return rate, post-purchase session length, voluntary feedback) do not, you are replicating the study's finding — invest in the affective layer.

## Study Limitations and Open Questions

- **Two stages only.** The study measured prepurchase and purchase but not post-purchase. The neuromarketing-consumer-journey-3x3-framework identifies post-purchase as the field's biggest gap. Whether the chatbot's affective flatness persists or compounds in the post-purchase stage (affecting satisfaction, loyalty, advocacy) is an open question.
- **Lab setting.** EDA and eye-tracking were measured in a controlled lab, not in the wild. Ecological validity is a known limitation of lab-based neuromarketing. The privacy-first behavioral proxies address this by enabling in-product measurement, but they require validation against the lab findings.
- **Valence not measured.** EDA captures arousal intensity but not valence (positive vs negative). The flat arousal finding means the chatbot did not change emotional intensity — but it does not tell us whether the emotional quality (positive vs negative) differed. Eye-tracking pupil dilation can partially address valence, but the study's primary affective measure (EDA) is valence-blind.
- **Single chatbot design.** The study tested one AI chatbot implementation against a control. The findings may be specific to that chatbot's design (personality, information quality, conversational style). Chatbots deliberately designed with the four practical implications (reassurance, playfulness, tailored info, visual/audio support) may produce different arousal profiles. This is an implementation opportunity, not a limitation of the methodology.
- **Generalization.** The study's participants and product context may not generalize to all markets, cultures, or product categories. Replication across contexts is needed — and the privacy-first behavioral proxies enable exactly this kind of in-the-wild replication without lab equipment.
