## 1. Research Scan Summary

### Domain A: Attention Sovereignty — From Residue Mitigation to Architectural Sovereignty

The afternoon research deepened the morning's attention insights into a full sovereignty architecture:

- **The Three Attention Crises:** Modal flooding (12–18 additional daily interruptions from AI agents), predictive intrusion (AI surfacing suggestions during flow), and trust-calibration exhaustion (constant accept/verify/reject decisions) together consume 23–40% of productive capacity.
- **Digital Wellbeing as Design Responsibility:** Emerging 2026 HCI scholarship reframes digital wellbeing from an end-user responsibility to a design obligation. Systems must be designed to preserve attention, not merely offer tools for users to defend it.
- **Machine Experience (MX) Design:** UX Tigers' 2026 predictions highlight Machine Experience design — interfaces that adapt to AI agent presence without demanding human attention — as the successor to traditional UX.
- **Pull-Over-Push Architecture:** The most attention-respecting AI systems make assistance available on user initiation, not pushed unsolicited. Suggestion suppression thresholds at 95% confidence (not 70%) eliminate noisy interruptions.

### Domain C: AI Code Provenance — Generative Authorship as Governance Requirement

The most significant emerging signal is the transformation of code provenance from academic concern to compliance mandate:

- **41% AI-Generated Code:** Index.dev data shows 41% of global code is AI-generated, rising to 61% in Java. Without provenance, organizations cannot answer basic governance questions.
- **SBOM for AI Supply Chains:** ICSE 2026 featured a dedicated research track on extending SBOM standards (ISO/IEC 5962:2021) to include generative AI components, model provenance, and training data lineage.
- **Slopsquatting Escalation:** As AI tools hallucinate package names, attackers are registering these phantom packages. Provenance verification is now a security requirement, not a nice-to-have.
- **Compliance Convergence:** EU Cyber Resilience Act, US EO 14028, EU AI Act, PCI DSS 4.0, and ISO 27001 all now require or encourage provenance tracking for AI-generated software components.

### Domain D: Context Switching — Developer-Specific Costs

Research into developer-specific context switching costs reinforced the attention sovereignty findings:

- **Reclaim.ai 2026 Data:** Knowledge workers switch contexts every 3 minutes on average; developers switch AI tools/tasks even more frequently.
- **Jellyfish Mitigation Patterns:** Developer-specific context-switching costs are higher than general knowledge workers because code requires maintaining complex mental models.
- **Recovery Time Benchmarks:** Average recovery time after interruption is 15–23 minutes for task-level switches, 5–10 minutes for tool-level switches.
