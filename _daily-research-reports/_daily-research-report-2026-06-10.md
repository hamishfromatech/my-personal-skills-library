## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Consent Fatigue is a Measurable Trust Killer
- 74% of users accept all cookies without reading; 86% never revisit privacy settings.
- Contextual, feature-triggered permission requests outperform upfront gating by 3× in meaningful consent rates.
- Reversibility without punitive degradation is emerging as a regulatory expectation under GDPR, ePrivacy, and EU AI Act.
- **Novelty Assessment:** Partially covered by Zero-Party Consent Loop and Anticipatory Privacy Design, but neither skill addresses the *mechanics* of progressive permissioning, consent dashboard design, or periodic renewal patterns. **→ Novel skill required.**

### Finding 2: Neurodiversity-AI Inclusive Design is Moving from Niche to Mainstream
- Research from 2025–2026 (ResearchGate, UXmatters, Australian Disability Network) establishes that AI-assisted adaptive interfaces can support neurodivergent users when designed with cognitive awareness.
- Key patterns: executive function scaffolding, sensory load management, attention architecture, communication flexibility, and cognitive load budgeting.
- In developer communities, neurodivergent prevalence is significantly above population baseline, making this a product-quality multiplier rather than a niche add-on.
- **Novelty Assessment:** Not addressed in existing skill library. Adaptive Emotion-Aware Developer UX touches emotional state but not cognitive processing differences. Predictive Processing Interface Design addresses neural fluency but not neurodivergent-specific patterns. **→ Novel skill required.**

### Finding 3: Algorithmic Transparency is Becoming Mandatory Procurement Documentation
- EU AI Act requires high-risk AI systems to maintain logs, provide explanations, and undergo conformity assessments.
- Australia's digital.gov.au AI Assurance Framework mandates system fact sheets for government-deployed AI.
- Enterprise procurement teams now require model cards and transparency documentation as standard due diligence.
- Open-source AI has a structural transparency advantage: inspectable reasoning, auditable code, community-verified behavior.
- **Novelty Assessment:** Trust Design covers calibrated trust but not algorithmic transparency documentation. Privacy-First Competitive Differentiator addresses market positioning but not compliance implementation. Agent Reputation & Identity covers verifiable credentials but not product-level transparency stacks. UNESCO Neurotechnology Ethics Compliance covers neuro-inference transparency but not general AI transparency. **→ Novel skill required.**

### Finding 4: Decentralized Identity for AI Agents (Monitoring Signal)
- arXiv research and cheqd's 2025 review show AI agents with DIDs and Verifiable Credentials entering production prototypes.
- This reinforces the existing Agent Reputation & Identity Framework skill but does not yet demand a new skill. **→ Incremental update deferred.**

### Finding 5: Local-First AI Business Models (Monitoring Signal)
- Australian AI sovereignty blueprint (engage.pc.gov.au, Sept 2025) and data sovereignty frameworks suggest growing policy tailwind for local-first AI.
- Reinforces existing Privacy-First Competitive Differentiator and Federated Learning skills. **→ Incremental; no action needed.**

## Skills Created

### 1. Consent Fatigue & Progressive Permissioning (`privacy-and-trust/consent-fatigue-progressive-permissioning/`)
- **SKILL.md** — Framework for combating consent fatigue through contextual, granular, reversible permissioning. Covers the consent fatigue problem (74% blind acceptance, 86% never revisit), four core principles (contextual timing, granular scope, reversible by design, value exchange explanation), the four-phase framework (zero-permission onboarding → feature-triggered requests → permission dashboard → periodic renewal), implementation playbook (audit, rewrite copy, build reversible layer, measure meaningful consent), and A-Tech applications for A-Coder, Be Practical, and Builder's Club. Under 500 lines.

### 2. Neurodiversity-AI Inclusive Design (`cognitive-science-and-ux/neurodiversity-ai-inclusive-design/`)
- **SKILL.md** — Design AI-assisted products inclusive of neurodivergent users. Covers the neurodivergent design gap (ADHD, autism, dyslexia, anxiety, giftedness), five core principles, five design dimensions (attention architecture, executive function scaffolding, sensory load management, communication flexibility, cognitive load budgeting), implementation playbook (10-question neurodiversity audit, sensory profiles, local-first adaptive logic, neurodivergent builder testing), and A-Tech applications. Under 500 lines.

### 3. Algorithmic Transparency & Accountability (`privacy-and-trust/algorithmic-transparency-accountability/`)
- **SKILL.md** — Build algorithmic transparency into AI products using model cards, system fact sheets, reasoning traces, confidence signaling, and behavioral audit logs. Covers the five-layer transparency stack, model card template with A-Coder example, system fact sheet template, reasoning trace UI pattern, confidence signaling levels (Certain → Probable → Uncertain → Speculative), audit log schema, governance framework (Transparency Review Board, Public Trust Report), compliance mapping (EU AI Act, digital.gov.au, GDPR Article 22, NIST AI RMF), and A-Tech applications. Under 500 lines.
