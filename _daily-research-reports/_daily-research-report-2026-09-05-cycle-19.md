## 2. Cross-Cycle Synthesis (what the week means)

1. **Openness became a ladder, then a compliance strategy.** K2 Horizon defines the top tier (open science: data/recipes/checkpoints/logs); the EU AI Act's 2027 disclosure deadline makes that tier the *cheap seat* for European market access. The two skills together (k2-horizon + eu-ai-act-gpai) give the library a complete "openness as strategy" argument for 2026–27 content.
2. **The open-weight debate got its rubric.** RedMonk's eight lenses slot above the library's quantitative evidence (adoption milestones, ARR trackers, Mozilla triangulation) as the evaluation layer — any future release can be scored in minutes across geography, licensing, capabilities, risks, economics, timing, size, distillation.
3. **Agent payments reached the formal-methods stage.** Within 48 hours: an application-profile specification for commerce settlement (ASP) and a Tamarin formalization of the four shipped protocols (x402/MPP/ACP/AP2) with 40 undocumented consistency findings. The library now holds all three layers: operational (x402-production-checklist), architectural (ASP), and formal (the analysis) — the same maturation curve payments and consensus infrastructure followed.
4. **OSS funding is now a five-leg portfolio.** Endowment / co-op / employment / patron / standards-participation — each targets a distinct failure mode, and the STA Standards network is the first leg that defends *governance* (who writes the rules) rather than *maintenance* (who keeps the code alive).
5. **Cognitive-science evidence keeps complicating easy stories.** The MIT study: the "good" pedagogical design disengages learners while the "bad" open design scores best on immediate tests; RecalibrateGPT: a chunk of AI frustration is interface-flow cost. Both feed the library's honesty norms — measurement horizons and interaction costs must be stated, not assumed.
