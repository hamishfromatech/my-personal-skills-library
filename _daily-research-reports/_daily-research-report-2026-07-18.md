## Research Findings

### 1. AI Employee: Agent-as-Teammate Management Framework (Developer Experience & Flow)

**Sources:** Google Cloud, IBM, MIT Sloan (Feb 2026), AP News (Nov 2025), Cyber.gov.au (May 2026), iTMunch (2026), Agentic.ai (2026), Wikipedia. Synthesized with existing A-Tech skill library research.

**What happened:** Enterprises are beginning to manage AI agents like team members — with onboarding protocols, role definitions, performance reviews, escalation procedures, and offboarding processes. The 2026 consensus definition of agentic AI across Google Cloud, IBM, MIT Sloan, and Wikipedia describes systems that "pursue goals, use tools, and take actions with limited supervision." This autonomy level requires organizational management structures that treat agents as teammates rather than tools.

**Key findings:**

- **The tool-to-teammate shift is driven by five factors:** (1) autonomy increase (agents pursue multi-step goals), (2) persistent identity (context across sessions = institutional knowledge), (3) specialization (role-specific agents), (4) collaboration patterns (human-agent handoffs), (5) accountability requirements (organizations need management structures for agent actions).

- **Three management maturity levels:** Level 1 (Tool: on-demand use, human as operator), Level 2 (Assistant: task delegation, human as supervisor), Level 3 (Teammate: role-based collaboration, human as collaborator + accountable owner). A-Tech target: Level 3 for routine domains, Level 2 for high-stakes, Level 1 for creative/architectural.

- **The Agent HR Lifecycle:** Onboarding (role definition, access provisioning, context seeding, supervised probation, calibration, graduation criteria) → Role Definition (YAML role cards with domain, authority, escalation, success metrics, constraints, privacy) → Performance Review (quarterly: quality, efficiency, collaboration, safety metrics) → Escalation Protocol (complexity, confidence, authority, novelty, risk boundaries) → Offboarding (knowledge transfer, access revocation, audit preservation, transition plan).

- **Five human-agent team design patterns:** (1) Human-in-the-Loop Sandwich (high-stakes), (2) Agent-First with Human Escalation (high-volume), (3) Parallel Agent-Human (creative), (4) Agent-as-Reviewer (QA/learning), (5) Multi-Agent Team with Human Lead (complex projects).

- **Critical human agency guardrails:** (1) accountability always with humans, (2) comprehension checkpoints before accepting output, (3) regular no-AI practice sessions, (4) skill rotation between AI-assisted and manual work, (5) transparent authority (always clear who decided and why).

- **Privacy-first agent management:** local-first processing, scoped credentials (not human-level), audit logging (local, tamper-proof), no behavioral surveillance of humans, data minimization in agent context.

**Novel vs. incremental:** NOVEL as the organizational management framework. The existing library has `ai-agent-evaluation-framework-2026` (evaluating agent performance), `trust-calibration-ux-pattern` (building trust), `transaction-closure-delegated-ai-governance` (governing delegated agents), `ai-engineering-culture-amplifier` (AI as cultural amplifier), `botsitting-botshitting-cycle` (the hidden human labor), `supervisory-engineering-work` (the creation-to-verification shift), and `uncertainty-routed-cascade-architecture` (confidence-based escalation). None provide the HR-lifecycle management framework for treating agents as organizational members with onboarding, role cards, performance reviews, and offboarding. This skill is the management layer that sits above the evaluation, trust, and governance skills.

**A-Tech values alignment:**
- **Open-source AI:** Agent management tooling (role cards, onboarding automation, performance dashboards) as open-source infrastructure; community-owned agent standards
- **Data privacy:** Local-first agent processing; scoped credentials; no human behavioral surveillance; audit logs stay local
- **Financial freedom:** Agent leverage = time leverage = income compounding; skill preservation = career sustainability; open-source infrastructure = no vendor lock-in
- **Practical implementation:** 10-step implementation checklist; 5 team design patterns; YAML role card template; anti-patterns catalog

### 2. Neuromarketing & AI Personalization Ethics 2026 (Marketing & Content)

**Sources:** Frontiers in Neuroergonomics (Gupta, Kapoor & Verma, 2025, PRISMA, 109 studies), iTMunch (Uttamchandani, 2025), Mordor Intelligence market data. Synthesized with existing A-Tech neuromarketing skill library.

**What happened:** Neuromarketing in 2025-2026 has been transformed by AI-driven real-time insights, wearable biometric devices, generative AI creative optimization, and immersive VR/AR testing environments. The global market is projected to surpass $3 billion by 2027. The 2025 Frontiers systematic review established the first standardized definition and the 3×3 typology (decision stages × attitude components). Simultaneously, the ethical risks have escalated: AI personalization can now target subconscious emotional responses at scale, creating unprecedented manipulation potential.

**Key findings:**

- **The 2025-2026 technology landscape:** (1) AI-driven real-time biometric analysis with instant campaign optimization, (2) IoT/wearable continuous biometric streaming for real-world context studies, (3) VR/AR immersive environment testing, (4) generative AI designing creatives optimized for attention/emotional resonance, (5) early brain-computer interface experiments.

- **The standardized definition (Frontiers 2025):** "An interdisciplinary area which applies neuroscience and cognitive neuroscience to business. It is about creating brain-friendly content or communication which helps to understand how consumers react at non-conscious level in real time, based on brain operating principles and the responses can be measured by various neuro-metric or non-neuro metric techniques."

- **The cross-modal insight:** No single neuromarketing tool captures the full picture. The 2025 review's key contribution: tools must be paired (valence needs arousal, arousal needs valence, neural needs attention). Seven core techniques cataloged with strengths and blind spots.

- **The A-Tech behavioral-signal proxy approach:** Maps seven neural constructs (attention, emotional arousal, cognitive load, engagement, reward anticipation, post-purchase satisfaction, decision confidence) to on-device behavioral signals (scroll depth, interaction speed, task completion time, session duration, conversion funnel progression, support ticket sentiment, time-to-decision). All proxies computed on-device. Zero biometric data collected, stored, or transmitted.

- **Seven ethical guardrails for AI-enhanced neuromarketing:** (1) transparency of mechanism, (2) welfare alignment test, (3) reversibility and exit, (4) data minimization and privacy, (5) manipulation boundary, (6) algorithmic fairness, (7) human agency preservation.

- **The nudge spectrum (9 levels):** From information (always acceptable) through defaults and personalization (with transparency + opt-out) to emotional targeting, dark patterns, and biometric manipulation (never acceptable).

- **The 8-check dark pattern audit:** opt-out visibility, shame/guilt usage, genuine free option, genuine time limits, vulnerability exploitation, data collection disclosure, default orientation, deception test.

- **Consumer journey application:** Pre-purchase (expertise content, AI discoverability, transparent comparison), Purchase (clear pricing, no dynamic price discrimination, no artificial urgency), Post-purchase (choice closure rituals, peak-end design, noble edge from open-source mission).

- **Regulatory context:** UNESCO neurotechnology ethics (mental privacy, cognitive liberty), EU AI Act transparency obligations, GDPR Article 22, Chile neuro-rights law, emerging US state biometric protections. A-Tech's privacy-first approach is compliant by design.

**Novel vs. incremental:** NOVEL as the integrated ethical framework for the current technology wave. The existing library has `neuromarketing-consumer-journey-3x3-framework` (the 3×3 typology from the same Frontiers review), `neuromarketing-three-layer-discipline` (basic/translational/applied distinction), `neuromarketing-research-landscape-2026` (bibliometric landscape), `hyper-nudging-ai-personalization-ethics` (Yeung's hypernudging framework), `dark-psychology-neuromarketing-autonomy-defense` (defense framework with five-pillar defense), `neuro-marketing-privacy-first-behavioral-analytics` (privacy-first analytics), `ai-enhanced-neuromarketing-social-media` (PLS-SEM validation), and `neuromarketing-sor-trait-moderation-model` (trait moderation). None integrate the 2025-2026 technology landscape (AI real-time insights, wearables, VR, generative AI, BCIs) with both the behavioral-signal proxy mapping table and the seven-guardrail framework specifically designed for the current AI personalization wave. This skill is the ethical operating framework that contextualizes the existing typology, architecture, and research skills.

**A-Tech values alignment:**
- **Open-source AI:** Open-source NLP models for emotion analysis; auditable marketing logic; community-contributed translational principles
- **Data privacy:** Core principle — behavioral signals over biometric surveillance; on-device processing; zero biometric data collection; compliant by design with all current and anticipated regulations
- **Financial freedom:** Ethical personalization builds trust → sustainable revenue; manipulation → churn and regulatory risk; transparency trust premium (52% pay 7% more per `ai-transparency-trust-premium`)
- **Practical implementation:** 7-guardrail framework + 9-level nudge spectrum + 8-check dark pattern audit + behavioral-signal proxy mapping table + consumer journey application + 6-metric measurement framework

## Synthesis: Cross-Skill Connections

The two new skills form a coherent narrative about the human side of AI maturity:

1. **AI Employee** addresses the organizational dimension — how to manage AI agents as team members while preserving human agency, skills, and accountability.

2. **Neuromarketing AI Personalization Ethics** addresses the consumer dimension — how to leverage AI-driven marketing personalization while preserving consumer autonomy, privacy, and trust.

The common thread: **as AI systems gain autonomy and influence, the frameworks that govern their relationship with humans (whether as teammates or as marketing targets) become the primary differentiator. Technology capability is commoditizing; governance, ethics, and human-centric design are the durable moats.**

Both skills share the same structural pattern:
- A maturity/classification framework (management levels / nudge spectrum)
