## 5. Synthesis: Cross-Cutting Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Minimum-Necessary Principle Across Domains
Three of the five new skills converge on a single principle: **release/share no more than necessary**.
- PPC: minimum-necessary subgraph for Chronicle disclosure (privacy)
- EHDS FL: data minimisation as EHDS compliance requirement (privacy)
- Framework model: open source the complement, not the commercial product (monetization)
This principle cuts across privacy, monetization, and even behavioral design (disclosure messages: share the minimum necessary to inform, not more).

### Pattern 2: The Dual-Axis Distinction
Two skills reveal that seemingly unitary concepts are actually dual-axis:
- Dual-Pathway Habit: strategy (ACC→RSC) vs execution (LOFC→CS) — orthogonal, not correlated
- Framework vs Open Core: the OSS-commercial relationship differs (puzzle pieces vs concentric circles)
Conflating the two axes in either case leads to failed interventions: habit products that target frequency when the bottleneck is strategy; open source strategies that compete with their own commercial product.

### Pattern 3: Regulation as Architecture Driver
Two privacy skills show regulation driving architectural design, not just compliance:
- EHDS FL: the 3-layer architecture exists because EHDS mandates it (Art. 33/46/50/53/71); the technical algorithms are secondary to the governance layer
- PPC: GDPR Art. 5(1)(c) data minimisation and HIPAA minimum-necessary are the optimization objectives, not afterthoughts
The implication: in regulated domains, the architecture IS the compliance. Bolt-on compliance fails.

### Pattern 4: Measurement Before Intervention
Two skills emphasize that the measurement gap causes silent failures:
- TIDEs: technostress is "insufficiently examined as a measurable and design-addressable phenomenon" — without measurement, IDE design is flying blind
- HAX longitudinal: developers' perception of AI impact diverges from telemetry (perceive code quality improved, debugging unchanged; reality: debugging unchanged, deletions +100/month)
The pattern: what feels different ≠ what actually changes. Triangulation (perception + system data) is the only way to close the gap.

### Pattern 5: AI Prefers Open Source
The framework model skill and the broader open source monetization research both flag that AI coding assistants prefer open source tools — more flexible, better documented. This creates a new channel: open source adoption via AI recommendation, not human discovery. The implication for A-Tech: open source isn't just a developer trust strategy; it's becoming an AI-mediated distribution channel.
