## 1. Research Summary

Run 3 operates the third sweep of the same calendar day, so the window covers the last 24–48 hours of the standing domains. The window produced three clusters: (a) the **OSS-funding mechanism pivot** — the strongest single mechanism proposal for open-source sustainability in years (the registry royalty), arriving as the maintainer-stipend institutional wave (Rust MiR first cohort, Servo's one-year donation-funded role) proves stipends work at single-project scale; (b) the **authority-and-verification convergence** in developer experience — Futurum's enterprise control-gap survey and the ARC pre-verification RCT both price the same thing from different ends: agents act faster than controls, and forcing review earlier recovers time; and (c) a **privacy-attack + privacy-audit doublet** — the LT-code gradient-inversion result (passive attackers exceed prior bounds) and the subgroup-concentrated DP-leakage audit (average-case audits hide the vulnerable records) sharpen the same lesson: realized leakage beats worst-case guarantees, and the distribution matters more than the mean. The commercial cluster: Anthropic's Claude for Financial Advisors as the first full-scale MCP data-residency wedge into a regulated vertical, one week after OpenAI's hosted-data alternative. The clusters:

1. **The registry royalty: pay the people who run the meter.** Laurie Voss's Sept 13 essay (seldo.com) reframes thirty years of failed voluntary funding with two facts: companies already pay for open source (>$1B/yr across JFrog $532M, Snyk ~$326M, Docker $207M, Chainguard, Sonatype, Sonar, Socket) — the money is captured by supply-chain vendors, not maintainers; and every attempt to charge at the license layer has lost to a full-free fork (React, Elastic/OpenSearch, HashiCorp/OpenTofu, Redis/Valkey) because the code game is won by free while the supply game is won by whoever is the default. The proposal: registries meter corporate use and charge for supply (Docker's rule: individuals and FOSS pay nothing), then pass a fixed royalty slice pro-rata to every package in paying customers' dependency trees — automatic, no application, no grants committee. The agentic trigger: agents consume open source exclusively through registries (the May 2026 RubyGems swarm; curl's bounty killed by AI garbage), making metered supply the natural agentic-era funding surface. **Novel** — grep `seldo|registry royalty|JFrog|dependency-tree royalty` returned zero skill matches.
2. **The authority boundary + the forced pre-verification card.** Two DevEx prints, same lesson from opposite ends. Techstrong/Futurum's "The AI Agent Race" (Aug 2026, n=839): enterprises let AI write the change (40.17%) before they let it decide the change goes live (6.20%); 75.21% report ≥1 AI-contributed production incident while only 32.06% have human approval gates; benchmark scores are system properties (SWE-bench Verified audited — 59.4% of difficult tasks had material issues; a 6-point infrastructure spread; up to 6× tokens without a purpose-built CLI); same-task token use varies 30× across frontier models — cost per accepted change is the enterprise metric. Xu et al.'s RCT (arXiv:2609.09766): mandatory pre-code **ARCs (AI Response Cards)** cut review time ~27%, raise quality ~24% and satisfaction ~26% at ~20% extra tokens — with confidence unchanged — the first experimental pricing of an interface fix for the verification bottleneck. **Both novel** — grep `Futurum|75\.21|cost.per.accepted` and `AI Response Cards|2609\.09766|cognitive load of AI assistance` zero skill matches.
3. **The privacy doublet: attack and audit converge on "realized ≠ promised."** Shariati & Meybodi (arXiv:2609.09659) reframe gradient inversion as erasure-code decoding: exact batch + label recovery from a single FedSGD round, certified without ground truth; a passive attacker observing an honestly trained network recovers 94–100% of ImageNet batches at sizes up to 128 — "the privacy leakage of federated learning has been underestimated." Sun et al. (arXiv:2609.09848) formalize the complementary audit failure: subgroup-targeted MIA shows DP protects the average while leakage concentrates (a tenth of records ≈ 40% of residual leakage), prior attacks underestimate subgroup leakage, and which records leak is a property of the release mechanism, not the record. Together: batch size is not privacy; average-case audits are not audits. **Both novel** — greps `LT-code|2609\.09659|gradient inversion` and `subgroup membership|2609\.09848|concentrated leak` zero skill matches.
4. **The salience flip + the residency wedge.** On the behavioral side, the Armenia CERGE-EI heterogeneity analysis (WP 823; Yerevan RCT, 291 households, 20 months) turns a null average effect into a design law: adding monetary cost information to peer-comparison energy reports flips response direction by subgroup — high-SES households show a significant +20.8 kWh differential increase under cost-added reports while tertiary-educated-female households show the strongest conservation (−31.8 kWh, p=.007), low-baseline households boomarang (+30.9 kWh), and electric-heating households conserve hugely (−47 to −90 kWh). Cost salience is not a neutral add-on; segmentation is the design. On the commercial side, Anthropic's Claude for Financial Advisors (Sept 14; 11 MCP connectors; Schwab's 16,000+ RIAs; Dynasty's embedded "AI operating system") is the first full-scale MCP data-residency wedge: M+N topology (each partner runs one server; client data never leaves the custodian) converts the compliance question ("where does client data go?" — "nowhere") into the go-to-market wedge, monetized razor-and-blades (free plugin → $70–120/user/month Enterprise → audit-log premium), four days after OpenAI's hosted-data ChatGPT for Financial Services. **Both novel** — greps `Yerevan|Armenia|CERGE` and `M plus N|M×N|data residency|Claude for Financial` (outside run-2's ETF-skill mention and README index) zero skill matches.
