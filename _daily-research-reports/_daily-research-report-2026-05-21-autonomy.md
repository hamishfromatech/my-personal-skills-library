## Executive Summary

Today's research identified a high-value concept at the intersection of behavioral psychology, AI tool adoption, and ethical persuasion: **Autonomy-Supportive Marketing** based on **Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**.

**Key Insight of the Day**: The AI tool market is saturated with controlling tactics (scarcity, guilt, FOMO) that trigger **psychological reactance** — a defense mechanism where users resist persuasion because they feel their freedom is threatened. For A-Tech's core audience (developers, privacy advocates, open-source builders), reactance is not a minor friction — it's a deal-breaker. The opportunity is to flip the script: market by *amplifying* user agency rather than restricting it.

A-Tech's values (open-source AI, data privacy, financial freedom, practical implementation) are *inherently* autonomy-supportive. This skill codifies how to weaponize that alignment.

### Autonomy-Supportive Marketing (`~/.skills/autonomy-supportive-marketing/`)
**Relevance**: 10/10 for all A-Tech products

**What It Is**
Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1980s–present) identifies three basic psychological needs that drive human motivation: **Autonomy** (self-chosen action), **Competence** (feeling capable), and **Relatedness** (belonging to something meaningful). When these needs are satisfied, motivation becomes *internalized* — users don't just comply, they commit. When these needs are thwarted (via controlling tactics), users experience reactance and either churn or actively oppose the brand.

**Why It Matters Now**
- **Algorithmic Aversion Research (Longoni & Cian, 2022)**: Consumers reject AI recommendations when they feel agency is removed. They accept them when framed as "data to help you decide."
- **Developer Psychology**: Engineers with high self-efficacy exhibit the *highest* reactance to controlling marketing. "Must-have" messaging backfires.
- **Privacy Reactance**: Users who discover data collection without control don't just switch — they become activists against the brand.
- **The Commoditizing AI Market**: As AI tools become interchangeable, the differentiator is not capability but *relationship*. Autonomy-supportive marketing builds relationships that controlling tactics destroy.

## Mechanics & Psychological Triggers

### 1. The Reactance Loop
Threat to Freedom → Reactance (anger, resistance) → Restoration Behavior (opposition, churn, negative advocacy)

**A-Tech Application**: When a competitor says "Stop wasting money on ChatGPT," developers feel their existing decision is attacked. When A-Tech says "Own your stack, or rent — here's the math for both paths," the same developer feels respected and informed.

### 2. The Competence-First Framing
Most AI marketing says "We do X for you." Autonomy-supportive marketing says "You become more capable of X."

**A-Tech Application**: A-Coder is "the IDE that makes you a 10x engineer" not "the IDE that codes for you." The Builder's Club is "where builders level up" not "where you get spoon-fed answers."

### 3. Transparent AI as Trust Multiplier
Research (Lee & Kim, 2025) shows that when AI systems explain their reasoning, reactance disappears. When users detect manipulation, reactance spikes.

**A-Tech Application**: A-Coder should surface *why* a suggestion was made: "This came from your local model analyzing the last 3 function calls. No data left your machine." This satisfies autonomy (you know what's happening), competence (you understand the logic), and relatedness (you're part of a privacy-respecting community).

### 4. The Opt-In Telemetry Paradox
Only 15-30% of users opt into telemetry when given a genuine choice. But the *trust* generated by offering that choice compounds into lower churn, higher NPS, and voluntary advocacy that outperforms forced data collection.

**A-Tech Application**: "Help us improve A-Coder by sharing anonymous patterns. You control what's shared and can pause anytime." The architecture ensures privacy; the invitation ensures goodwill.

## Application to A-Tech Projects

### Be Practical (Book & Playbooks)
- **Scars Not Wounds Integration**: Frame founder stories around *choice* — "I chose to build local-first because I value ownership. You can too."
- **Autonomy Contracts**: End every chapter with "Here's what I did. Here are three paths. Which aligns with your situation?"
- **Volitional Guarantee**: "If this isn't the most practical AI book you've read, keep it and we'll refund you." Signals confidence and respects buyer autonomy.
- **Endowed Progress**: Pre-populate playbooks with 20% completion (template outlines, starter prompts) so users feel competence immediately.

### A-Coder (IDE)
- **Choice-Rich Onboarding**: "Do you want A-Coder to suggest completions automatically, or only when you press a key? You can change this anytime."
- **Transparent AI**: Every suggestion shows its source and reasoning.
- **Deferential Feature Messaging**: "Teams that add collaboration reduce context-switching by 40%. Explore when you're ready." (Not "Upgrade to unlock collaboration.")
- **Feedback Autonomy**: When users dismiss a prompt, ask "Was this not relevant, or not the right time?" — then respect the answer.

### Open Source AI Builder's Club
- **Invitation Framing**: "The club isn't for everyone. It's for builders who believe AI should be owned, not rented. If that resonates, you're already one of us."
- **Competence Ladders**: Public skill trees where members choose their progression.
- **Relatedness Rituals**: Weekly "Show Your Scars" sessions — lessons learned, not just wins.
- **Contribution Autonomy**: Members choose how they participate. No forced engagement metrics.

## Ethical Cognitive Biases (Aligned)

| Bias | Application | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | Give genuine value first, no strings | Free open-source tools + public playbooks with no email gate |
| **Endowed Progress** | Pre-fill onboarding to trigger competence | "Your first playbook template is ready — 3 sections already outlined" |
| **Goal Gradient Effect** | Visualize proximity to meaningful outcomes | "You're 2 steps away from deploying your first local AI agent" |
| **Social Proof** | Showcase peer builders, not celebrities | "127 builders deployed local AI agents this month" |
| **IKEA Effect** | Let users customize their tools | "Build your own IDE plugin. We'll publish it in the registry" |

## Anti-Patterns (Avoid)

| Tactic | Why It Backfires |
|---|---|
| Dark patterns (hidden subscriptions, hard-to-cancel) | Destroys open-source trust foundation |
| Fake scarcity ("Only 3 copies left" digital) | Triggers reactance in transparency-valuing developers |
| Guilt-based messaging ("If you care about privacy, you'll buy...") | Manipulates relatedness into obligation |
| Feature cramming without consent | Violates autonomy; feels like surveillance |
| Leaderboards that shame non-participants | Undermines relatedness; creates anxiety |
