## 1. Research Scan Summary

### Domain A: Neuro-Marketing Privacy-First Behavioral Analytics

The 2026 systematic review by Sarkar & Sharma (Journal of Marketing & Social Research) consolidates the full transition from biometric neuromarketing to privacy-first behavioral analytics. Key signals:

- **The 90% subconscious thesis remains robust:** Over 90% of consumer decisions are subconscious, but the measurement methodology is pivoting from EEG/fMRI (declining due to cost, ecological validity limits, and regulatory pressure) to behavioral signal analytics.
- **Behavioral proxies are sufficient:** The same brain regions activated by traditional neuromarketing stimuli (PFC for value, amygdala for emotion, NAcc for reward anticipation, insula for threat detection) produce observable behavioral signatures without biometric capture.
- **Regulatory pressure is accelerating the shift:** EU Digital Services Act and FTC AI guidance (2025–2026) are actively targeting biometric manipulation and dark patterns. GDPR Article 9 special category data restrictions make biometric collection legally hazardous.
- **Cultural neuroscience gap:** Most neuromarketing studies generalize from Western populations. Emotional triggers vary significantly cross-culturally, creating both a methodological challenge and a market opportunity for culturally adaptive behavioral analytics.

**Cross-reference:** Existing `neuromarketing`, `trust-first-neuromarketing`, and `affective-computing` skills cover aspects of this domain. No existing skill unified the systematic review findings with a production-ready privacy-first signal stack and cultural adaptation framework. Novel gap requiring new skill.

### Domain B: DORA AI Attribution & Developer Experience 2026

The 2025 DORA State of AI-assisted Software Development report (5,000 professionals) and subsequent 2026 analyses from Oobeya and practitioner commentaries reveal a measurement crisis:

- **DORA metrics are necessary but no longer sufficient:** Deployment frequency and lead time can rise while change failure rate and rework rate also rise — an unstable pattern DORA alone cannot explain.
- **The AI attribution gap is the critical blind spot:** DORA does not know whether code was AI-assisted or human-authored. Without segmentation, engineering leaders cannot determine if AI is helping or hurting.
- **Three tensions define AI-era delivery:** (1) Verification tax — time saved writing is re-spent auditing; (2) Expertise paradox — AI bridges knowledge gaps while degrading deep expertise; (3) Workflow gap — AI speeds prototyping but the "last mile" of production integration neutralizes gains.
- **DX Core 4 framework emerges:** Speed, Effectiveness, Quality, Business Impact — a balancing model that resists metric gaming (you cannot inflate speed while effectiveness and quality collapse).

**Cross-reference:** `developer-experience-devex-2026`, `ai-assisted-engineering-discipline-2026`, and `the-80-percent-problem` cover aspects. No existing skill operationalized the four-layer engineering intelligence stack (DORA + DevEx + AI attribution + business alignment) with the 2025 DORA report's seven team archetypes and AI capabilities model. Novel gap requiring new skill.

### Domain C: Agentic Commerce Market Map 2026

Antler's January 2026 deep-dive into agentic commerce infrastructure maps 50+ startups across three layers. Key findings:

- **Access layer (identity/permissions):** KYA (Know Your Agent) is the foundational unsolved problem. No standardized way to attach cryptographically verifiable identity to an agent. AP2, Visa TAP, and ERC-8004 are competing for standard status.
- **Discovery layer (storefront/GEO):** Merchants risk invisibility as discovery shifts from Google Search to ChatGPT/Perplexity. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is emerging as a distinct discipline. Data monetization for agent queries is a new revenue model.
- **Payment layer:** x402 ($600M annualized), AP2 (60+ partners), and Mastercard Agent Pay are production-ready. The microtransaction economics are viable: $0.001 transactions on Solana/Base.
- **Timing risk is acute:** 95% of AI agent projects fail to reach production (MIT 2025). Core infrastructure is immature and fragmented. Gartner predicts 40% of AI projects will fail by 2027.
- **B2B vs. consumer dynamics differ:** Consumer adoption may J-curve once trust barriers are crossed (64% openness, 10% current usage). B2B adoption is constrained by legacy systems — 39% of procurement remains manual.
