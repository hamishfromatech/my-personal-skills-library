# UC Berkeley Intensified Work Study — Extraction

## Source
UC Berkeley researchers (preliminary findings published in Harvard Business Review, March 2026)
Study: 200-person US technology company, observed over extended period

## Core Finding
AI does not simply make work more efficient — it intensifies work. Workers using AI at high enthusiasm levels take on more tasks, work faster, and multitask unsustainably because AI makes "doing more" feel possible.

## Observed Behaviors
- Prompting AI during lunch, in-between moments, and just before leaving
- Agents working while humans are away, creating continuous work involvement
- Fewer pauses, less recovery time
- Multiple agents running in parallel
- Previously deferred tasks now tackled simultaneously
- Colleagues taking up "vibe coding" require coaching and review, adding coordination overhead

## Widening Job Scope
- Experiments with AI accumulated into meaningful widening of job scope
- Workers tried new things because the barrier was lower
- But the accumulation became unsustainable
- "Once the excitement wears off, it's a recipe for cognitive overload and burnout."

## Cognitive Debt (Margaret-Anne Storey, University of Victoria)
- Agentic AI creates "invisible decisions"
- Engineering concerns shift from technical debt to cognitive debt
- One team studied hit a wall: no one could explain how different parts worked together or why design decisions were made
- "This is not just about code quality. It is about whether individual developers and product teams can maintain a coherent mental model."

## A-Tech Implications
- AI tools should not lower the barrier to starting tasks without also providing closure mechanisms
- Comprehension checkpoints and decision logs directly counter cognitive debt
- Session boundaries (lunch, end-of-day) should be enforced by the tool, not left to willpower
