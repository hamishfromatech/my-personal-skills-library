# Self-Determination Theory: Foundational Research

## Original SDT Papers

**Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (1985).** *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*. New York: Plenum.

The foundational monograph establishing SDT's organismic dialectical approach. Introduces the concept that intrinsic motivation requires supportive social contexts and that controlling environments undermine natural growth tendencies.

**Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000).** "Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being." *American Psychologist*, 55, 68-78.

The most-cited SDT overview paper. Formalizes the three basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness), their cross-cultural universality, and their role in predicting wellness and optimal functioning.

**Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2000).** "The 'what' and 'why' of goal pursuits: Human needs and the self-determination of behavior." *Psychological Inquiry*, 11, 227-268.

Distinguishes between intrinsic goals (personal growth, relationships, community) and extrinsic goals (wealth, fame, image). Demonstrates that pursuit of intrinsic goals predicts well-being even when extrinsic goals are achieved.

## The Six Mini-Theories

### 1. Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET)
**Focus:** How social contexts affect intrinsic motivation.
**Key finding:** Rewards, deadlines, and surveillance can undermine intrinsic motivation by shifting the perceived locus of causality from internal to external.
**Implication for developer tools:** Gamification with external rewards can backfire. Points and badges should celebrate genuine competence, not manufacture it.

### 2. Organismic Integration Theory (OIT)
**Focus:** How extrinsic motivation becomes internalized.
**Key finding:** Autonomy-supportive contexts promote integration of external values; controlling contexts promote introjection (internal pressure) or external compliance.
**Implication for developer tools:** Documentation and tutorials should invite exploration, not prescribe rigid paths.

### 3. Causality Orientations Theory (COT)
**Focus:** Individual differences in motivational orientation.
**Key finding:** Three orientations: autonomy (acting from interest), control (acting for rewards), and impersonal (anxiety-driven).
**Implication for developer tools:** Developer populations skew heavily autonomy-oriented. Tools that respect this orientation perform better.

### 4. Basic Psychological Needs Theory (BPNT)
**Focus:** The universality of autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
**Key finding:** All three needs are essential; thwarting any one produces distinct functional costs.
**Implication for developer tools:** A tool cannot compensate for relatedness-thwarting with competence-support. All three matter.

### 5. Goal Contents Theory (GCT)
**Focus:** What goals people pursue and why it matters.
**Key finding:** Extrinsic goal pursuit (wealth, fame, image) predicts lower wellness even when successful.
**Implication for developer tools:** Community metrics should celebrate contribution quality, not follower counts or revenue leaderboards.

### 6. Relationships Motivation Theory (RMT)
**Focus:** How relationships satisfy needs.
**Key finding:** High-quality relationships satisfy not only relatedness but also autonomy and competence.
**Implication for developer tools:** Mentorship and peer support should enhance, not replace, individual agency.

## SDT in Software Engineering and Open Source

**Lakhani, K. R., & Wolf, R. G. (2005).** "Why Hackers Do What They Do: Understanding Motivation and Effort in Free/Open Source Software Projects."
- Found that enjoyment-based intrinsic motivation is the strongest driver of open-source contribution
- Extrinsic rewards (pay, career) play secondary roles
- Relatedness needs are satisfied through community reputation and peer recognition

**Ye, Y., & Kishida, K. (2003).** "Toward an understanding of the motivation of open source software developers."
- Identified "cognitive evaluation" as a key mechanism: developers contribute because the activity itself is intellectually satisfying
- Confirmed that controlling environments (corporate mandates, strict governance) reduce contribution quality

**Bitzer, J., Schrettl, W., & Schröder, P. J. H. (2007).** "Intrinsic motivation in open source software development."
- Demonstrated that intrinsic motivation in OSS is self-reinforcing: successful contributions increase perceived competence, which increases motivation for further contributions
- This is the competence → motivation → competence feedback loop

## Key SDT Metrics

**Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI):**
- 7-point Likert scale measuring interest/enjoyment, perceived competence, effort/importance, tension/pressure, and perceived choice
- Widely used in gaming, education, and workplace studies
- Adaptable to developer tool evaluation

**Basic Psychological Needs Scale (BPNS):**
- Measures autonomy, competence, and relatedness satisfaction
- Available in general and domain-specific (work, relationships, etc.) forms
- Can be adapted for "developer tool needs satisfaction"

**Work Climate Assessment:**
- Assesses autonomy support vs. control in organizational contexts
- Relevant for evaluating open-source community governance styles

## Practical Applications in Technical Environments

**Autonomy-Supportive Language Patterns:**
- "You might consider..." vs. "You should..."
- "Here's one approach..." vs. "The correct way is..."
- "What would you like to explore next?" vs. "Complete Chapter 3."

**Competence-Feedback Patterns:**
- Specific, informational feedback: "This function handles edge cases well" vs. "Good job"
- Process praise over person praise: "You thought through the error handling carefully" vs. "You're so smart"
- Optimal challenge calibration: tasks at the edge of current ability, not far beyond

**Relatedness-Building Patterns:**
- Using member names in community interactions
- Celebrating non-code contributions equally
- Creating shared rituals and inside references
- Transparency in decision-making and conflict resolution
