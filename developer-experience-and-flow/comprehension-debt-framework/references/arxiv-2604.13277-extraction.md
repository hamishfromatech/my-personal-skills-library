# arXiv:2604.13277 — Comprehension Debt in GenAI-Assisted Software Engineering Projects

**Author:** Muhammad Ovais Ahmad  
**Date:** April 14, 2026  
**Venue:** Proceedings of the 2026 International Conference on Evaluation and Assessment in Software Engineering Companion

## Study Design

- **Population:** 207 undergraduate software engineering students
- **Data:** 621 reflective diaries over eight weeks
- **Method:** Qualitative study investigating how GenAI tools contribute to Comprehension Debt (CD)

## Definition of Comprehension Debt

CD refers to the growing gap between what a development team knows about its codebase and what it actually needs to understand in order to maintain and modify it effectively. CD is distinct from traditional technical debt because it resides in the collective cognition of development teams rather than in the codebase itself.

## Four Accumulation Patterns

### 1. AI-as-Black-Box Code Acceptance
Students pasted AI-generated code without reading or understanding it, treating the AI as an oracle. Result: acquired functionality they could not explain or modify independently.

### 2. Context-Mismatch Debt
AI-generated code was technically correct but mismatched to the project's architecture, conventions, or constraints. Result: invisible bridging work and architectural drift.

### 3. Dependency-Induced Atrophy
Students stopped learning foundational concepts because the AI handled them. Result: shrinking collective knowledge and growing dependence on AI assistance.

### 4. Verification-Bypass
Students skipped verification steps because the AI's output "looked right." Result: superficial tests, ignored edge cases, and divergent mental models of system behavior.

## One Mitigating Pattern

### Comprehension Scaffold
Students who used GenAI as a tutor rather than a replacement — asking for explanations, generating examples, checking understanding — built deeper knowledge and could independently modify code later.

## Pedagogical Implications

The paper argues for explicit pedagogical strategies to mitigate CD:
- Verification practices
- Structured retrospectives
- Active learning assessments

These are framed as essential for software engineering education, not optional add-ons.

## Limitations

- Student population may not generalize directly to professional developers
- Reflective diaries are self-reported and subject to social desirability bias
- Eight-week window may not capture long-term CD accumulation
