## Core Thesis

Agentic coding tools create genuine behavioral addiction through dopamine-driven feedback loops. The faster developers get at agentic coding, the more they want to use it. This is simultaneously satisfying, frustrating, and exhilarating — like a slot machine where every prompt pull delivers random rewards and sometimes amazing payouts.

## Key Data Points

### Multitudes Study (500+ developers)
- Engineers using AI tools experienced a **19.6% rise in out-of-hour commits**
- "Out-of-hour" = submissions outside typical schedules
- Correlates with cognitive overload and physical exhaustion

### ActivTrak Productivity Lab Study (163,638 employees, Jan 2023–Dec 2025)
- AI drove a **46% increase in Saturday productive hours**
- AI drove a **58% increase in Sunday productive hours**
- Conclusion: "AI is adding to workloads"
- Sharp increase in time spent for every activity category:
  - Messaging: +145%
  - Collaboration: +5%
  - Business management tools: +94%
- High-performing employees are "adopting it and doing more — not the same amount more efficiently"

### Developer Testimonials

**Steve Yegge (Longtime developer/blogger):**
- AI-powered coding is "genuinely addictive"
- Crashes and falls asleep after long vibe coding sessions
- Regrets contributing to unrealistic industry standards
- "The better you get at it, the more you want to use it"
- "It doles out dopamine and adrenaline shots like they're on a fire sale"

**Eren Celebi (Principal engineer, WPP):**
- "I'm coding into later hours of the day not because I'm told to do so, but because I can't get myself to get up from the computer."

**Doug Sims (Senior software engineer, Paramount):**
- After coding with AI for a year: more productive than ever, but also far busier
- "It's such an opportunity. It's kinda like you're a kid in a candy shop."
- Attributes intensified speed to rapid feedback loop: test ideas faster, discard failures faster, run with promising ideas faster
- "Until sometime last year, I had a normal social life... But I feel compelled to be doing side projects and learning constantly... And the weekends just disappear."
- Positive reinforcement from agentic tools drives him to take on more and work through nights and weekends
- "I would always stay up late at night if I felt like I was really onto something new. But now it feels like I'm onto something new every day."

**Rajesh Padmakumaran (VP, AI practice leader, Genpact):**
- AI tools make it incredibly easy to generate scaffolding code, APIs, tests, documentation in minutes
- "Creates a powerful sense of momentum"
- Developers quickly get into flow state moving from idea to working software much faster
- "A bit addictive"

## UC Berkeley / Harvard Business Review Preliminary Findings

Researchers observed a U.S.-based technology company to study how generative AI changes work habits.

**Key findings:**
- Productivity gains lead enthusiastic adopters to take on more work, work faster, and unsustainably multitask
- AI makes "doing more" feel possible
- Once excitement wears off, recipe for cognitive overload and burnout
- Workers prompt AI during lunch, in-between moments, just before leaving so agents work while away
- Experiments accumulate into meaningful widening of job scope
- Agentic coding drives increased multitasking; engineers carry growing number of open tasks
- Running multiple agents in parallel
- Tackling previously-deferred tasks because they can now be run in background
- Engineers frequently pinged to coach and review "vibe coding" by non-technical colleagues

## Cognitive Debt: Margaret-Anne Storey

Professor of computer science, University of Victoria Canada.

- Agentic AI and coordination overhead leads to growing number of **"invisible decisions"**
- Engineering concern shifting from technical debt to **cognitive debt**
- One team she worked with hit a wall when no one could explain how different parts of the system worked together or why certain design decisions were made
- "This is not just about code quality. It is about whether individual developers and product teams can maintain a coherent mental model of what the system is doing and why."

**Eren Celebi on debugging:**
- If Claude uses a library he's not familiar with, he loses ability to debug his own code
- "I end-up chasing around the AI looking for answers, like a junior engineer would send Slack messages to the senior asking for advice."

## Finding Balance: Counterexamples

**Alon Sabi (Backend developer, founder SREDify):**
- Admits going to sleep later out of excitement
- But agentic coding has also freed him to step away from screen
- Now primarily thinking through bigger questions rather than typing functions
- Goes for walks outside while working in his head
- Previous engineering manager experience helped: used to breaking processes into chunks and delegating
- For individual contributors, managing agents is a significant shift
- "Prioritization, pace, and sequencing is controlled by humans"
- "There are tons of benefits. But we also need to understand there's a limit to how much we can grasp as human beings and how much we can crank out without introducing too many bugs and too many issues."
