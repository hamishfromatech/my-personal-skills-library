# Autonomy Language Guide for A-Tech

## The Principle

Every word in marketing, product copy, onboarding, and sales communicates either *control* or *choice*. Controlling language triggers psychological reactance in A-Tech's core audience (developers, privacy advocates, open-source builders). Autonomy-supportive language activates internalized motivation and long-term loyalty.

This guide provides a complete replacement framework for all customer-facing communications.

---

## Controlling vs. Autonomy-Supportive Language Swaps

### Urgency & Scarcity

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Act now before it's too late" | "Here's what becomes possible when you're ready" | Removes time pressure; respects decision timeline |
| "Don't miss out" | "Explore when it makes sense for you" | Eliminates FOMO guilt; invites volitional action |
| "Limited time offer — expires midnight" | "This pricing is available for builders who join this quarter" | Frames time boundary as community rhythm, not pressure |
| "Only 3 spots left" | "We cap cohorts so everyone gets direct mentorship" | Same scarcity, different framing — service to member, not manipulation |
| "Your competitors are already using AI" | "Some teams move fast, some move deep — both are valid" | Removes threat to identity |

### Capability & Competence

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Our AI does the coding for you" | "A-Coder amplifies what you already know" | Preserves developer identity |
| "No coding required" | "Full control when you want it, smart defaults when you don't" | Doesn't insult technical audience |
| "Let us handle the complex parts" | "You handle the architecture; we'll handle the boilerplate" | Clear division of labor respects expertise |
| "You don't need to understand AI to use this" | "You understand more than you think — here's the full model for when you want it" | Assumes competence, offers depth |
| "Simplified for non-technical users" | "Configurable from zero-code to full-pipeline" | Power users aren't alienated |

### Pricing & Value

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Stop wasting money on subscriptions" | "Own your stack, or rent — here's the math for both paths" | Presents choice, respects existing decisions |
| "The smart choice is the annual plan" | "Annual plans work best for builders who know they'll be shipping all year" | User situation determines "smart," not marketer |
| "Don't overpay for Big Tech solutions" | "Compare total cost of ownership including data egress and lock-in risk" | Informational, not shaming |
| "You can't afford NOT to buy this" | "Here's what builders saved in their first year — use it or not, it's good intel" | Social proof without threat |

### Privacy & Trust

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Trust us with your data" | "Your data never leaves your machine — you don't have to trust us" | Removes need for trust through architecture |
| "We take privacy seriously" | "Here's exactly what we collect, why, and how to turn it off" | Transparency beats promises |
| "Your data is safe with us" | "We can't see your code. The architecture makes it impossible." | Demonstrable > claim |
| "Sign up to unlock your privacy" | "Download the open-source core. No account required." | Immediate autonomy, no gate |

### Community & Belonging

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Join thousands of founders who..." | "A small group of builders is doing something different — see if it resonates" | Intimacy over mass |
| "The AI revolution is here — are you in?" | "Some people want to own their AI. Others are fine renting. Both are valid." | No forced identity |
| "Be part of the movement" | "Build what you believe in. If that's local AI, you're already one of us." | Self-selection, not recruitment |
| "Exclusive community access" | "We keep the club small so everyone gets feedback on their builds" | Service rationale, not status manipulation |

### Onboarding & Product

| Controlling (Avoid) | Autonomy-Supportive (Use) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Complete your profile to continue" | "Your profile helps us personalize — skip it if you prefer defaults" | User controls depth |
| "Enable notifications for the best experience" | "Notifications are off by default. Turn on what matters to you." | Opt-in, not opt-out |
| "You haven't used feature X yet" | "Feature X is available when you're ready — here's what it does" | No guilt, just information |
| "We noticed you didn't finish the tutorial" | "Tutorials are always here. Most builders skip to the IDE and reference later." | Normalizes user preference |
| "Recommended: Enable all AI features" | "Start with 0-3 AI features. Add more as you find rhythm." | User paces themselves |

## Tone Checklist

Before publishing any customer-facing content, verify it passes these checks:

- [ ] Does the message assume the user is competent? (No "dumbing down")
- [ ] Does the user have a clear choice at every step? (No forced progression)
- [ ] Is the user in control of their data and experience? (Opt-in defaults)
- [ ] Does the message invite rather than demand? ("Can" not "must")
- [ ] Is the user's existing stack/decisions respected? (No competitor trashing)
- [ ] Is the community framed as shared mission, not exclusive club? ("We" not "us vs. them")
- [ ] Are costs and tradeoffs presented transparently? (No hidden lock-in)
- [ ] Does the user know how to change their mind later? (Easy reversibility)

## Examples in Context

### Email Subject Lines

| Controlling | Autonomy-Supportive |
|---|---|
| "Last chance: Playbook Edition ends tonight" | "Your playbook template is ready when you are" |
| "Don't let another day go by without AI" | "Three builders shared what they shipped this week" |
| "You're missing out on local AI" | "Curious about local AI? Here's a no-code way to explore" |

### Pricing Page Copy

**Controlling:**
> "Choose the Pro plan before prices go up. The free version won't get you to production."

**Autonomy-Supportive:**
> "Start with the open-source core — it's production-ready and always free. When your team needs compliance modules or priority inference, the Pro plan is here. No pressure: migrate when your situation calls for it."

### Product Announcement

**Controlling:**
> "You need to update to v3.0 immediately. Critical security fixes included."

**Autonomy-Supportive:**
> "v3.0 is available with security improvements we recommend. You're in control of when to update — here's what's changed and why it matters for your setup."

---

## Note on When Control Works

Autonomy-supportive marketing is not "weak" marketing. It is the *strongest* form of persuasion for internalized, long-term commitment. However, there are narrow contexts where controlling language is appropriate:
- **Security incidents**: "Update immediately" when there's an active exploit.
- **Legal compliance**: "You must accept the revised terms to continue using the hosted service."
- **Safety-critical systems**: "This configuration is not recommended for production."

Even then, frame the control as *information* about consequences, not as authority over the user.
