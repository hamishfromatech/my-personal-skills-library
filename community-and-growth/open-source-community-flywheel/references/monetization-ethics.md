# Monetization Ethics for Open Core Businesses

## The Non-Negotiables

### 1. Functional Completeness
The open-source version must be a complete, usable product. If the free version is intentionally crippled, it is not open core—it is freemium with source code available.

### 2. Operational Separation
Paid features should address the needs of organizations operating at scale: security, compliance, audit, support, hosting, and administration. They should not be creative or productivity features that individual developers need.

### 3. Contribution Attribution
If the community builds a feature, that feature belongs to the community. It cannot be moved to a paid tier without explicit consent and compensation.

### 4. Pricing Transparency
Publish how pricing is calculated. Show the cost structure. Explain why the price is what it is.

### 5. No Surveillance Revenue
Never monetize user data, behavior tracking, or community intelligence. This violates the privacy value and destroys trust permanently.

## The A-Tech Promise

> "Every feature you need to build with open-source AI is free. Every service you need to build *with confidence at scale* is fairly priced. We will never sell your data. We will never wall off community-built features. We will always explain our pricing before it changes."

## Revenue Model Validation Checklist

Before introducing any paid feature or service:

- [ ] Does the free version remain fully functional for individual use?
- [ ] Is the paid feature addressing organizational, not individual, needs?
- [ ] Would a reasonable community member understand why this costs money?
- [ ] Is the pricing published transparently?
- [ ] Is there a free path for education, non-profits, and open-source projects?
- [ ] Are community contributors eligible for free or discounted access?
- [ ] Will this pricing decision make sense in a public retrospective?

If any answer is no, do not ship.
