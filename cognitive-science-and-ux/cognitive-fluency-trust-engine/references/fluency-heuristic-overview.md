# Fluency Heuristic: Academic Foundations

## Core Papers and Researchers

### Reber, Schwarz, & Winkielman — Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure
The foundational work establishing that ease of processing creates positive affect, which then transfers to judgments of truth, beauty, and liking.

**Key finding**: When information is processed fluently, people not only like it more—they believe it more readily. This is not a conscious decision; it is a System 1 (automatic) judgment.

**Implication for marketing**: The *form* of your message shapes its perceived credibility before the *content* is even evaluated.

### Schwarz — Metacognitive Experiences in Judgment and Decision Making
Schwarz demonstrated that people use their own subjective processing experience as information. "If it feels easy, it must be right."

**Key finding**: Disfluency triggers analytic thinking; fluency triggers intuitive acceptance.

**Implication**: For initial trust-building and conversion (where you want intuitive acceptance), fluency is essential. For complex technical deep-dives (where you want critical evaluation), controlled disfluency can be appropriate—but only *after* trust is established.

### Loewenstein — Information-Gap Theory of Curiosity
Curiosity is an aversive state caused by a perceived gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. The gap must be specific and the answer must feel attainable.

**Key finding**: Vague mystery does not trigger curiosity. Precise, closable gaps do.

**Implication**: Headlines, email subject lines, and CTA copy should create a specific, measurable gap: "The 3-line config that reduced our inference costs by 40%" rather than "How we saved money with AI."

### Norton, Mochon & Ariely — The IKEA Effect
People place disproportionate value on products they partially created themselves.

**Key finding**: Labor leads to love, but only when the task is completed successfully. Incomplete tasks do not generate the effect—and may create frustration.

**Implication**: Design first-contribution experiences so success is guaranteed. A user who successfully submits their first pull request, no matter how small, will value the community more highly.

### Zajonc — Mere Exposure Effect
Repeated exposure to a stimulus increases preference for it, even when the exposure is subliminal.

**Key finding**: Familiarity breeds liking, not contempt.

**Implication**: Consistent terminology, color palettes, avatar styles, and messaging rhythms across all A-Tech properties build unconscious preference over time.

## Synthesis for A-Tech

These five mechanisms form a stack:

1. **Mere Exposure** creates baseline familiarity.
2. **Fluency** makes that familiar content feel trustworthy.
3. **Curiosity Gap** creates the motivation to engage deeper.
4. **IKEA Effect** transforms engagement into ownership.
5. **Positive Affect** from fluency makes the whole experience feel rewarding.

This stack is ethical because it is transparent, consensual, and value-driven. It makes genuine value easier to perceive and act on—it does not substitute manipulation for value.
